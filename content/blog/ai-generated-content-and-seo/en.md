## The short answer

No, **Google does not penalize content simply because AI wrote it**. In its official guidance for creators, Google states that it rewards **quality and helpfulness**, not a particular way of producing content. Trouble starts when AI is used to **mass-produce pages to manipulate rankings** — that violates spam policies whether a human or a model wrote the text.

## Google's position

- **The outcome matters, not the tool.** Helpful, accurate, original content can rank regardless of how it was created.
- **E-E-A-T** — experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness — remains the reference for judging quality.
- **Scaled content abuse policy:** generating many pages primarily to manipulate search rankings with little value for users. It covers automated generation, manual mass copywriting and stitching together other people's content.
- Google encourages transparency: if readers would reasonably want to know how content was made, explain it.

Details are in the Google Search Central documentation on spam policies.

## Yandex's position

Yandex does not ban AI-assisted text, but it has long fought **low-value content**. In Yandex Webmaster, pages can be flagged as low-value or low-demand and dropped from search. The logic is the same: text with no new information, written for keywords, loses visibility.

## What actually hurts

- **Thousands of near-identical pages** for every city or query with a couple of words swapped.
- **Factual errors** — models confidently invent numbers, dates and names.
- **Rehashing the top results** with no first-hand experience or detail.
- **No author** and no sign that a real person or company stands behind the text.
- **Publishing without editing** — padded intros, repetition, stock phrases.

## How to use AI without losing quality

1. **Start from expertise.** Gather your own data, examples and specialist answers — a model structures well but does not know your experience.
2. **Use AI for groundwork:** outlines, headline options, rephrasing, translation, checking topic coverage.
3. **Verify every fact.** Numbers, names, versions, quotes — check against the original source.
4. **Edit by hand.** Cut filler, add specifics and real examples.
5. **Name the author** and their expertise, especially in money and health topics.
6. **Publish less, but better.** One useful article beats ten rewrites.

## Quick pre-publishing checklist

| Question | If "no" |
|---|---|
| Does the text offer something competitors don't? | Add experience, an example, data |
| Are all facts verified? | Check sources |
| Does it answer the question up front? | Restructure |
| Is the author clear? | Add a byline and bio |
| Was the page made for readers, not a keyword? | Merge or remove it |

## FAQ

### Can Google tell that text was written by AI?

It matters less than you think: Google says it evaluates usefulness, not origin. Weak text ranks poorly no matter who wrote it.

### Do I need to label AI content?

There is no mandatory label for search, but transparency builds trust. If readers would want to know how the material was produced, say so.

### Can I use AI to translate articles for a multilingual site?

Yes, that is common practice if a native speaker proofreads the result. Unchecked machine translation often sounds unnatural and gets terminology wrong.
