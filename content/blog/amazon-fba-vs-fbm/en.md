## The short answer: what is the difference

- **FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon)** — you send goods to Amazon fulfillment centers. Amazon stores them, picks and packs orders, ships, handles returns and answers customers on delivery issues.
- **FBM (Fulfillment by Merchant)** — inventory stays with you or your logistics partner (3PL). Picking, shipping, returns and support are on you.

The **referral fee** is the same in both models — it depends on the category. The difference is who pays for logistics and how.

## Comparison on the key factors

| Factor | FBA | FBM |
|---|---|---|
| Fees | Per-unit fulfillment fee (based on size and weight), monthly storage, aged inventory surcharges, inbound, removal and disposal fees | Your own costs for warehouse, packaging, carriers, staff and returns |
| Prime | FBA offers get the Prime badge automatically | Only through Seller Fulfilled Prime, with strict delivery and quality requirements |
| Inventory risk | Stock is locked in Amazon's network, capacity limits apply, aged stock gets more expensive | Stock is under your control and easy to move to another channel |
| Control | Amazon sets packaging, delivery speed and returns handling | You control packaging, inserts, carriers and communication |
| Workload | Lower: the main job is planning shipments | Higher: daily picking, shipping and customer support |
| Returns | Handled by Amazon under its policies | Handled by you, under marketplace rules and deadlines |

## Where FBA wins

- **Prime and buyer trust.** Fast delivery and the familiar badge lift conversion.
- **Scale without a warehouse.** No need to hire packers or negotiate with carriers.
- **Customer support** for shipping and returns is on Amazon's side.

The price is ongoing storage and fulfillment fees. A slow-moving product in FBA turns unprofitable quickly.

## Where FBM wins

- **Bulky and heavy items**, where FBA fees eat the margin.
- **Slow movers and the long tail** — no storage fees at Amazon.
- **Products with special packaging**, personalization or made-to-order assembly.
- **Flexibility:** the same stock can sell through your own site and other marketplaces.

## Criteria for sellers entering from abroad

If you enter Amazon from, say, Uzbekistan, logistics to the target country becomes the main question:

1. **Delivery time.** Buyers expect fast shipping. Sending each order individually from abroad is almost always too slow, so goods are usually brought into the target country in advance.
2. **Import and customs.** Decide who acts as importer, how duties and taxes are handled and which documents and certifications your category requires.
3. **FBA prep.** Labeling every unit, packaging to Amazon's requirements, box labels. Many sellers use a prep service in the target country.
4. **Returns.** FBM needs a clear way to accept returns in the target country — usually your 3PL's address.
5. **Working capital.** FBA requires paying for production, freight and storage long before sales come in.

In practice, sellers from abroad pick one of two setups:

- **FBA:** a batch is shipped to the target country, cleared through customs and prepped, then sent into Amazon's warehouses.
- **FBM via a 3PL:** the batch sits in a logistics partner's warehouse in the target country, which picks and ships orders.

## How to decide

- Calculate the **full cost per unit** under both models: product, international freight, duties, prep, Amazon or 3PL fees, returns, advertising.
- Assess **sell-through speed.** Fast sellers are FBA candidates; slow ones fit FBM.
- Factor in **size and weight.** The bigger and heavier the item, the more carefully you should compare fees.
- Consider a **hybrid:** bestsellers via FBA, the rest via FBM, plus FBM as a backup when FBA stock runs out.

## FAQ

### Can I use FBA and FBM at the same time?

Yes. The same product can be offered under both models, and different products can use different ones. A hybrid is often optimal: it reduces the risk of zero sales while an FBA shipment is in transit.

### Does the fulfillment method affect the Buy Box?

Delivery speed and reliability are among the factors Amazon considers, so FBA often helps. It is not a guarantee: price, seller rating and stock availability matter too.

### What should I choose for a first market test?

A small FBA batch if the product is compact and you can accept storage fees. If the item is large or demand is very uncertain, start with FBM through a 3PL in the target country.
