## The short answer

- **amoCRM** is a specialised sales CRM. It is quick to learn, strong at messengers and pipelines, but does very little outside sales.
- **Bitrix24** is an all-in-one platform: CRM, tasks and projects, file storage, team chat, a website builder, a contact centre. It does more, but the learning curve is steeper.

If you need order in your leads and visibility into managers, amoCRM usually wins. If sales must connect to tasks, projects and internal teamwork, Bitrix24 does.

## Comparison by key criteria

| Criterion | amoCRM | Bitrix24 |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Sales and communication | CRM + tasks + intranet |
| Learning curve | Fast, minimal interface | Longer, many sections and settings |
| Sales entities | Deals, contacts, companies | Leads, deals, contacts, companies, smart processes |
| Automation | Digital Pipeline, Salesbot | Robots, triggers, business processes |
| Messengers | A core strength, unified inbox | Available via Open Channels |
| Tasks and projects | Basic deal-related tasks | Full task manager, projects, Gantt |
| Hosting | Cloud only | Cloud and self-hosted on-premise edition |
| Pricing model | Per user | Plan per company with a user limit |

## Sales and pipeline

Both systems support multiple pipelines, kanban views of deals and conversion reports.

- In **amoCRM** everything revolves around the deal: easier to explain to managers, fewer extra steps.
- **Bitrix24** has a separate **lead** entity and a mode that works without it, plus **smart processes** — custom object types for non-standard flows (returns, contracts and so on).

## Ease of use

amoCRM is simpler. A new manager usually finds their way around quickly. Bitrix24 feels heavy for teams that only need a CRM, and without careful setup of permissions and menus employees get lost.

The flip side: amoCRM's simplicity means a lot has to be added via widgets or built through the API.

## Automation

- **amoCRM**: actions on pipeline stages (tasks, messages, owner changes, webhooks) and Salesbot chatbots. Enough for a typical sales process.
- **Bitrix24**: stage robots and triggers plus a **business process** designer — visual flows with conditions, approvals and loops. More flexible, harder to maintain.

## Integrations

Both offer a **REST API**, webhooks and an app marketplace: telephony, mailing, payments, 1C, online stores. The on-premise Bitrix24 edition gives access to the code and server, which matters for deep customisation and data residency requirements. amoCRM runs only in the vendor's cloud.

## Tasks and teamwork

Bitrix24 is clearly stronger here: projects, subtasks, time tracking, Gantt charts, workgroups, internal chat and video calls. In amoCRM tasks are tied to deals and exist mainly so a manager does not forget to call back.

## Total cost of ownership

Comparing licence prices alone is misleading. Factor in:

- **pricing model**: amoCRM costs grow with each new user, while a Bitrix24 plan covers a set number of employees;
- **paid add-ons**: widgets, telephony, connectors;
- **implementation**: pipelines, permissions, automation, integrations and training;
- **ongoing support**: who changes the setup when your process changes;
- for on-premise — **servers, updates and administration**.

## Decision guide by business type

- **Small business selling through messengers and social media** — amoCRM.
- **Sales team focused on manager control and fast launch** — amoCRM.
- **Company that wants CRM, tasks and an intranet in one place** — Bitrix24.
- **Complex processes with approvals and many roles** — Bitrix24.
- **Requirement to keep data on your own servers** — on-premise Bitrix24.
- **Highly unusual logic and in-house accounting systems** — consider a custom CRM, or either option with serious API integration.

## FAQ

### Can I migrate data between amoCRM and Bitrix24?

Yes. Contacts, companies and deals can be exported and imported via files or the API. Message history and automation settings are harder to move and are usually rebuilt.

### Which one is better for an online store?

Both can handle orders and customer communication. The deciding factor is often whether a ready integration exists for your store platform and accounting system.

### Can I use both at the same time?

Technically yes, but it usually creates duplicate data. Most teams pick one CRM for sales and connect other tools to it.
