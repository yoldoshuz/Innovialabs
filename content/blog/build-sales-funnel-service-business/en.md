## The short answer: six funnel steps

In a service business, customers rarely buy right away: they need to see that you can solve their problem and talk to a person. A working funnel looks like this:

1. **Ad or another traffic source** — attract someone with a specific problem.
2. **Landing page** — explain what you do, for whom, and why you can be trusted.
3. **Lead form** — capture contact details with minimal friction.
4. **Qualification** — decide whether this is your customer.
5. **Consultation** — dig into the problem and propose a solution.
6. **Deal** — proposal, contract, payment.

The key rule: every step needs a **metric** and an **owner**. Then you see exactly where customers drop off.

## Step 1. Traffic source

Services do well with channels that capture existing demand: **search ads** (Google Ads, Yandex Direct) on high-intent queries, plus **paid social** on Meta and Telegram for narrow segments.

- Run separate campaigns for separate services and segments.
- Tag every link with **UTM parameters**: source, campaign, ad.
- Metrics: CTR, cost per click, share of relevant traffic.

## Step 2. Landing page

One service, one page. Someone who searched "CRM setup for a clinic" should land on exactly that, not on the company homepage.

What the landing page needs:

- a headline that echoes the query and the customer's outcome;
- who the service is for and which problems it solves;
- how the process works and what the customer receives;
- proof: case studies, reviews, certifications — real ones only;
- answers to objections (timelines, payment terms, guarantees);
- a clear call to action on every screen.

Metrics: lead conversion rate, scroll depth, and mobile traffic share with its own conversion rate.

## Step 3. Lead form

Every extra field lowers conversion, but a form that is too simple invites irrelevant leads. A good balance for services: **name, phone or messenger** and one qualifying question (for example, task type from a list).

- Offer an alternative: a button that opens Telegram or WhatsApp.
- Store UTM parameters and click IDs (GCLID, fbclid) in hidden fields so each lead links back to the ad.
- Send leads straight into a **CRM**, not an inbox.

## Step 4. Qualification

The goal is to separate qualified prospects quickly without spending expert time. Speed is critical: the longer someone waits, the less likely they are to respond.

A simple qualification checklist:

- does the prospect have a problem you solve;
- does the budget fit your range;
- who makes the decision;
- when do they need to start.

Record the status in the CRM: "qualified" or "unqualified" with a reason. Rejection reasons are some of the most valuable data for tuning ads.

## Step 5. Consultation

A consultation is not a company presentation; it is a review of the customer's problem. Prepare a structure: questions about the situation, goals, constraints, next step. End the call by agreeing on a specific action and date.

Metrics: show-up rate, share of consultations followed by a proposal.

## Step 6. Deal

Send the proposal quickly while the conversation is fresh. Record the amount and stage in the CRM and set a follow-up task.

It is important to send the closed deal and its value back to ad platforms as offline conversions. That way the algorithms learn to find people who pay, not just people who fill in forms.

## Metrics across the funnel

| Step | Metric | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| Ad → landing page | CTR, cost per click | Ad platform |
| Landing page → lead | Lead conversion, CPL | GA4, Yandex Metrica |
| Lead → qualification | Qualified share, response time | CRM |
| Qualification → consultation | Meetings booked rate | CRM, calendar |
| Consultation → deal | Close rate, average deal size | CRM |
| Whole funnel | CAC, ROMI | CRM + end-to-end analytics |

## Common mistakes

- Judging ads by cost per lead instead of cost per customer.
- Handling leads in messengers and spreadsheets without a CRM.
- Replying to a lead hours later.
- Not recording rejection reasons.

## FAQ

### Do I need a CRM if I get only a few leads?

Yes. Even with low volume, a CRM keeps the history, source and stage of every customer. Without it you cannot tell which channel brings revenue.

### Which funnel step should I fix first?

The one with the biggest drop compared with what is normal for your business. Often it is response speed and qualification: they are easier and cheaper to fix than buying more traffic.

### How many fields should the form have?

The minimum needed to get in touch and do a first qualification. Usually that is a contact and one or two questions; the rest is clarified on the call.
