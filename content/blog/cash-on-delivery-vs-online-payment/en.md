## The short answer

**Cash on delivery (COD)** helps you sell to people who do not know you yet: they pay only once the product is in their hands. The price of that comfort is **refusals at the door**, wasted courier trips and cash handling. **Online payment** removes most of those risks, but some buyers will not prepay an unfamiliar store. For most shops the best strategy is to keep both and gently steer buyers toward prepayment.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Cash on delivery | Online payment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trust from new buyers | High: the risk sits with the store | Lower, especially for a new brand |
| Order refusals | Can happen right at the door | Rare: the buyer has already committed |
| Delivery costs | Paid even for orders not collected | Only for actual sales |
| Cash | Change, collection, reconciliation | None |
| Speed of funds | After the courier hands in cash or the delivery partner pays out | Instantly or on the provider's settlement schedule |
| Returns | Easier: the buyer can simply decline | Requires a refund process |
| Fraud | Fake orders and pranks | Chargebacks and stolen cards |

## Where COD costs you money

**Refusals.** The buyer changed their mind, found it cheaper or stopped answering the phone. You pay for delivery both ways, and the item is off sale while it travels back.

**Wasted trips.** Even when the order is eventually collected, a second delivery attempt costs courier time and money.

**Cash handling.** Couriers need change, cash must be brought back and reconciled, and discrepancies or losses happen. With delivery partners you also wait for the collected cash to be transferred.

**Fake orders.** Without prepayment, placing an order to someone else's address or phone costs nothing.

## Where online payment costs you sales

- A new store with no reviews feels risky.
- A clumsy payment form or a redirect to an unfamiliar page scares people off.
- The method your audience prefers, such as a specific payment app, is missing.
- It is unclear how to get money back if the product does not fit.

## How to encourage prepayment without losing buyers

Banning COD usually hurts conversion. Softer measures work better:

1. **Reward prepayment.** A small discount, free delivery or a gift for paying online.
2. **Familiar payment methods.** Connect the providers your audience actually uses and keep payment to one or two steps.
3. **Clear refund terms.** Explain next to the pay button how and when money is returned.
4. **Trust signals.** Real reviews, contact details, company information and a clear delivery policy.
5. **Rules by zone and amount.** Keep COD for the city and restrict it for remote regions or large orders.
6. **Partial prepayment.** For expensive or custom items, take the delivery fee or part of the price upfront.
7. **Order confirmation.** A call or message before dispatch reduces refusals at the door.

## How to choose your setup

- **New brand, low-priced goods:** offer both, reward online payment.
- **Expensive or made-to-order goods:** full or partial prepayment.
- **Regions and long-distance delivery:** lean toward prepayment, because returns are costly.
- **Repeat customers:** COD can be offered as a convenience.

Most importantly, run the numbers on your own data: collection rate, two-way delivery cost and cash handling expenses. Then the decision rests on your business, not on generic advice.

## FAQ

### Should I drop cash on delivery entirely?

Usually not. For many buyers it is the way to try a new store. It is smarter to restrict it where risk is high and make online payment more attractive.

### How do I reduce refusals with COD?

Confirm orders before dispatch, show an exact delivery date, send a tracking number and a reminder on delivery day. Repeat refusers can be switched to mandatory prepayment.

### Can the buyer pay the courier by card?

Yes, if the courier or delivery partner has a terminal or QR payment. That removes some of the cash problems but does not solve refusals.
