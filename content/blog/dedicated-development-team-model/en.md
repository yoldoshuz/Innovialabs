## The short answer: what a dedicated team is

A **dedicated team** is a model where a vendor assembles a permanent team for your product: developers, testers, a designer and a manager. These people work only for you, and you pay **for the team's time**, usually monthly.

The key difference from project work: you are not buying a finished result against a fixed spec, you are buying **production capacity** that you steer. The vendor is responsible for people, processes and quality; you are responsible for product decisions.

## Who is on the team

The composition depends on the product, but a typical setup looks like this:

- **Developers** — frontend, backend and mobile in the right proportion.
- **QA engineer** — manual and automated testing.
- **Designer** — often part-time.
- **Project manager or tech lead** — organises the work and talks to you.
- **DevOps** — sets up infrastructure, usually part-time too.

A good practice is to start with a small core and expand once the workload is clear.

## How billing works

The usual scheme is a **monthly rate** per specialist or for the whole team. The total depends on:

- the number of people and their seniority (junior, middle, senior);
- full-time or part-time involvement of certain roles;
- the tech stack and how rare the specialists are;
- requirements on time zone, language and meeting attendance;
- whether management, infrastructure and tools are included in the rate.

Agree in advance how vacations, sick leave and replacements are handled, and how much notice is needed to scale the team up or down.

## Who is responsible for what

| Area | Client | Vendor |
|---|---|---|
| Product vision and priorities | yes | advises |
| Backlog and task setting | yes, often with the PM | details tasks |
| Hiring and replacing people | approves | yes |
| Processes, code review, testing | oversees | yes |
| Infrastructure and access | owns the accounts | configures |

The most common problem is a client expecting the team to decide **what** to build. In this model product decisions stay with you. If nobody on your side leads the product, you need a **product owner** — your own or provided by the vendor.

## When a dedicated team beats project work

**A dedicated team fits when:**

- the product evolves over a long time rather than being delivered once;
- requirements change along the way based on user feedback;
- you need to scale development quickly without hiring in-house;
- keeping product knowledge within one stable team matters.

**A project (fixed-price) model is better when:**

- the task is small and well described;
- there is a precise spec and a clear deadline;
- little or no further development is planned after delivery.

There is also a middle option, **time and material**: you pay for hours actually spent without a fixed team. It is more flexible than a fixed price but does not guarantee the same people.

## How to start without unnecessary risk

1. **Describe goals** for the coming months, not just a feature list.
2. **Check the people**: technical interviews or a trial task.
3. **Agree on processes**: sprints, demos, reporting, communication channels.
4. **Secure ownership**: code and data belong to you; repositories and cloud live on your accounts.
5. **Start with a trial period** and judge pace and quality by real results.

## Common mistakes

- **No product owner** on the client side, so the team works without direction.
- **Tracking hours instead of outcomes**: what was shipped matters more than time logged.
- **Too large a team at the start**, which inflates coordination costs.
- **No documentation**, so knowledge disappears when people change.

## FAQ

### Can a dedicated team work alongside my in-house developers?

Yes, this is a common setup. Agree on shared rules — one repository, code review, coding standards and a common backlog — so you do not end up with two parallel products.

### How do I know the team is effective?

Look at release regularity, the number of bugs after release and predictability: does the team deliver what it planned for the sprint? A demo at the end of every sprint makes progress visible.

### What happens to the product if the cooperation ends?

If the code, repositories, cloud accounts and documentation are on your side from the start, another team can pick up the work. Define the handover process and notice period in the contract in advance.
