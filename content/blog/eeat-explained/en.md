## The short answer

**E-E-A-T** is a set of four quality criteria from Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines:

- **Experience** — the author has first-hand contact with the topic: used the product, visited the place, did the work.
- **Expertise** — the author has knowledge and skills in the subject.
- **Authoritativeness** — the site or author is known as a go-to source in the field.
- **Trust** — the content is accurate and honest, and the site is safe. Google calls trust **the most important** element; the other three support it.

Keep in mind: **E-E-A-T is not a metric** or a score. Quality raters do not change the rankings of specific sites; their ratings help Google check how well its algorithms surface quality content.

## What YMYL is and why the bar is higher

**YMYL (Your Money or Your Life)** covers topics where mistakes can harm a person's health, finances, safety or well-being: medicine, finance, law, major purchases, news about important events.

For YMYL the E-E-A-T bar is **higher**. A sneaker review can rely on personal experience, while advice on treatment or taxes should come from qualified specialists and cite verifiable sources.

## On-site signals that demonstrate E-E-A-T

| Criterion | What you can do |
|---|---|
| Experience | Original photos and screenshots, real case breakdowns, an inside view of the process |
| Expertise | Author bylines, author pages with credentials, review by a specialist |
| Authoritativeness | Mentions and links from industry sites, publications, reviews on external platforms |
| Trust | Contacts, legal details, an About page, privacy policy, HTTPS, clear returns and delivery terms |

A few more practical signals:

- **Published and updated dates**, especially where information goes stale.
- **Links to primary sources**: official documentation, laws, research.
- **Correcting mistakes** and stating limitations honestly.
- **Schema.org markup** for authors and the organization, tying content to real people.
- **No misleading ads** or aggressive pop-ups.

## How to audit your own site

1. Open any article and ask: is it clear **who wrote it** and why they can be trusted?
2. Can a visitor find **contacts and company information** within a minute?
3. Does the text offer something competitors lack: **first-hand experience, examples, data**?
4. Are important claims backed by **sources**?
5. Is information on key pages up to date?

## Common mistakes

- Inventing "experts" with stock photos, which destroys trust.
- Publishing mass content without fact-checking, including AI-generated text.
- Hiding contacts and legal information.
- Believing E-E-A-T can be "switched on" with a single author page.

## FAQ

### Is E-E-A-T a ranking factor?

Not as a single factor. Google uses many signals that together help identify content aligned with E-E-A-T. It is worth working on, but it cannot be measured with one number.

### Can I use AI for content and keep E-E-A-T?

Yes, if a person with real experience in the topic reviews the result, facts are verified and the text is genuinely useful. Google evaluates content quality, not how it was produced.

### Does a small business need E-E-A-T?

Yes, trust above all. Clear contacts, real reviews, honest service descriptions and named authors are basics any site can provide.
