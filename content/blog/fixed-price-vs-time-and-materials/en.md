## The short answer

**Fixed Price** means you agree on a set amount for a clearly described scope of work. **Time and Materials (T&M)** means you pay for the time the team actually spends, at agreed rates.

If you know exactly what needs to be built and do not plan to change it, choose Fixed Price. If the product will take shape as you go, T&M is more honest and usually more cost-effective.

## How risk is distributed

The core difference between the models is who carries the uncertainty.

- **Fixed Price**: the vendor carries the risk of a wrong estimate, so they add a buffer for the unexpected. You pay for predictability.
- **T&M**: the client carries the risk. If the task turns out harder, more hours are spent. But you do not overpay for "insurance" you may never need.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Parameter | Fixed Price | Time and Materials |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | Known upfront | A range is known, final cost depends on actual work |
| Changes | Through a formal change request | Handled as part of normal work |
| Start | Requires a detailed specification | Can start from a general vision |
| Control | Based on the final result | Ongoing: reports, demos, task tracker |
| Client involvement | Minimal after the spec is signed | Continuous: priorities, acceptance |

## What drives the total cost

Fixed Price often looks cheaper on paper, but the final figure depends on several factors:

- **Risk buffer.** The vaguer the requirements, the bigger the safety margin in the price.
- **Cost of changes.** Every deviation from the spec is documented and billed separately.
- **Preparation time.** A detailed specification takes weeks of work on its own.

With T&M, the total depends on how well you manage priorities. A clear backlog and regular acceptance keep the budget under control.

## Which projects suit each model

**Fixed Price works well when:**

- the project is small with a clear scope: a landing page, a corporate site, a specific integration;
- design and a detailed specification are ready;
- the budget is strictly limited and approved in advance, for example in a tender.

**Time and Materials works well when:**

- you are building an MVP and will test hypotheses with real users;
- the product evolves over a long period and requirements change;
- there are complex integrations or unknown technical risks;
- you need a dedicated team for an extended time.

## Common mistakes

- **Fixed Price on a vague spec.** The result is endless arguments about what was in scope.
- **T&M without oversight.** Without a backlog, reports and demos, the budget quietly expands.
- **Choosing by the proposal total alone.** Compare what is included, not just the numbers.

## Hybrid options

Projects are often split. For example, a **fixed-price discovery phase** followed by development on T&M. Or T&M with a **budget cap**: you pay for actual work, but never more than an agreed amount without separate approval.

## FAQ

### Which model is cheaper?

There is no universal answer. With a clear scope, Fixed Price gives predictability; with changing requirements, T&M usually costs less because you do not pay for a risk buffer.

### Can we switch models mid-project?

Yes. It is common to run one phase on one model and the next on another. Just define the transition process in the contract.

### How do I control the budget on T&M?

Agree on regular time reports, a demo after every sprint and a budget cap that cannot be exceeded without your approval.
