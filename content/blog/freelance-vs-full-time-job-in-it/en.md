## The short answer

**A full-time job** fits if you value stability, learning from a strong team and social benefits. **Freelancing** fits if you already have experience and a financial cushion, and you are ready to find clients, keep records and be responsible for everything. Early in a career, employment usually gives more; later, freelancing becomes a real alternative.

## Comparison on key criteria

| Criterion | Full-time job | Freelance |
|---|---|---|
| Income stability | Fixed salary on time | Depends on the flow of projects; empty months happen |
| Income ceiling | Limited by grade and company policy | Depends on rate and workload, and on your sales skills |
| Growth and learning | Code review, mentors, long complex projects | Variety of tasks, but little feedback |
| Taxes | Usually withheld and paid by the employer | You register, keep records and pay yourself |
| Social protection | Paid leave, sick leave, pension contributions — per local law | You have to arrange all of it yourself |
| Freedom | The company sets schedule and tasks | You choose projects, clients and hours |
| Non-technical work | Minimal | Sales, negotiation, invoicing, finding clients |

## Pros and cons of a full-time job

**Pros:**

- predictable income and a clear career ladder;
- growth through experienced colleagues and code review;
- large projects where you see how a product evolves over years;
- the employer handles taxes and reporting.

**Cons:**

- less freedom over tasks and schedule;
- income growth depends on company decisions;
- risk of depending on a single employer.

## Pros and cons of freelancing

**Pros:**

- you choose projects, stack and clients;
- flexible hours and location;
- no hard ceiling: income grows with reputation and rate.

**Cons:**

- an unstable flow of work;
- a large share of time goes to sales, communication and bookkeeping;
- no paid vacation or sick leave unless you budget for them;
- you mostly grow on your own, without code review or mentors.

## Taxes and paperwork

The rules depend on your country and the form of work. Before going freelance, find out:

- which legal status you will work under and how to register;
- which tax regime fits your activity;
- how to receive payments from foreign clients and what documents are required;
- how to make pension and other mandatory contributions yourself.

If in doubt, consult an accountant or tax advisor — it costs less than fixing mistakes later.

## How to choose for your stage and personality

**Lean toward full-time if:**

- you are a junior or just entering the profession;
- you need external structure and feedback;
- you have no financial cushion;
- you are uncomfortable selling your services.

**Lean toward freelancing if:**

- you have experience and a portfolio to show clients;
- you can estimate tasks and negotiate deadlines and budgets;
- you have a cushion for several months without projects;
- you are disciplined and handle uncertainty calmly.

**A middle path** is a full-time job plus small side projects, if your contract allows it. It lets you test freelancing without the risk.

## Common mistakes

- Going freelance right after learning, with no experience working in a team.
- Not including taxes, vacation, sick days and idle time in your rate.
- Working with one big client — the same dependency, without employee protections.
- Not putting agreements in writing: scope, deadlines, payment.

## FAQ

### Can I start my career as a freelancer?

You can, but it is harder: there are no mentors or code reviews, and clients hesitate to trust a project to someone without a portfolio. It is usually easier to gain experience in a team first.

### How do I calculate a freelance rate?

Start from your target income and add taxes, vacation, sick days, time spent finding clients and gaps between projects. A rate equal to a full-time salary divided by hours almost always turns out too low.

### Can I return to a full-time job after freelancing?

Yes. Experience working directly with clients, estimating tasks and owning results is valued by employers — just be ready to show your projects and talk about them.
