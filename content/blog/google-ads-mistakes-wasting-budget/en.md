## The short answer

Google Ads budget is usually lost not to tough competition but to default settings and skipped steps. The main causes:

1. No conversion tracking, or tracking that counts the wrong thing.
2. Ads showing outside your target area.
3. A Search campaign also serving on the Display Network.
4. No negative keywords, and nobody reads the search terms report.
5. Smart bidding switched on without data.

Below is how to spot and fix each one, plus a few less obvious mistakes.

## Mistakes and fixes

### 1. No conversion tracking

**Symptom:** you see clicks and spend but cannot tell which keywords and ads bring leads.

**Fix:** install the Google tag or import conversions from GA4. Track real actions: form submissions, calls, purchases, messenger clicks. Verify each conversion with a test lead.

A related problem is **conversions that count the wrong thing**. If a page view or any click is a primary conversion, bidding will optimize for it. For leads, counting is usually set to "One" per click, so repeated form submissions do not inflate the numbers.

### 2. Wrong location settings

**Symptom:** the locations report shows cities and countries you do not serve.

**Fix:** in location options choose "Presence" instead of "Presence or interest". Exclude areas you do not serve and check the user location report.

### 3. Display Network left on

**Symptom:** a Search campaign gets lots of impressions and cheap, low-quality clicks, and segmenting by Network shows Display.

**Fix:** turn off the Display Network in the Search campaign settings. For display advertising, build a separate campaign with its own ads, audiences and budget. Keep Search Partners only after you have evaluated their traffic.

### 4. No negative keywords

**Symptom:** the search terms report is full of "free", "jobs", "DIY", competitor names or products you do not sell.

**Fix:** create a shared negative keyword list before launch and grow it from the search terms report. Remember that negatives do not cover close variants, so add word forms explicitly.

### 5. Premature smart bidding

**Symptom:** you set a target CPA on a new campaign and get almost no impressions, or your cost per click jumps.

**Fix:** start with a strategy that gathers data: Maximize clicks with a bid limit, or Maximize conversions without a target. Add a target CPA or ROAS once conversions flow steadily, and set the target close to your actual results rather than your wish.

### 6. Broad match with manual bids

**Symptom:** broad keywords on manual CPC bring in a flood of irrelevant searches.

**Fix:** use broad match only with conversion-based smart bidding. Without it, stay with phrase and exact.

### 7. Sending traffic to the home page

**Symptom:** high bounce rate, few leads, a "below average" landing page experience.

**Fix:** point each ad group to a page that answers its searches. Check speed and the mobile version.

### 8. Accepting recommendations blindly

**Symptom:** keywords, budgets and match types change without your involvement.

**Fix:** review your auto-apply recommendation settings and keep only those you understand and agree with.

### 9. Constant tweaking

**Symptom:** the bid strategy is permanently in learning.

**Fix:** batch your changes and give the campaign time to collect data before the next edit.

## Quick audit

| Check | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Conversions are active and count the right action | Goals → Conversions |
| Location option is "Presence" | Campaign settings → Locations |
| Display Network is off in Search | Campaign settings → Networks |
| Negative keywords exist | Shared library → Negative keyword lists |
| Bid strategy matches data volume | Campaign settings → Bidding |

## FAQ

### How do I estimate how much budget is wasted?

Look at spend on search terms with no conversions, on locations outside your area and on the Display segment of Search campaigns. Together they show where to start.

### Do I need to pause the campaign to fix these?

Usually not. Locations, networks, negatives and conversions can be changed on a live campaign. Changing the bid strategy triggers a learning period, so do it separately from other edits.

### How often should I run this audit?

Regularly in the first weeks after launch, then on a schedule, and always after major changes to your website, forms or account structure.
