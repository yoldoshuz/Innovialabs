## The short answer

A **Google Business Profile** is the listing that appears in Google Maps and in the local pack for searches like "dentist near me". For a local business, it is often the main source of calls and direction requests.

The order of work:

1. Create the profile and check for duplicates.
2. Verify ownership.
3. Fill in categories, address, hours, contacts and services.
4. Add photos and a description.
5. Collect reviews and reply to them.
6. Keep information and posts up to date.

## Creation and verification

Go to **business.google.com** with a business Google account and search for your company name. If a listing already exists (a user or Google may have created it), request access instead of creating a new one: duplicates hurt ranking and can lead to suspension.

When creating, set:

- **Name**: exactly as on your signage and documents, with no keywords or city names.
- **Business type**: a location customers visit, or a service area if you travel to customers (couriers, repair technicians). For service-area businesses, hide a home address.

**Verification**: Google offers the methods available to you, such as a video showing the place and signage, phone, SMS, email or postcard. For video, prepare in advance: the exterior sign, the entrance, the workspace and a document or tool proving you run the business. Record it as one continuous clip.

## Filling in the listing

### Categories

The **primary category** affects visibility more than anything else. Choose the most precise one: "Dental clinic", not "Medical center". Add secondary categories only for real lines of business.

### Core details

- Address with an accurate map pin.
- Opening hours, including special hours for holidays.
- A phone number that actually gets answered, and a website.
- Services or products with descriptions.
- Attributes: payment methods, accessibility, parking.

### Description

In plain language: what you do, for whom, and what makes you different. No links, all caps or keyword lists.

## Photos and posts

**Photos** help customers understand where they are going. Add:

- a logo and cover image;
- the storefront with signage, so the entrance is easy to find;
- the interior and the work itself;
- your team and products.

Use real photos, not stock images, and refresh them when things change.

**Posts** (updates, offers, events) appear in the listing. They are not a major ranking factor, but they show the business is active and give customers a reason to choose you.

## Reviews: collecting and replying

Reviews influence both ranking and customer choice.

- **Ask every customer**, not just happy ones. Create a short review link from the profile dashboard, send it after a purchase or service, and display a QR code.
- **Never buy reviews or offer discounts for them.** It violates the policies; such reviews get removed and the profile may be restricted.
- **Reply to every review.** For positive ones, keep it short and specific. For negative ones, stay calm, do not argue, and offer to resolve the issue directly.
- Abusive reviews or reviews from non-customers can be reported, but removal is not guaranteed.

## Why profiles get suspended

Common reasons for restriction or suspension:

- keywords or a city in the business name;
- a virtual office, a coworking space without staffed presence, or a PO box as the address;
- multiple profiles for one location;
- frequent changes to name, address or category;
- fake reviews;
- business types not eligible for profiles (for example, rentals without a customer-facing office).

If your profile is restricted, fix the violation and appeal through the Google help center with evidence: signage photos, registration documents, a lease.

## FAQ

### How long does verification take?

It depends on the method: video and phone are often reviewed faster, while a postcard takes longer. After verification, edits to the listing also go through review.

### Can I create a profile without an office?

Yes, if you serve customers in person at their location. Choose the service-area type and hide your address. Purely online businesses are not eligible.

### What affects position in Google Maps?

Google names three factors: relevance to the query, distance from the searcher, and prominence, which includes reviews and mentions across the web. A complete, up-to-date profile supports relevance and prominence.
