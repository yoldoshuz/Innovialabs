## The main difference in brief

Google and Yandex solve the same problem: show the best answer to a query. That is why **most of the work is shared**: technical cleanliness, speed, clear structure, content that matches intent. The differences are in emphasis:

- **Google** relies more on **link signals**, content quality and expertise, plus technical aspects such as Core Web Vitals.
- **Yandex** puts more weight on **region**, **user behavior** and **commercial factors**, meaning how convenient the site is for buying or ordering.

## Comparison by key points

| Aspect | Google | Yandex |
|---|---|---|
| Links | Important signal, paid links violate the rules | Weaker influence, SEO links can be penalized |
| Region | Inferred from language, domain, content and user location | Site region is set explicitly and strongly affects results |
| Behavioral factors | Not officially confirmed as a direct factor | An important part of ranking |
| Commercial factors | Considered through quality and trust | Significant on their own: contacts, prices, range, delivery |
| Indexing | Usually fast | Can take longer |
| Tools | Google Search Console | Yandex Webmaster, Metrica |
| Local search | Google Business Profile | Yandex Business |

## Region: where the gap is widest

In Yandex, **regionality** is one of the key settings. For many commercial queries, results differ noticeably between cities. To appear in the right region:

- set the region in Yandex Webmaster;
- add your company to Yandex Business with an address;
- show the address and a local phone number on the site.

Google determines region indirectly. Google Business Profile helps, as does content that mentions the city and, for multilingual sites, **hreflang** attributes.

## Behavioral and commercial factors

Yandex cares about what users do after clicking: did they find the answer, did they go back to the results. That is not an invitation to fake behavior, which is punished hard. It is a reason to build pages that are easy to use:

- a clear first screen and a fast answer;
- visible **prices**, payment and delivery terms;
- contacts, company details, ways to get in touch;
- a broad, well-structured product range;
- reviews and information about the company.

The same improvements help in Google too; there they work indirectly, through overall page quality.

## How to optimize for both at once

1. **Technical base**: correct robots.txt and sitemap.xml, canonical URLs, no duplicates, a fast mobile version.
2. **Connect both consoles**: Search Console and Yandex Webmaster surface different issues.
3. **One page, one intent**: build your keyword map from both engines' data (Wordstat for Yandex, Search Console and Keyword Planner for Google).
4. **Commercial elements** on every sales page.
5. **Local listings**: Google Business Profile and Yandex Business.
6. **Natural links and mentions** instead of bought ones.
7. **Track rankings separately** for each engine: the same page can rank differently.

## Common mistakes

- Focusing on one engine when your audience uses both.
- Not setting a region in Yandex for a local business.
- Betting on bought links, which may not work in Yandex and violate Google's rules.
- Ignoring the usability of commercial pages.

## FAQ

### Which search engine matters more for a business in Uzbekistan?

It depends on your audience. Check your analytics to see where visitors come from and split your effort accordingly. Basic optimization works for both anyway.

### Should I build separate site versions for Google and Yandex?

No. It is even risky, because showing different content can look like cloaking. Build one quality site and account for each engine's specifics in settings and content.

### Why does my site rank well in Google but poorly in Yandex?

Common reasons are a missing region setting, weak commercial factors or indexing problems in Yandex. Start by checking Yandex Webmaster.
