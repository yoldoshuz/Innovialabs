## The short answer

- Pick **Google Workspace** when your team lives in the browser, edits documents together a lot and wants minimal administration.
- Pick **Microsoft 365** when **desktop Office apps** matter: complex spreadsheets and macros, exchanging .docx and .xlsx with partners, and company Windows PCs that are managed centrally.

Both include business email on your domain, calendar, video calls, cloud storage and office apps. The difference is **where and how** people work with documents.

## Key differences

| Area | Google Workspace | Microsoft 365 |
|---|---|---|
| Office apps | Docs, Sheets, Slides in the browser | Word, Excel, PowerPoint: web, desktop and mobile |
| Collaboration | Native and real-time, no files to save | Cloud co-authoring, smoothest in the web apps |
| Communication | Gmail, Chat, Meet | Outlook, Teams |
| Storage | Drive and shared drives | OneDrive and SharePoint |
| Administration | Admin console, easier to learn | Admin center, Entra ID, Intune: more power, more complexity |
| Offline | Offline mode in Chrome and mobile apps | Full desktop apps |

## Office apps

**Excel** is still the standard for heavy analysis: Power Query, Power Pivot, VBA, large models. Google Sheets is better for collaboration and integrations through Apps Script, but slows down on heavy files.

**Word** handles complex layouts better: contracts with numbered clauses, styles, tables of contents, tracked changes with outside lawyers. Google Docs is simpler and faster for internal documents.

## Collaboration and storage

In Google Workspace, collaboration is the foundation: one document, one link, edits appear instantly. Microsoft 365 supports co-authoring too, but the team has to get used to keeping files in OneDrive or SharePoint instead of emailing attachments.

Both suites have a separate space for team files: **shared drives** in Google and **SharePoint sites** in Microsoft. Work documents belong there so they do not disappear when an employee leaves.

## Administration and security

Both platforms support two-factor authentication, password policies, device management and data access rules. Microsoft 365 offers deeper control over Windows PCs and more granular access policies, but needs an experienced admin. Google's Admin console is simpler, and a small company can often manage it in-house.

## Pricing tiers

Both are sold **as a per-user monthly subscription** with several tiers. Prices vary by country and change over time, so compare current offers. Look at:

- storage per user or pooled per organization;
- maximum meeting participants and meeting recording;
- whether desktop Office apps are included: the entry-level Microsoft 365 business tier usually offers only web and mobile versions;
- security, archiving and eDiscovery features on higher tiers.

## Migration: plan ahead

1. **Email.** Mailboxes must be moved and the domain's MX records switched. Schedule it for a quiet period.
2. **Files.** Decide what to move and what to archive. Check how formulas and macros behave after conversion.
3. **Calendars and contacts.** Migration tools move them, but verify recurring events and shared calendars by hand.
4. **Integrations.** Your CRM, website forms, mailing services and SSO into other systems may be tied to the old platform.
5. **Training.** A short "where things live now" guide cuts down questions in the first weeks.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing by one manager's habits rather than how the whole team works.
- Paying for a higher tier for a single feature two people use.
- Splitting the team across both suites without clear file-sharing rules.

## FAQ

### Can Google Workspace handle Word and Excel files?

Yes, Docs and Sheets open and edit Office formats. Simple documents convert cleanly, but complex formatting, VBA macros and some Excel functions may behave differently or not work.

### What should a small company without an IT person choose?

Usually Google Workspace, since it is easier to set up and administer. The exception is when core processes depend on desktop Excel or Word.

### Can we use both suites at once?

Technically yes, but it doubles the cost and complicates access management. Most companies pick one main platform and buy specific apps for individual employees when needed.
