## The short answer

A business channel has four main sources of subscribers:

1. **Your own traffic**: website, bot, customers, packaging, physical locations. The cheapest and most relevant.
2. **Cross-promotion** with channels that share your audience but are not competitors.
3. **Ads in other channels**: paid posts.
4. **Telegram Ads**: Telegram's official ad platform.

The key rule: **one invite link per source**. Then you can see which channel works and which one only burns budget.

## Tracked invite links

In the channel settings you can create **additional invite links**. For each one you can set:

- a **name**, such as "site footer", "post in channel X", "QR at checkout";
- an **expiry date** and a **usage limit**;
- **join requests**, if you want to approve subscribers manually or through a bot.

Telegram shows how many people joined through each link. If you know what a placement cost, the **cost per subscriber** is simple: placement cost divided by the number of people who joined via its link.

## Start with your own traffic

People who already bought from you or showed interest are your best audience.

- **Website**: a visible "Our Telegram channel" button in the header or footer and a block after checkout. Say what people get: new arrivals, offers, practical tips.
- **Bot**: after a purchase or an answered question, the bot suggests subscribing and sends the link. If the bot handles join requests, it can greet each newcomer right away.
- **Offline**: a QR code on receipts, packaging, the front desk or business cards, with a separate link for each place.
- **Newsletters and other social networks**: regular mentions with a concrete reason to subscribe.

Don't make subscribing a **requirement** for getting an answer from the bot. It annoys people and brings subscribers who leave at the first chance.

## Cross-promotion

- Look for channels of **similar size** with the same audience but a different product: a salon and a cosmetics store, a renovation studio and a furniture maker.
- Agree on a format: swapping posts, a joint giveaway, or a shared **channel folder** that subscribers add with one link.
- Write the recommendation **in your own words**, not as a copy of their description.

## Ads in other channels

Before buying a post, check the channel:

- the ratio of **post views** to subscribers; a very low ratio suggests a dead or inflated audience;
- the **subscriber graph**: sudden steps with no visible reason are a warning sign;
- real **comments and reactions**;
- how many **ads** already run there and what kind.

Channel analytics services make these checks easier. Write the ad post **natively**: the problem, why the channel is useful, an example of content, the link. One post, one clearly named link.

## Telegram Ads

**Telegram Ads** shows short sponsored messages in public channels. You can target specific channels, topics and other parameters and send people to your channel, a post or a bot.

- It fits when you need a **predictable, scalable** flow rather than one-off posts.
- The ad text is short, so the benefit has to be stated sharply.
- Compare it with paid posts on one metric: the **cost of a subscriber who stays**.

Terms and ad requirements change, so check the platform's official page.

## Common mistakes

- Buying "subscribers" or fake growth. They don't read, and they distort stats and reach.
- Advertising an empty channel. Fill it with posts first so newcomers have something to read.
- Looking only at growth and ignoring how many people **left** after a campaign.
- Using one link for every source.

## FAQ

### How many subscribers do I need before buying ads?

There is no magic number. What matters more is having a few dozen solid posts and a clear rhythm, so new subscribers stay.

### Which is better: ads in channels or Telegram Ads?

They are different tools. Channel posts give a native recommendation from the author; Telegram Ads gives controlled, scalable reach. Try both and compare the cost of a subscriber who stays.

### How do I know subscribers didn't leave a week later?

Watch unsubscribes in channel stats after each campaign and compare periods. If unsubscribes rise after an ad, that channel's audience isn't a fit.
