## The short answer

Employers value both, but at different moments. **Hard skills** — languages, frameworks, databases, tools — get you through the technical interview. **Soft skills** determine how easy you are to work with and how much you can be trusted. As grades rise, technical levels among candidates even out, and soft skills become the deciding factor.

## Soft skills that matter in IT

- **Communication.** Asking clear questions, explaining decisions, writing understandable messages and task descriptions.
- **Estimation.** Sizing tasks, spotting risks and flagging early when an estimate changes.
- **Ownership.** Taking a task to the result, not to "my part is done".
- **Handling feedback.** Accepting code review comments calmly and giving constructive feedback to others.
- **Self-organization.** Setting priorities and staying on track without constant supervision.

## What is expected at each grade

| Grade | Hard skills | Soft skills in focus |
|---|---|---|
| Junior | Stack basics, solving assigned tasks | Asking questions in time, accepting feedback, learning |
| Middle | Building features independently, understanding project architecture | Estimation, ownership of results, clear team communication |
| Senior | Architecture decisions, system quality and reliability | Influencing the team, mentoring, negotiating with the business, handling uncertainty |

A **junior** who says "I'm stuck" in time is more valuable than one who silently spends three times longer on a task.

A **middle** developer does not just complete the task but understands why it is needed and warns about risks.

A **senior** is responsible not only for their code but for how the team works and where the system is heading.

## How to show soft skills in an interview

Saying "I'm a good communicator" is useless. You need **specific stories**. A handy structure is STAR:

- **S**ituation — what the context was;
- **T**ask — what needed to be done;
- **A**ction — what you personally did;
- **R**esult — how it ended and what you learned.

Prepare 3–4 stories in advance:

- a time you missed or nearly missed a deadline and what you did;
- a disagreement with a colleague and how you reached a decision;
- tough feedback you received and what you changed;
- a task nobody wanted that you took on.

Soft skills show in technical interviews too: **think aloud**, clarify requirements, admit when you do not know something and explain how you would find out.

## How to show them at work

- Post a short status on your tasks without being asked.
- Raise risks right away, not on the deadline day.
- In code review, comment on the code, not the person.
- After finishing a task, check that it actually solves the user's problem.
- Ask for feedback yourself — from your lead and teammates.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating soft skills as optional.** A technically strong but unmanageable developer is hard to scale within a team.
- **Confusing soft skills with charisma.** This is about predictability and clarity, not being the life of the party.
- **Neglecting hard skills.** Without a technical foundation, soft skills will not get you through the interview.

## FAQ

### Can I develop soft skills if I'm an introvert?

Yes. Most of the skills that matter — written communication, estimation, ownership — do not require being outgoing. They are skills you build through practice.

### What matters more for a first job?

Hard skills get you through the technical part. But among candidates with a similar level, companies often choose the one who explains decisions better and reacts calmly to feedback.

### How do I know which soft skills to improve?

Ask your lead or mentor for feedback and look for recurring comments in code reviews and retrospectives.
