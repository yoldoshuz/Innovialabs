## What a tech lead is

A **tech lead** is an engineer responsible for the technical direction of a team. They still write code, but their main output is the work of the whole team: sound architectural decisions, predictable releases and a clear technical plan.

Note that the role differs between companies. In some, the tech lead also acts as the people manager; in others, a separate manager handles people while the tech lead focuses on technology. Clarify expectations before accepting the role.

## Responsibilities beyond coding

- **Technical vision**: architecture, standards, tool choices, managing technical debt.
- **Planning**: breaking large initiatives into phases, estimating, identifying dependencies and risks.
- **Distributing work**: who does what, balancing task complexity and people's growth.
- **Quality**: code review, agreements on testing, CI and releases.
- **Communication**: translating business requirements into technical terms and back.
- **Protecting team focus**: filtering out urgent but unimportant requests.
- **Growing engineers**: feedback, mentoring, support on hard problems.

The share of your own code usually drops noticeably. That is normal, and it is often the biggest source of discomfort for new tech leads.

## Key skills

| Skill | What it looks like | How to practice |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | A multi-sprint roadmap with risks | Plan one initiative end to end |
| Delegation | Handing off a task with context and done criteria | Delegate interesting tasks, not just chores |
| Stakeholder communication | Explaining trade-offs without jargon | Join meetings with product and business |
| Decision-making | Deciding with incomplete information, in writing | Write short documents with options |
| Feedback | Specific, timely, relevant | Practice in code reviews and 1:1s |

## Delegation: the biggest barrier

Strong developers find it hard to hand off work: doing it yourself feels faster and safer. But if the tech lead takes every hard task, the team doesn't grow and the lead becomes a bottleneck.

Good delegation includes:

1. **Context**: why the task matters and how it connects to the goal.
2. **Done criteria**: what counts as a result.
3. **Boundaries**: what the person decides alone and what they discuss.
4. **Checkpoints**: when you will look at intermediate results.

Hand off the whole task, not step-by-step instructions. Support people, but don't rewrite their work for them.

## How to step into the role

1. **Tell your manager about the goal.** Without that, you may not be considered.
2. **Lead one project**: plan it, distribute tasks, track status.
3. **Write technical documents** with options and trade-offs and bring them up for discussion.
4. **Build a relationship with product**: take part in requirements and estimation discussions.
5. **Help colleagues grow**: mentoring, reviews, onboarding.
6. **Ask for feedback** from the team and your manager on how you are doing.

## Common mistakes of new tech leads

- Continuing to work as the most productive developer and neglecting the team.
- Making every decision alone instead of involving people.
- Avoiding hard conversations about work quality and deadlines.
- Promising deadlines to the business without checking with the team.

## FAQ

### How is a tech lead different from an engineering manager?

A tech lead focuses on technical decisions and team output; a manager focuses on people: hiring, development, performance evaluation. In small teams the roles are often combined, so clarify the specific set of responsibilities.

### Does a tech lead need to be the strongest developer on the team?

No. You need enough technical depth to make and explain decisions, but you don't have to be the best at everything. Relying on your teammates' expertise matters more.

### Can I go back from tech lead to an engineering role?

Yes, in many companies this is a normal move. Leadership experience remains valuable in a strong individual contributor role as well.
