## The short answer: where to start

The most common mistake is choosing a CRM by its ads or by the longest feature list. A small business needs a system that sales staff will **actually use every day**. So the order is:

1. Map your sales process.
2. List must-have features and integrations.
3. Shortlist 2-3 candidates.
4. Trial them on real deals.
5. Estimate the total cost.

## Step 1. Map your sales process

Before any vendor demo calls, answer these questions:

- **Where do customers come from?** Website, Instagram, Telegram, calls, marketplaces, referrals.
- **What stages does a deal go through?** For example: inquiry, qualification, proposal, payment, delivery.
- **Who does what?** How many salespeople, is there a manager, who owns repeat sales.
- **Where do you lose customers today?** Forgotten callbacks, lost messenger inquiries, slow replies.

Put it on one page. This document becomes your yardstick for comparing systems.

## Step 2. List your must-haves

Split requirements into "must have" and "nice to have". Must-haves usually include:

- A **deal pipeline** with configurable stages.
- **Tasks and reminders** for salespeople.
- **Messengers and telephony** — chats and calls inside the customer card.
- **Website forms** — inquiries land in the CRM automatically.
- A **mobile app**, if your team works on the road.
- **Access rights**, so salespeople see only their own deals.
- **Reports** on stage conversion and team performance.

## Step 3. Check integrations

A CRM rarely lives alone. Find out how it connects to what you already use:

| What to connect | What to check |
|---|---|
| Website and forms | Ready widget or API, passing UTM tags |
| Messengers | Telegram, WhatsApp, Instagram — native or via a third-party service |
| Telephony | Your provider, call recording |
| Accounting and 1C | Syncing products, invoices, stock |
| Payments | Invoices and payment links from the deal card |

If an integration is missing, check for an **open API** and webhooks — then it can be built.

## Step 4. Trial on real deals

Demo data shows nothing. During the trial:

- set up the pipeline for your own process;
- connect at least one real lead source;
- let 1-2 salespeople run **real deals** in the system for a week or two;
- ask them what feels awkward — their opinion matters more than the owner's.

## Step 5. Estimate the total cost

The license price is only part of the spend. Account for:

- the per-user plan and how it grows with the team;
- paid add-ons and integrations;
- implementation and setup;
- staff training;
- migrating data from spreadsheets or an old system;
- support and customization.

Compare systems by the sum of these factors, not by the plan price.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying "for growth"** — a complex system nobody fills in.
- **No owner for the rollout** — someone has to configure it and keep the team disciplined.
- **Migrating chaos** — old spreadsheets moved as-is, duplicates included.
- **No working rules** — what counts as a lead, when to move a stage, when to close a deal.

## FAQ

### Cloud or self-hosted CRM?

For a small business cloud is usually easier: no server of your own, updates arrive automatically. Self-hosted is worth considering with special data storage requirements or deep customization.

### How many systems should I compare?

Two or three are enough. More candidates drag out the decision and leave no time to test each on real deals.

### Can I start with spreadsheets and move to a CRM later?

Yes, while inquiries are few and you have one salesperson. Once leads start getting lost or you have several channels, a spreadsheet no longer copes — that is the signal to switch.
