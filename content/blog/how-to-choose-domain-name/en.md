## The short answer

A domain is an address customers will say out loud, type by hand and see in ads. So the main test is simple: **it is easy to remember, dictate and type without mistakes**. Everything else, from the zone to typo variants and legal checks, helps protect that address.

## Length and memorability

- **Shorter is better**, but not at the cost of meaning. A short clear word beats a string of letters.
- **One or two words** is the sweet spot. Long phrases like "best-windows-in-tashkent" are hard to remember and easy to mistype.
- **Match your business name.** If the domain differs from the brand, customers will look for you in the wrong place.

## Spelling: the dictation test

Imagine reading the address over the phone. If you have to add "with a hyphen", "double s" or "s-h, not x", the domain will lose visitors.

What to avoid:
- **Hyphens.** People forget them and confuse them with underscores when dictating.
- **Numbers** unless they are part of the brand: is it "2" or "two", "4" or "for"?
- **Double letters where words join**, like "shopperry": people drop one.
- **Ambiguous transliteration.** This matters a lot for Uzbek and Russian names: "sh" or "sch", "ch" or "tch", "x" or "h", "ya" or "ia". Uzbek letters o‘ and g‘ lose their apostrophe in a Latin domain, so plan for that too.

The check is easy: dictate the name to a few people and ask them to write it down. If even one writes it differently, consider another option or buy that variant as well.

## Choosing a zone

| Zone | When it fits |
|---|---|
| **.uz** | The business operates in Uzbekistan and local trust and recognition matter |
| **.com** | International audience or plans to expand abroad |
| Other country zones | A separate market with its own site |
| Topic zones (.tech, .app, .shop and others) | As an addition, when the main option is taken, or for a separate project |

A zone gives no search ranking advantage by itself, but it shapes how people perceive the address. Local audiences usually expect the national zone or .com.

## Trademark checks

Before buying, make sure the name **does not match someone else's registered trademark** in your field. Otherwise the domain can be disputed, and you may have to rebrand after already investing in marketing.

What to check:
- the national trademark database in the country where you operate;
- the WIPO Global Brand Database if you plan to enter other markets;
- a web and social media search for companies with similar names in the same niche.

If the brand matters to you, consider registering your own trademark as well.

## Defensive registrations

Buy not only the main domain but also variants that protect you from confusion and competitors:

- **common typos**: a missing or doubled letter, neighboring keys;
- **key zones**, for example .uz and .com;
- **an alternative transliteration** if the name can be written in more than one way.

These extra domains do not need content: just redirect them to the main address.

## Checking a domain's history

If the name was used before, look at its past: what the site used to contain and whether the domain ended up on spam blocklists. An old bad reputation can hurt email delivery and search visibility.

## Common mistakes

- **Registering the domain to an employee or contractor.** Your company should be the owner in the registry, and you should hold access to the control panel.
- **Forgetting to renew.** Turn on auto-renewal and keep a working email address for notifications.
- **Not checking social networks.** It helps when the domain and account handles match.
- **Choosing a name tied to one product or city** when you plan to grow.

## FAQ

### Do I need a keyword in the domain for SEO?

Not really. A keyword in the domain has minimal effect, and a long keyword-stuffed address is harder to remember. It matters more that the domain matches your brand and is convenient for people.

### What if the domain I want is already taken?

Check whether it is in use. If not, you can try contacting the owner, but the price may be high. It is often easier to add a short clear word to the name or choose another zone.

### How many domains should I buy at once?

The main domain plus the variants that genuinely protect the brand: key zones and the most likely typos. Buying dozens of variants just in case usually makes no sense.
