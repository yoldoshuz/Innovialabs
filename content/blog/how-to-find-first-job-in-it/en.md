## The short answer

Landing a first IT job is not one lucky application — it is a **system**: a clear target role, ready materials (resume, GitHub, portfolio), several search channels and regular applications with a look at the results. Without experience you compete not on years but on **evidence**: projects, attention to detail and how fast you learn.

## Step 1. Define your target role

Applying to everything works worse than searching for a specific role. Pick one or two, for example:

- Junior Frontend Developer;
- Junior QA Engineer;
- Backend developer intern;
- Junior Data Analyst.

Prepare your resume, profile and projects for that role. If you search in two directions, make two separate resumes.

## Step 2. Prepare the basics before applying

Before you start, have these ready:

- **a one-page resume** with projects near the top;
- **GitHub** with pinned projects and READMEs;
- **2–4 finished projects** matching the target role;
- **LinkedIn** with a clear headline;
- **a cover note template** you adapt for each vacancy.

## Step 3. Where to look

Use several channels at once — each has its own vacancies.

| Channel | What to know |
|---|---|
| Job boards (hh.uz, LinkedIn Jobs and others) | The most vacancies, but also the most competition. Set up filters and alerts |
| Telegram channels with IT vacancies | Popular in Uzbekistan and the CIS. Roles close quickly — respond the day they are posted |
| Company career pages | Some vacancies are only published there |
| Referrals | Often the most effective channel: your resume reaches the hiring manager directly |
| Internships and junior programs | Designed specifically for people without experience |
| Meetups, hackathons, tech communities | Connections that later turn into referrals |

## How to get a referral

Referrals are not only for people with connections. You can build them:

- tell friends and classmates that you are looking for work, and what kind;
- go to meetups and ask speakers questions;
- join hackathons — a way to show how you work;
- message developers at companies you like: briefly, politely, asking about the team rather than "please get me hired".

An example short message:

> Hi! I'm a junior frontend developer and I'm interested in your company. I saw the internship opening — could you share what the team looks for in candidates? My GitHub: link.

## Step 4. How many applications to send

There is no exact number — a lot depends on the market and the role. Consistency and quality matter more:

- **set a weekly goal** you can sustain, for example 5–10 tailored applications a week;
- **tailor every application**: headline, skill order, a couple of sentences in the cover note;
- **keep a tracker**: company, date, channel, status, next step;
- **review your funnel** every two weeks.

| What happens | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No replies at all | Resume or choice of vacancies | Rewrite the resume, check fit with requirements |
| Calls, but rejections after the first chat | Self-presentation | Rehearse talking about yourself and your projects |
| Failing technical interviews | Gaps in fundamentals | Revisit the basics, practice typical tasks |

## Step 5. How to stand out

- **Apply fast**: your chances are better in the first hours or day after posting.
- **Write a cover note**: 3–5 sentences on why this company and which of your projects fits.
- **Do take-home tasks carefully**: a README, clean code, a setup that runs.
- **Show interest in the product**: try the company's site or app and mention it.
- **After an interview**, send a short thank-you message.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting to feel "fully ready" and not applying for months.
- Sending the same resume to hundreds of vacancies without tailoring.
- Applying only to roles that require nothing — there are few and competition is huge.
- Not asking for feedback after a rejection.

## FAQ

### Should I apply if I don't meet every requirement?

Yes, if you meet the core ones. Job requirements often describe an ideal candidate, not the minimum bar.

### Should I accept an unpaid internship?

Judge by value: is there a mentor, real tasks and a clear timeframe? If you are being used as free labor with no learning, keep looking.

### How long does finding a first job take?

It depends on the role, the market, how well you prepared and how regularly you apply. Watch your funnel: if applications are not working, change the approach rather than just sending more.
