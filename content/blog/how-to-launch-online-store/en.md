## The short answer

An online store goes live in six stages, and the order matters:

1. **Niche and catalog** — what you sell, to whom, and how you present it.
2. **Platform** — what the site runs on.
3. **Payments** — how money reaches you.
4. **Delivery** — how goods reach the customer.
5. **Legal basics** — what you need to sell legally.
6. **Testing** — checking the whole customer journey before the first real order.

Below is what to do at each stage, with a short checklist.

## 1. Niche and catalog

A website will not fix a weak assortment. Before development, answer three questions: who the buyer is, why they would buy from you rather than on a marketplace, and how many items you can actually keep in stock.

A catalog is data, not pictures. The cleaner it is before development, the faster and cheaper the launch.

**Checklist:**

- Categories and their hierarchy are defined.
- Each category has a list of attributes for filters (size, color, material).
- Products have SKUs, prices, stock levels and consistent naming rules.
- Photos share one style; descriptions are written, not copied from the supplier.
- You know where stock data comes from: a spreadsheet, 1C or a warehouse system.

## 2. Platform

There are three main paths:

| Option | Good fit when | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS store builder | Small catalog, need to start fast | Limited customization, dependence on the vendor |
| Ready-made CMS (WooCommerce, OpenCart, etc.) | Medium catalog, standard processes | Plugins need updates and compatibility checks |
| Custom development | Non-standard logic, integrations, high load | Slower start, needs a team for maintenance |

**Checklist:**

- The platform supports the payment providers and couriers you need.
- There is an integration or API for your accounting system.
- Multiple languages are possible if you sell in more than one.
- It is clear who will update and maintain the site after launch.

## 3. Payments

In Uzbekistan the baseline is **Payme** and **Click**; for international sales, gateways such as Stripe. Cash on delivery is still a valid option, especially early on.

**Checklist:**

- A contract with the payment provider is signed; live and test keys are issued.
- An order becomes "paid" only after the provider confirms it to your server, not when the customer is redirected back.
- Cancellations and refunds are planned: who performs them and how the order reflects them.
- The customer gets a payment confirmation by SMS, email or Telegram.

## 4. Delivery

Decide who delivers: your own courier, a delivery service or customer pickup. That choice drives shipping calculation at checkout and the order statuses.

**Checklist:**

- Delivery zones and terms are described on the site in plain language.
- Shipping cost is calculated automatically or from a clear table.
- Orders have statuses: received, packed, handed to courier, delivered.
- Customers learn about status changes without calling the store.

## 5. Legal basics

Requirements depend on the country and business form, so do the final check with a lawyer and an accountant. Usually you need:

- a registered company or sole proprietorship;
- a **public offer** or terms of sale, plus a returns policy;
- a **privacy policy** and consent to personal data processing;
- receipts issued according to local rules (fiscal receipts in Uzbekistan).

Also check personal data storage rules: in Uzbekistan the law requires citizens' data to be stored inside the country, which affects your hosting choice.

## 6. Pre-launch testing

Walk through the entire customer journey on real devices, phones first.

**Checklist:**

- A test order goes from cart to "delivered".
- Test-mode payment works, and so do cancellation and refund.
- Errors are handled: declined card, out-of-stock item, wrong address.
- Emails and notifications arrive and look right.
- Analytics is installed, with a goal on order completion.
- The site loads quickly on mobile data.
- Live keys are in production; test keys are not.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with design before the catalog is ready.
- Marking an order paid because the customer landed on the "Thank you" page.
- Launching without an order-handling process: who calls, packs and ships.
- Postponing legal documents "for later".

## FAQ

### Should I start with my own store or a marketplace?

A marketplace brings traffic immediately but takes a commission and control over your customer base. Your own store needs investment in promotion, but customers and data stay with you. Many sellers start on a marketplace and build their own site in parallel.

### Can I launch without online payments?

Yes, cash on delivery works for the first sales. Add online payment soon after launch, though: some buyers will not place an order without it.

### Do I need a 1C integration from day one?

If you have few items and stock rarely changes, you can start with manual updates. Once mismatches between the site and the warehouse start causing cancelled orders, the integration pays for itself.
