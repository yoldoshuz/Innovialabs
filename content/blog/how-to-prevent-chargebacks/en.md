## The short answer

A **chargeback** is a refund forced by the cardholder's bank rather than issued by the store. The buyer calls their bank and says they did not make the payment, did not receive the item or got the wrong thing. The bank pulls the amount back from the merchant and opens a dispute. Even if you win, you spend time on it; if you lose, you lose the money, often the product, and usually a dispute fee as well. Preventing a chargeback is almost always cheaper than fighting one.

## Why chargebacks happen

The reasons fall into three groups:

- **Fraud.** Someone paid with a stolen card. The real cardholder sees an unfamiliar charge and disputes it.
- **Service complaints.** The item never arrived, arrived damaged, does not match the description, or a subscription could not be cancelled.
- **Friendly fraud and confusion.** The buyer did not recognise the store name on the statement, forgot the purchase, a family member paid, or someone deliberately disputes a legitimate purchase.

The second and third groups depend heavily on you: how clearly you sell and how quickly you respond.

## How the dispute process works

Details vary by payment provider, bank and card network, but the overall flow is:

1. The buyer contacts their bank.
2. The issuing bank raises a chargeback and the amount is held or debited from the merchant.
3. Your payment provider notifies you with a **reason code** and a response deadline.
4. You gather evidence and submit a response.
5. The bank decides. Additional rounds are possible.

Response windows are limited, so make sure dispute notifications reach a named person instead of getting lost in a shared inbox.

## What a chargeback costs

Direct and indirect losses:

- the purchase amount and often the product itself;
- the provider's fee per dispute, regardless of the outcome;
- staff time spent collecting evidence;
- a **rising dispute ratio**: if it crosses the card networks' or provider's thresholds, you may face fines, extra monitoring or loss of card acceptance.

The last point is the most dangerous: losing the ability to accept cards is far worse than losing one dispute.

## How to prevent chargebacks

### A clear descriptor

The **statement descriptor** is the text the buyer sees on their bank statement. It should match your recognisable store or brand name, not a legal entity name the buyer has never heard of. If your provider allows it, add a website or phone number.

### Delivery proof

- ship valuable orders with tracking;
- keep proof of delivery, a signature or a photo;
- for digital goods, log access or download events with date and IP.

### Fraud checks

- use **3-D Secure** where available: after successful authentication, liability for fraud disputes often shifts to the issuing bank;
- enable CVC and address checks if your provider supports them;
- review suspicious orders: card country differs from shipping country, many attempts with different cards, rush delivery of expensive goods to a new address.

### Responsive support

Many chargebacks start because the buyer could not reach you.

- show contacts on the site, in the confirmation email and, where possible, in the descriptor;
- reply quickly and refund on your own when a complaint is valid: a normal refund costs less than a dispute;
- for subscriptions, make cancellation easy and send reminders before renewal charges.

### Transparent terms

Publish delivery, return and subscription terms before payment and keep a record of the buyer's acceptance.

## What to collect for a dispute response

| Dispute reason | Useful evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Fraud | 3-D Secure result, IP, customer order history |
| Item not received | Tracking number, proof of delivery |
| Not as described | Product page as of the order date, messages, photos |
| Subscription | Accepted terms, reminders, usage history |

## FAQ

### How is a chargeback different from a refund?

A refund is initiated by the store and you control it. A chargeback is initiated by the buyer's bank, the money is taken forcibly, and you pay a dispute fee and risk your standing with the provider.

### Should I fight every chargeback?

No. Respond when you have strong evidence. If the complaint is valid or evidence is thin, it is wiser to accept the loss and fix the cause behind the dispute.

### Does 3-D Secure protect against all disputes?

No. It reduces risk on fraud disputes but does not cover claims such as "item not received" or "not as described".
