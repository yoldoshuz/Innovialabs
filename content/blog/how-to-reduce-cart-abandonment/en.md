## The short answer

Carts are abandoned for two kinds of reasons. The first kind is **unavoidable**: people compare prices, save items for later or just browse. The second kind is **the store's own problems**: surprise shipping costs, forced registration, an awkward form, a missing payment method.

Focus on the second kind. Fix the store first, then set up a **recovery flow** for people who leave anyway.

## Main causes and what to do

| Cause | Fix in the store |
|---|---|
| Unexpected shipping, fees, taxes | Show delivery cost on the product page and in the cart, add a city-based calculator |
| Forced registration | Guest checkout, account creation after payment in one click |
| Few payment options | Add popular local methods, cards, cash on delivery |
| Long or complex form | Remove extra fields, add autofill and phone sign-in |
| Slow or unsuitable delivery | Several carriers, pickup points, clear delivery times |
| Low trust in the site | Contacts, company details, return terms, reviews, HTTPS |
| Errors and slow loading | Monitor checkout errors, optimize the mobile version |
| Unclear return terms | A short return policy next to the pay button |

## Fixes in the store itself

**Transparent price.** The most common reason to leave is a final total that is higher than expected. Show delivery as early as possible: "Delivery in Tashkent from …", a free-shipping threshold in the cart, a cost estimate by address before payment.

**Buying without barriers.** Guest checkout, minimal fields, autofill. If you need accounts for a loyalty program, offer one after payment.

**Payment people are used to.** Buyers in Uzbekistan expect Payme, Click and Uzcard/Humo cards; international buyers expect Visa/Mastercard. Make sure that after a failed payment the buyer can pick another method immediately without refilling the form.

**A persistent cart.** The cart should survive a closed tab and a switch from phone to laptop for signed-in users, and at least stay in the browser for guests.

**Trust signals.** A support phone or messenger link on the checkout page, clear delivery times and a link to return terms.

## Recovery flows

If the buyer left a contact or is signed in, you can bring them back. A typical sequence:

1. **First reminder** after a few hours: "You left items in your cart" with a link that opens the same cart.
2. **Second** a day later: answers to common questions about delivery and payment, product reviews.
3. **Third**, if needed: a time-limited perk such as free delivery. Do not lead with a discount, or you will teach people to abandon carts to get a promo code.

Channels include **email, a Telegram bot, push notifications and SMS**. Use only those the buyer has consented to, and always include a way to opt out.

Technical essentials:

- the cart is stored on the server and linked to a user or contact;
- the link restores the cart in one click, without signing in or searching for items;
- reminders stop as soon as the order is paid;
- if an item went out of stock or changed price, the message reflects that.

## How to measure

- **Cart abandonment rate**: how many created carts did not become orders.
- **Step-by-step checkout funnel**: where exactly people drop off.
- **Recovery flow conversion**: how many orders came from reminder links.
- **Profitability**: revenue from recovered orders minus discounts and messaging costs.

Watch the trend after each change rather than a single day.

## Common mistakes

- Launching reminder emails before fixing hidden costs in checkout.
- Offering a discount in the very first reminder.
- Sending reminders to people who already bought.
- Messaging without consent.
- Counting every cart as "abandoned", including price-comparers, and expecting all of them to return.

## FAQ

### When should the first reminder go out?

Usually within a few hours, while the purchase is still on the buyer's mind. Find the exact interval by testing: shorter for impulse purchases, longer for expensive items people think about.

### Can I recover a buyer who left no contact?

Not directly, but you can reach them with retargeting ads if your privacy policy and cookie consent allow it. A better approach is to ask for phone or email on the first checkout step so the contact is saved early.

### Do I need a discount to recover a cart?

Not always. A reminder and answers to doubts are often enough. Keep the discount for the last message and check whether it pays off.
