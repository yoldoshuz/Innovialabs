## How to start freelancing

Freelancing is not just coding or designing on your own terms. It also means sales, negotiation and bookkeeping. The right time to start is when you can **take a task to a finished result without a mentor**. The short plan:

1. Choose a niche where you are genuinely strong.
2. Build a portfolio of 2-3 clear case studies.
3. Find your first orders on platforms or through your network.
4. Put every job in writing.
5. Decide in advance how you will receive payments.
6. Collect reviews and repeat clients deliberately.

## Choosing a niche

"I build websites" is too broad: a client cannot see why you are better than a hundred others. A niche combines a **skill** with a **client type**:

- Next.js landing pages for online schools;
- Telegram booking bots for salons and clinics;
- mobile app UI design for startups;
- improvements to online stores on a specific CMS.

A narrow niche makes your portfolio, profile text and estimates easier: tasks repeat, so you get faster and more confident. You can widen it later.

## Platforms vs direct clients

| | Platforms (Upwork, Fiverr, Kwork, etc.) | Direct clients |
|---|---|---|
| Order flow | Available from day one, but crowded | You have to find it |
| Payment protection | Often escrow | Only the contract and prepayment |
| Fees | The platform takes a cut | None |
| Price | Pressure to undercut | Easier to hold your rate |
| Relationship | Tied to the platform | Long-term, through referrals |

A sensible starting strategy is to **use both in parallel**. Platforms give you first reviews and negotiation practice, while direct clients from your network, Telegram communities and LinkedIn gradually become the core of your income.

## Landing the first orders

- **A profile for one niche**: the headline states which problem you solve, not just "Frontend developer".
- **A portfolio with context**: the task, what you did, the result. A study project works too if it looks like a real-world task.
- **A personal proposal**: reread the brief, ask 1-2 clarifying questions and suggest a concrete approach. Template proposals get ignored.
- **A small first project**: a small task lowers the client's risk and earns you a review.
- **Your network**: tell former colleagues and classmates what you do. First orders often come from there.

## Contracts and terms

Even with a small client, fix the agreement in writing — a contract, or at least a detailed message the client has confirmed. Include:

- **scope**: what is included and, just as important, what is not;
- **deadlines** and milestones;
- **price and payment schedule**: prepayment, milestone payments;
- **number of revision rounds**;
- **transfer of rights** to code and designs after payment;
- what happens if the project is stopped.

One rule that saves a lot of stress: with direct clients, **do not start without a prepayment**, and do not hand over source files before the final payment.

## Getting paid from Uzbekistan

Payment services change their coverage, so check the current terms before your first order. The main options:

- **Withdrawing from a platform** to a service such as Payoneer and then to a card or bank account — confirm that the route is supported for Uzbekistan residents.
- **SWIFT transfer** to a foreign-currency account at a local bank — suitable for direct international clients.
- **Local clients** — payment in sum under a contract.

On the tax side, look at the legal statuses available: **individual entrepreneur**, self-employed status, or **IT Park** residency for exporting IT services. Conditions depend on the type of activity and change over time, so a one-off consultation with an accountant is worth it.

## Building a reputation

- Deliver **on time or warn early** — silence hurts more than a delay.
- After a project, politely ask for a review and permission to show the work.
- Offer post-launch support: that is how repeat clients appear.
- Write short case studies in your profile or blog — they sell better than a list of technologies.

## FAQ

### Can I start freelancing without company experience?

Yes, if you can finish projects on your own. Start with small tasks in your niche and study projects presented as case studies. Still, working in a team speeds up growth noticeably: you learn processes you will later need with clients.

### What if a client does not pay?

Prevention matters most: prepayment, milestone payments and handing over source files only after the final payment. If a problem arises, rely on the written agreement, and on a platform, contact its support.

### How many projects do I need in a portfolio?

Two or three strong projects in your chosen niche, with a clear description of the task and result, are enough. Ten unrelated pieces without context are less convincing.
