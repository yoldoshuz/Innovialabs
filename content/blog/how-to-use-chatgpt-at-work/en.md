## What to delegate to AI and what not to

A good rule: **AI writes the draft, a person writes the final version**. ChatGPT and similar assistants are strong where you need to write, rephrase, structure or explain something quickly. They are weak where you need exact facts, fresh data and accountability for a decision.

| Good fit | Use with care |
|---|---|
| Drafts of emails and replies | Legal and financial conclusions |
| Report structure, key points, document summaries | Figures, dates, quotes, source links |
| Excel / Google Sheets formulas, explaining errors | Calculations going straight into official reports |
| Translation and editing | Texts containing confidential data |

## Emails

```text
Write a polite email to a client: the delivery is delayed by 3 days
because of customs. Offer a discount on shipping for the next order.
Business tone, no excessive apologies, under 120 words.
```

State **who it is for, why, the tone and the length** — the answer will be close to what you need right away. Ask for 2-3 versions and pick the best.

## Reports and documents

```text
Below are my notes on the month's results. Create a report structure
for my manager: results, problems, plan for next month.
Keep it short, use bullet points. Do not add data that is not in the notes.
[paste notes]
```

The line "do not add data that is not in the notes" noticeably reduces the risk of made-up facts.

## Spreadsheets

```text
In Google Sheets, column A has dates and column B has sales amounts.
I need a formula that sums sales for the current month.
Explain the formula step by step.
```

AI is also useful for explaining someone else's complex formula, suggesting a pivot table or finding the cause of an error. Test the formula on a few rows where you already know the result.

## Research and making sense of information

```text
Explain in simple terms what [term] means and give an example
from retail. If you are not sure, say so.
```

For a first look at a topic, comparing options and preparing questions for an expert, AI saves time. But verify specific facts, laws and prices against the original source.

## Always double-check

- **Figures, dates, names and titles** — the model can be confidently wrong.
- **Links and sources** — they may not exist.
- **Calculations** — recompute key numbers yourself or in a spreadsheet.
- **Tone and promises** in client emails: AI may promise something your company does not do.
- **Compliance** with company rules and the law.

## Data safety

- Do not paste passwords, client personal data, contracts or internal financials into public versions of these services.
- Find out whether your company has a business plan or an approved tool and what its data processing terms are.
- Anonymize: replace names and amounts with placeholders when they do not matter for the task.

## FAQ

### Can I fully hand client correspondence to ChatGPT?

Better not. Use it for drafts, but send emails only after reading them: the model can get facts wrong or promise too much.

### How do I get more accurate answers?

Give context: who you are, who the text is for, what format and length you need. Attach the source data and ask the model not to add anything beyond it.

### Is it safe to upload work documents?

It depends on the service and plan. Check company rules and the data processing terms first, and anonymize confidential information where possible.
