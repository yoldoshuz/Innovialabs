## The short answer: what roles exist in IT

IT is not only programmers. Every digital product is built by a team where each person owns a different area:

- A **developer** writes the product's code.
- A **QA engineer** checks that the product works correctly.
- A **designer** is responsible for how the product looks and how easy it is to use.
- A **Project / Product Manager** organizes the work and decides what to build and why.
- A **DevOps engineer** owns infrastructure, deployment and stability.
- A **data analyst** turns data into conclusions and decisions.

The right choice is the role whose **everyday tasks you find interesting**, not the one people talk about most.

## Comparing the roles

| Role | Daily work | Entry barrier | Key aptitudes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frontend developer** | Builds interfaces and connects them to the server | Medium | Attention to detail, visual thinking, logic |
| **Backend developer** | Writes APIs and business logic, works with databases | Medium or higher | Systems thinking, comfort with abstraction |
| **QA engineer** | Finds bugs, writes test cases, automates checks | Below medium for manual testing | Thoroughness, describing problems clearly |
| **UI/UX designer** | Researches users, designs screens, builds mockups | Medium | Empathy, sense of composition, defending decisions |
| **Project / Product Manager** | Plans, talks to the team and client, sets priorities | Usually after experience in another role | Communication, organization, handling uncertainty |
| **DevOps engineer** | Sets up servers, CI/CD, monitoring, containers | High, often after development or sysadmin work | Reliability mindset, love of automation |
| **Data analyst** | Writes SQL queries, builds reports and dashboards | Medium | Analytical mind, curiosity about numbers |

**Entry barrier** here means roughly how much you need to know for a first job, not how hard the profession is overall. Every role has a path to senior level.

## What about demand

Demand for specific roles shifts with the market, so do not rely on someone else's rankings. Check for yourself:

- Open job listings in your city and for remote work.
- Count **how many junior-level openings** each role has.
- Write down the requirements that repeat most often.

That gives you a current picture for your own situation.

## A simple self-test

Answer honestly which statements fit you:

1. **I like seeing the result of my work on screen right away** — look at frontend or design.
2. **I want to know how things work under the hood** — backend or DevOps.
3. **I notice mistakes and inconsistencies others miss** — QA.
4. **I enjoy organizing people and processes** — management, usually after some team experience.
5. **I like finding patterns in numbers** — data analytics.
6. **I think about whether things are convenient for people** — UX design.

If several fit, that is fine. Try each direction with a small practical task and compare which one felt engaging and which felt like a chore.

## How to test your choice quickly

- **Do a mini-project**: a simple web page, test cases for an app you use, a screen mockup, or a report on an open dataset.
- **Talk to practitioners**: ask what a normal working week looks like for them.
- **Look at the routine**, not the final result: routine takes up most of the time.

## Common mistakes when choosing

- **Choosing by salaries in the news**: without interest in the tasks, it is hard to reach the level where those salaries are paid.
- **Betting on the "easiest" profession**: an easy entry often means heavy competition among beginners.
- **Fear of choosing wrong**: skills transfer between roles, and you can change direction later.

## FAQ

### Can I get into IT without programming?

Yes. Design, manual testing, project management and analytics need less code or none at all. Still, a basic understanding of how software is built helps in any role.

### Can I switch roles once I already work in IT?

Yes, it happens often: QA engineers move into development, developers move into DevOps or management. Team experience makes that move easier.

### Which role should I pick if everything sounds interesting?

Start with the one where you can get a first practical result fastest, and try the others through mini-projects in parallel. After a few weeks your preferences usually become clearer.
