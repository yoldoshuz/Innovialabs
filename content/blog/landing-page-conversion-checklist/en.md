## The short answer

Low landing page conversion is rarely about button color. Usually visitors **don't understand the offer**, **don't find what the ad promised**, or **give up waiting for the page to load**. So work in this order:

0. Tracking works.
1. The offer is clear within seconds.
2. The page matches the ad.
3. The page loads fast.
4. The mobile layout is comfortable.
5. The CTA is visible and unambiguous.
6. The form is short and doesn't break.
7. There are reasons to trust you.

## 0. Make sure leads are counted

Before fixing anything, make sure you see the real picture.

- **How to verify:** submit a test lead from a desktop and a phone. It should reach your CRM or inbox and show up as a Metrica goal and a GA4 event.
- Compare weekly lead counts in analytics and the CRM. A big gap means you should look for a bug, not redesign.

## 1. The offer is clear immediately

The first screen should answer: **what this is**, **who it's for** and **why choose you**. Abstract slogans like "Solutions for your growth" don't work.

- **How to verify:** a five-second test. Show the first screen to someone outside the team for a few seconds and ask what the page offers. If the answer is vague, rewrite the headline.
- The headline states the result for the customer; the subheadline explains how you deliver it.

## 2. Message match with the ad

If someone clicked an ad for "Flower delivery within an hour in Tashkent", the page must say the same — not open a generic catalog.

- **How to verify:** list your ad headlines and compare them with the first screen. The key phrase, price or promotion from the ad should be repeated.
- Build separate page versions for different ad groups, or swap the headline based on the UTM tag.

## 3. Speed

A slow page loses visitors before they even see the offer, and the ad click has already been paid for.

- **How to verify:** PageSpeed Insights, the Core Web Vitals report in Search Console, and the browser's Network tab with throttling enabled.
- Typical causes: heavy images and video on the first screen, many third-party scripts (widgets, pixels, chats), unoptimized fonts.
- Compress images into modern formats, lazy-load what sits below the first screen and remove unused scripts.

## 4. Mobile layout

A large share of ad traffic comes from phones, so review the page on phones first.

- **How to verify:** open the landing page on a real budget smartphone, not only in an emulator. Check mobile traffic share and conversion by device in analytics.
- Text is readable without zooming, buttons are easy to tap, nothing overflows the screen, and pop-ups don't cover the content.
- The phone number is a `tel:` link, and the messenger opens in one tap.

## 5. CTA

- **One primary action** per page. Secondary actions are visually weaker.
- Button text describes the outcome: "Get a quote", not "Submit".
- The button is visible on the first screen and repeated after key sections.
- **How to verify:** click maps and scroll maps in Metrica. If few people scroll to the button, or they click non-clickable elements, change the structure.

## 6. Form

- Only the fields you need to contact the customer. A manager can clarify the rest on a call.
- A phone mask with country code, clear errors next to the field, and entered data that isn't wiped on error.
- After submission, a clear message about what happens next and when.
- **How to verify:** fill in the form yourself on a phone, with and without mistakes. Use Session Replay (Webvisor) to see which field people abandon.

## 7. Trust signals

- Real contacts, address and company details.
- Genuine reviews and case studies with specifics; client logos only with permission.
- Guarantees, payment and refund terms, and a privacy policy near the form.
- **How to verify:** ask a few customers what made them hesitate before contacting you, and review the questions managers hear most often. The page should answer them.

## How to use the checklist

Fix one block at a time and compare results over a comparable period. With enough traffic, check major changes with an A/B test rather than a before-and-after comparison, which is skewed by seasonality and ad settings.

## FAQ

### Where should I start if traffic is low?

With points 0–2: tracking, offer and ad match. You can check them without much traffic — tests with real people and a comparison with your ads are enough.

### What is a normal landing page conversion rate?

There is no single benchmark: it depends on the niche, price, traffic source and what counts as a conversion. It is more useful to compare the page with its own past results and to compare channels with each other.

### Should I start with a redesign?

Usually not. A redesign changes everything at once, so you can't tell what helped or hurt. Step-by-step fixes from the checklist give more predictable results.
