## The main mistakes at a glance

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Weak hero | Unclear what is offered | Outcome headline + one button |
| Too many CTAs | Visitors choose nothing | One primary call to action per page |
| Walls of text | Nobody reads the page | Short paragraphs, lists, subheadings |
| Poor mobile layout | Mobile visitors drop off | Design for the narrow screen first |
| Stock clichés | No trust, no difference | Real photos of the product and team |
| Slow loading | People leave before the hero appears | Compress media, cut scripts |
| No proof | Promises don't persuade | Testimonials, case studies, product demos |

Each is covered in more detail below.

## Weak hero

**What it looks like:** a headline like "We are a team of professionals" or the company name in big letters, an abstract background and a "Learn more" button.

**Why it's bad:** visitors decide in a few seconds whether to stay. If the first screen doesn't make clear what you do and for whom, they leave.

**How to fix it:**

- The headline states a concrete result for the customer: what they get.
- The subheadline says for whom and how.
- The button uses a verb that describes the next step: "Get a quote" instead of "Learn more."
- Test it: show the first screen to someone outside the project for five seconds and ask them to retell what is offered.

## Too many calls to action

**What it looks like:** "Buy," "Subscribe," "Download catalog," "Call us," "Chat" — all the same size and colour on one screen.

**Why it's bad:** when everything is highlighted, nothing is. A choice between equal options gets postponed.

**How to fix it:**

- Pick **one target action** and make it the primary button.
- Turn other actions into secondary buttons or text links.
- Repeat the primary button after key blocks, with the same label.

## Walls of text

**What it looks like:** ten-line paragraphs, small type, text stretched across the full screen width.

**Why it's bad:** people scan landing pages rather than read them. A solid block gets skipped entirely, along with your key arguments.

**How to fix it:**

- Two to four lines per paragraph, one idea each.
- Subheadings that carry the meaning of a block on their own.
- Lists instead of comma-separated runs, **bold** for key words.
- A comfortable line length is roughly 60–80 characters, so cap the text column width.

## Poor mobile layout

**What it looks like:** tiny text, buttons that are hard to tap, tables that scroll sideways, a pop-up that can't be closed.

**Why it's bad:** a large share of landing page traffic comes from phones, especially from social and messenger ads.

**How to fix it:**

- Design **mobile first**, then expand to desktop.
- Make tap targets at least 44–48 px.
- Turn tables into cards and dense blocks into accordions.
- Test on real phones, not only in a browser emulator.

## Stock clichés

**What it looks like:** handshakes, smiling people in headsets, light bulbs and puzzle pieces.

**Why it's bad:** these images appear on thousands of sites. They don't explain the product and they lower trust.

**How to fix it:**

- Show the **real product**: the interface, the item, the work process.
- Team and office photos, even carefully shot on a phone, are more honest than stock.
- No photos? Use diagrams, on-brand illustrations or screenshots.

## Slow loading and missing proof

Two mistakes that often go unnoticed.

- **Speed.** Background video and uncompressed images slow down the first screen. Use modern image formats (WebP, AVIF), lazy-load everything below the fold and check the page in PageSpeed Insights.
- **Proof.** Promises without testimonials, case studies or a demo don't persuade. Add what you genuinely have, and never invent reviews or numbers.

## How to find your own mistakes

1. Look at scroll and click analytics: where people leave and what they ignore.
2. Run a short usability test with three to five people from your target audience.
3. Fix one problem at a time and compare the results.

## FAQ

### Which mistake should I fix first?

Start with the hero. Every visitor sees it, while later blocks are seen only by those who stay. Then move to the mobile layout and the number of calls to action.

### Do I need a full redesign if my landing page underperforms?

Not necessarily. Often it's enough to rewrite the headline, remove extra buttons, shorten the text and fix the mobile layout. A redesign makes sense when the offer itself or the audience changes.

### How do I know a fix worked?

Compare your target metric before and after, such as the share of visitors who submit a request. An A/B test is the most reliable method if you have enough traffic.
