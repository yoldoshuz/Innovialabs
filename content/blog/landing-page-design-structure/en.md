## The core block sequence

A landing page is a conversation with a visitor, laid out across screens. The block order follows the questions people ask:

| Block | Visitor's question |
|---|---|
| **Hero** | What is this, and is it for me? |
| **Value** | What do I get? |
| **Proof** | Why should I trust you? |
| **Details** | How does it work, what is included, what does it cost? |
| **Objections** | What if it doesn't fit? |
| **Call to action (CTA)** | What do I do right now? |

This is not a rigid template. For a complex product the details block grows; for a simple one it shrinks. But if any question goes unanswered, the visitor leaves to find the answer somewhere else.

## Hero

The job is to explain what is offered and to whom within a few seconds.

- The **headline** describes the result for the customer, not the company name. Not "Innovative solutions" but something concrete like "Office lunch delivery by 1 pm."
- The **subheadline** clarifies how and for whom it works, in one or two sentences.
- **One primary button** with an action verb: "Get a quote," "Book a demo."
- The **visual** shows the product or the result: the interface, the item itself, the process. An abstract mood image explains almost nothing.

On mobile, the headline and button should fit on the first screen without scrolling.

## Value

Here you turn features into benefits. A good format is three or four cards, each with a short heading and one line of explanation.

- Use the customer's language: not "API integration" but "orders land in your accounting system automatically."
- One idea per card. Beyond six cards, attention scatters.
- Icons help scanning but do not replace text.

## Proof

Without trust, value is just a promise. Use what you really have:

- testimonials with name, role and photo — anonymous quotes are weaker;
- client or partner logos (only with their permission);
- case studies describing the task and what was done;
- certificates, awards, press mentions;
- product demonstrations: screenshots, video, a live demo.

Never invent numbers or reviews. If proof is thin, honestly show your process and team instead.

## Details

This block is for people who are already interested and want to understand more.

- **How it works** — three to five numbered steps.
- **What is included** — plans or packages. If you have prices, show them: a hidden price often raises more doubts than a high one.
- A **plan comparison** works well as a table with the recommended option highlighted.

## Objections

Collect doubts from sales calls and customer messages and answer them in advance.

- An **FAQ** with expandable answers saves space.
- Guarantees, refund terms and free trials reduce perceived risk.
- Place the answer to "what if it doesn't work for me?" near the button.

## Call to action

The final block restates the main offer and gives one clear button.

- Keep the form minimal: often a name and a phone number or messenger is enough.
- Under the button, say what happens next: "We'll call you during business hours to confirm the details."
- Repeat the same button after key blocks so people don't have to scroll back up.

## Page-wide design decisions

- **One target CTA** per page. Style secondary actions as links.
- **Visual hierarchy**: a large block heading, short text, plenty of white space.
- **Rhythm**: alternate block backgrounds so the page doesn't blur into one feed.
- **Mobile first**: check every block on a narrow screen.
- **Speed**: compress heavy images and video, or some visitors leave before the page loads.

## FAQ

### How long should a landing page be?

It depends on how complex and expensive the offer is. A simple, low-cost product can be explained in a few screens, while an expensive service needs more proof and detail. Length is justified when every block answers a real question.

### Can I change the order of blocks?

Yes. If your strongest argument is well-known clients, move proof right under the hero. Check the order with A/B tests or at least scroll analytics.

### Does a landing page need a menu?

Usually a short anchor navigation or no menu at all is enough. A menu that links to other pages pulls visitors away from the target action.
