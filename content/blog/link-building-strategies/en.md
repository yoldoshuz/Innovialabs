## Which links are safe

A simple test: **a link is safe if it would make sense even without search engines**. If real people click it, it sits on a relevant site and is surrounded by normal content, it is a good link.

Risky links are the ones created only for rankings: bought placements on low-quality sites, private blog networks, mass link blasts, and "you link to me, I link to you" schemes. Google and Yandex have long been able to detect these patterns and either ignore the links or demote the site.

## Safe link sources

### Digital PR

You give journalists and media something worth writing about: your own research, an expert comment, a useful tool, an unusual case. The link appears as a source citation.

- Watch for journalist requests in industry channels and services.
- Respond quickly with a concise, substantive comment.
- Publish data that only you have.

### Guest posts

These work when the article is genuinely useful for the host site's audience. Write for publications in your niche and place the link where it helps the reader — to an in-depth resource, not your homepage with a commercial anchor.

### Directories and listings

You need **quality, industry-specific** directories: maps, business directories, associations, profiles on platforms in your field. Hundreds of generic directories with no traffic will not help and may hurt.

### Partners and clients

Suppliers, integrators, events where you spoke — all of them can link to you from "Partners" or "Speakers" pages or from joint materials.

### Unlinked mentions

Find places where your brand is already mentioned without a link. Politely ask them to add one — it is one of the easiest ways to earn a natural link.

## Anchor text distribution

**Anchor text** is the clickable text of a link. A natural profile is varied:

| Anchor type | Example |
|---|---|
| Branded | "Example", "example.uz" |
| Naked URL | https://example.uz/blog |
| Generic | "here", "on their site", "read more" |
| Partial match | "guide to setting up a CRM" |
| Exact match | "CRM implementation" |

The rule of thumb: **branded and generic anchors should dominate**, exact-match commercial anchors should be rare. If most links use the same commercial phrase, it looks like manipulation.

## Link velocity

Links should appear **steadily and for an explainable reason**. A sudden spike is fine when there is an event behind it — coverage in a major outlet or a viral piece. It looks suspicious when a small site suddenly gains hundreds of links from unrelated domains for no reason.

A practical plan:

1. Audit your current backlinks and your competitors'.
2. Create 2–3 assets worth linking to: research, a calculator, an in-depth guide.
3. Each month work several channels: PR, guest posts, partners, mentions.
4. Track new links in Google Search Console and Yandex Webmaster.

## Common mistakes

- Buying "done-for-you" link packages without checking the sites.
- Using the same commercial anchor for every link.
- Links from off-topic sites just to increase the count.
- Chasing the number of domains instead of quality and traffic.
- Having no content that people would naturally link to.

## FAQ

### Is it ever okay to pay for links?

Google's guidelines explicitly prohibit buying links that pass ranking value. Paid placements are fine when the link carries `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"` — then it works as advertising rather than a ranking signal.

### How many links do I need to rank higher?

There is no universal number. It depends on competition in your niche, the quality of competitors' links and your content. Comparing your profile with the sites in the top results is more useful than aiming for a count.

### Do nofollow links help at all?

They do not pass value directly, but they bring traffic, make the profile look natural and may be treated by search engines as a hint.
