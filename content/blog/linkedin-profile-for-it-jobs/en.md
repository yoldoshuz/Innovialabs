## How recruiters find candidates

Recruiters search LinkedIn much like you search an online store: with keywords and filters. Typically that means a **role** ("Backend Developer"), **skills** ("Python", "Django"), **location** and availability. If those words are missing from your profile, you will not be found.

So the main goal is a profile that:

- **shows up** for the right searches;
- makes it clear within seconds **who you are and what you can do**;
- makes it **easy to contact** you.

## Photo, banner and profile URL

- **Photo**: your face close up, a neutral background, good lighting. Profiles without a photo inspire less trust.
- **Banner**: a calm image related to your work, or simply a solid color.
- **Custom URL**: replace the auto-generated link with a short one using your name — handy for your resume.
- **Location**: list your real city. Recruiters often filter candidates by country and city.

## Headline

Your headline appears next to your name in search results. It is the most important field on your profile.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Looking for a job" | "Frontend Developer · React, TypeScript, Next.js" |
| "Student" | "Junior Python Developer · Django, PostgreSQL, REST API" |
| "IT specialist" | "QA Engineer · Manual & Automation Testing, Playwright" |

Formula: **role + 2–4 key technologies + an optional specialization**. Use the role titles that appear in job postings.

## About section

This is where you describe yourself in your own words, but with keywords. A structure that works:

1. **Who you are**: role and main stack.
2. **What you do**: the problems you solve and projects you have worked on.
3. **Where you are strong**: 2–3 specific skills or areas.
4. **What you are looking for**: type of role, work format (office, remote, hybrid).
5. **How to reach you**: email or Telegram.

Write short paragraphs. Skip generic phrases like "motivated and responsible" — show that through facts in your experience instead.

## Experience

Describe experience as you would on a resume: results and technologies, not duties.

- Use a job title the market recognizes.
- In the description, 3–5 bullets: what you did, with what, and what changed.
- Add **skills** to each position — LinkedIn links them to your experience.
- No commercial experience? List internships, freelance, volunteer and study projects.

## Skills and Featured

**Skills** are used in search. Add the core technologies of your stack and pin the ones most relevant to your target role at the top. Do not turn the list into everything you have ever seen.

**Featured** is a block for links and posts. Put there:

- your portfolio site;
- 1–2 of your best GitHub projects;
- an article or post about your project, if you have one.

## Settings that affect visibility

- **Open to work**: specify target roles, work format and locations. You can make it visible to recruiters only if you do not want your current employer to see it.
- **Public profile**: check in the settings that your profile is visible to people outside your network.
- **Profile language**: if you target international companies, fill in the profile in English. LinkedIn lets you add a second language version.

## Activity

You do not need to post every day, but a minimal level of activity helps:

- leave thoughtful comments on posts from people in your field;
- share finished projects and what you learned;
- connect with colleagues, classmates, meetup attendees and recruiters at tech companies;
- add a short note explaining why when you send a connection request.

## FAQ

### Do I need LinkedIn if I only look for jobs in Uzbekistan?

It is useful. Many local companies and recruiters also search for candidates on LinkedIn, and your profile works as a public resume that is easy to link to.

### Which language should my profile be in?

If you are considering international companies, English. You can add a second version of the profile in Russian or Uzbek.

### Can my current employer see Open to work?

If you choose recruiters-only visibility, no frame appears on your photo. LinkedIn tries to hide the status from recruiters at your own company, but it cannot fully guarantee it.
