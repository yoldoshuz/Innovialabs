## The short answer

A loyalty program gives customers a reason to **come back to you specifically**. There are five main models:

| Model | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Points** | Purchases earn points that are swapped for discounts or gifts | Frequent purchases, a broad range |
| **Cashback** | Part of the amount goes back to a bonus balance | Simple purchases, mass market |
| **Tiers** | The more a customer spends, the higher their status and perks | Stores with a core of regulars |
| **Paid membership** | Customers pay a fee and get benefits | Stores with a strong perk: delivery, exclusives |
| **Referral** | A reward for bringing in a new customer | Products people like to recommend |

Models can be combined, for example cashback whose percentage grows with the tier.

## Points

Customers collect points for purchases, reviews or birthdays and redeem them at a set rate.

- **Economics.** The cost arises when points are redeemed, not when they're issued. Some points will never be spent, but planning around that is risky.
- **Strengths.** Flexibility: you can reward useful actions, not just purchases.
- **Pitfalls.** Confusing redemption rates, high redemption thresholds and points that expire quietly. Customers lose sight of the value and lose interest.

## Cashback

Essentially points expressed in money: "get 5% back in bonuses" is clear instantly.

- **Economics.** The cashback rate is effectively a discount deferred to the next purchase. Build it into your margin.
- **Strengths.** The simplest message of all.
- **Pitfalls.** Easy for competitors to copy, and a percentage race eats profit. Be upfront about limits on how much of an order can be paid with bonuses.

## Tiered programs

Statuses like "Basic", "Silver" and "Gold" based on spend over a period.

- **Economics.** Most of the cost goes to your best customers, who already bring in much of the revenue. The program is there to keep them.
- **Strengths.** Status, and the fear of losing it, is a strong motivator. Pairs well with non-monetary perks: early access to new arrivals, a personal manager.
- **Pitfalls.** Unreachable thresholds demotivate. Downgrading a tier without warning annoys people.

## Paid membership

Customers pay a fixed monthly or annual fee and get, say, free delivery and members-only prices.

- **Economics.** You get predictable subscription revenue, and a paying member tends to buy more often to get their money's worth.
- **Strengths.** The strongest attachment of any model.
- **Pitfalls.** If the benefit isn't felt, people cancel. Work out in advance what the perks cost you when members use them heavily.

## Referral programs

The customer gets a bonus for inviting a friend, and the friend gets a discount on their first order.

- **Economics.** You pay only for a result: a completed order.
- **Strengths.** New customers arrive through someone they trust.
- **Pitfalls.** Abuse: self-referrals and fake accounts. Credit the bonus after payment and once the return window has closed.

## How to choose

1. **Look at purchase frequency.** If people buy once every few years, points barely work. Referrals and great service do better.
2. **Do the margin math.** Every customer benefit is a cost. Decide first how much you can afford to give back.
3. **Start simple.** One clear mechanic beats three confusing ones.
4. **Connect your systems.** The program should work across every channel (website, app, physical store) through your CRM.
5. **Measure.** Compare purchase frequency and average order value of members against non-members.

## FAQ

### Which is better: a discount or cashback?

A discount works at the moment of purchase; cashback motivates a return visit. If the goal is repeat purchases, cashback is usually more useful. If you need to close the first sale, a direct discount is stronger.

### Should bonuses expire?

Expiry encourages people to spend bonuses and limits your accumulated liability. But warn customers before bonuses expire, or it feels like a trick.

### Do I need a separate system for a loyalty program?

For simple cashback on a website, the platform's module or your CRM is often enough. Once the program spans several channels or includes tiers and referrals, you need a single system with an API.
