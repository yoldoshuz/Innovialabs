## The short answer

Each of the four metrics answers a different question:

| Metric | Question | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** (Customer Acquisition Cost) | What does it cost to acquire one customer? | Acquisition costs / Number of new customers |
| **LTV** (Lifetime Value) | How much profit will a customer bring over time? | Average order value × Purchases per lifetime × Gross margin |
| **ROAS** (Return on Ad Spend) | How much revenue did ads generate? | Revenue from ads / Ad spend × 100% |
| **ROMI** (Return on Marketing Investment) | How much profit did marketing generate beyond its cost? | (Gross profit from marketing − Marketing costs) / Marketing costs × 100% |

The key difference: **ROAS counts revenue, ROMI counts profit**. A high ROAS does not mean you are making money.

## A worked example

The numbers are illustrative, to show the mechanics. An online store in one month:

- spent **$2,000** on ads;
- spent another **$1,000** on sales staff handling leads;
- got **50 new customers**;
- average first order value is **$80**;
- gross margin (gross profit as a share of revenue) is **40%**.

**CAC** = ($2,000 + $1,000) / 50 = **$60** per customer.

**ROAS** = (50 × $80) / $2,000 × 100% = $4,000 / $2,000 × 100% = **200%**.

Sounds good: every ad dollar returned two. Now let's count profit.

**ROMI on the first purchase** = ($4,000 × 40% − $2,000) / $2,000 × 100% = ($1,600 − $2,000) / $2,000 × 100% = **−20%**.

On the first purchase alone, ads lose money. Now account for repeat customers. Suppose the store's data shows an average customer makes **5 purchases** over their lifetime.

**LTV** = $80 × 5 × 40% = **$160** of gross profit per customer.

**ROMI based on LTV** = (50 × $160 − $2,000) / $2,000 × 100% = ($8,000 − $2,000) / $2,000 × 100% = **300%**.

**LTV / CAC** = $160 / $60 ≈ **2.7**.

The takeaway: ads pay off, but **not immediately**, only through repeat purchases. So the business needs working capital to wait for the return, and it needs retention work: if customers stop coming back, the whole model turns negative.

## A useful derivative: break-even ROAS

For the first purchase to at least cover ad spend:

**Minimum ROAS = 1 / Gross margin × 100%**

At a 40% margin, that is 250%. Anything below means a loss on the first sale. This simple check stops you from celebrating a "200% ROAS".

## How the metrics work together

- **CAC without LTV** says nothing: whether $60 per customer is a lot depends on what the customer brings in.
- **ROAS** is handy for day-to-day campaign management: comparing ads, placements and audiences.
- **ROMI** answers the business question: are we making money on marketing?
- **LTV / CAC** shows the health of the whole model. LTV must clearly exceed CAC, because rent, salaries and taxes are paid out of the difference.
- **CAC payback period** is how many months it takes a customer to repay their acquisition cost. The shorter it is, the less cash is locked up in growth.

## Common mistakes

- **Calculating LTV from revenue instead of profit.** $400 of revenue at a 40% margin is $160, not $400.
- **Leaving costs out of CAC**: salaries, tools, agency fees, first-order discounts.
- **Making LTV up.** Calculate it from real cohorts: take customers acquired in one month and see how much they bought in the following months.
- **Mixing channels.** A blended CAC can hide that one channel is profitable while another burns budget.
- **Trusting only last-click attribution** when calculating ROAS by channel.

## FAQ

### How is ROMI different from ROI?

ROI is a general return metric for any investment: equipment, a project, a business. ROMI applies the same principle only to marketing spend. The formula is similar; the difference is which costs and which profit you include.

### What if I do not have enough data for LTV yet?

Calculate LTV over a short horizon, such as the customer's first 3 or 6 months, and compare it with CAC. This is a conservative estimate: if you already break even on it, the model is solid. Extend the horizon as data accumulates.

### Can these metrics be used in B2B with long sales cycles?

Yes, with adjustments: include sales costs in CAC, link ad leads to deals in your CRM and calculate ROMI on closed deals with a delay equal to your sales cycle.
