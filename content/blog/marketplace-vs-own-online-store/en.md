## The short answer

- A **marketplace** (Uzum Market, Wildberries, Ozon, Amazon) is like renting space in a huge mall: shoppers are already there, but the platform sets the rules, fees and storefront.
- **Your own online store** is your own shop on your own street: every decision and every customer is yours, but you have to bring people in.

It is not an either-or choice. For most brands the sensible strategy is marketplaces for reach and your own store for margin, loyalty and customer data.

## Comparison by key criteria

| Criterion | Marketplace | Own store |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic | From day one | Must be acquired: ads, SEO, social |
| Fees | Per sale plus logistics and paid services | Payment processing, hosting, maintenance |
| Customer data | Mostly held by the platform | Fully yours |
| Brand and storefront | Template listing next to competitors | Any design and presentation |
| Pricing | Constant side-by-side comparison | Less direct pressure |
| Rules | Set by the platform | Set by you |
| Launch | Fast | Longer: development and setup |

## Marketplace strengths

- **A ready audience** that came to buy.
- **Trust**: shoppers know the platform, its payments and returns.
- **Logistics and pickup points**: many platforms handle storage and delivery.
- **Fast demand testing** for a new product without investing in a website.

## Marketplace weaknesses

- **Commissions and fees.** Besides a percentage per sale there can be charges for logistics, storage, returns and in-platform promotion. Calculate margin per product after all deductions.
- **Price pressure.** Shoppers see dozens of similar offers side by side, and competition often comes down to price.
- **The customer belongs to the platform.** You usually cannot freely contact buyers again, which makes loyalty hard to build.
- **Dependency.** A change in fees, ranking rules or a blocked listing can crash sales overnight, and you have almost no control over it.

## Own store strengths and weaknesses

**Pros:**

- **Brand control**: design, copy, packaging, service.
- **Customer data**: purchase history, contacts, segmentation, repeat sales via email, messengers and loyalty programmes.
- **Your own pricing** and promotions without competitors on the same page.
- **Flexibility**: any payment methods (for example Payme, Click, Stripe), delivery options and integrations with CRM and 1C.

**Cons:**

- **You pay for traffic**: ads, SEO, content.
- **Launch and maintenance costs**: development or a platform subscription, hosting, security, updates.
- **Trust must be earned**: shoppers check a new store more carefully.

## Where the costs come from

No specific numbers here — they depend on your niche and platform. The structure looks like this:

- **Marketplace**: sales commission, logistics and storage, returns handling, in-platform ads, content for listings.
- **Own store**: development or platform subscription, hosting, payment processing, integrations, traffic acquisition, support.

The classic mistake is comparing a marketplace commission to zero. Acquiring a customer for your own store costs money too.

## How to combine both channels sensibly

1. **One source of stock.** Connect marketplaces and the website to a single accounting or inventory system (such as 1C) so you never sell the same item twice.
2. **Different roles per channel.** Marketplaces for first contact and bestsellers, your site for the full range, bundles and exclusives.
3. **Work on repeat purchases.** Package inserts, brand and service help move buyers to your own channels — within the platform's rules.
4. **Separate analytics.** Track margin and acquisition cost per channel.
5. **Diversify.** Do not keep all revenue on one platform.

## FAQ

### Where should a small business start?

Often with a marketplace: it lets you test demand without paying for a website and traffic. Once you have repeat customers and some recognition, your own store helps raise margin and retention.

### Do I need my own store if marketplace sales are already good?

Ideally yes, at least as insurance. Depending on a single platform is a serious risk: rules can change, and without your own channel you have nowhere to move customers.

### How do I avoid stock confusion across channels?

Use one system as the source of stock and sync it automatically with every platform and your site. Manual updates across several channels almost always lead to errors.
