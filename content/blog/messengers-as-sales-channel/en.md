## The short answer

Messengers became a sales channel because **they are easier for the customer**: no form to fill in, no waiting for a call, no hunting for an answer on a website. People ask a question in an app they already use and get a reply in the same thread. This approach is called **conversational commerce**: buying through a dialogue.

It works when every participant has a role: the **channel** attracts and warms up, the **bot** answers instantly and collects details, the **manager** closes the deal.

## Why customers prefer chatting

- **Asynchronous.** They can write any time and reply when convenient.
- **History at hand.** Order details, address and agreements stay in the chat.
- **Fewer barriers.** No sign-up, no searching for the right page.
- **Personal touch.** A chat feels like a conversation, not a questionnaire.

For a business this has a flip side: customers expect a **fast reply**. A message left unanswered for too long often means a lost sale.

## Where messengers fit in the customer journey

| Stage | What happens | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Someone discovers you | Channel, ads, link from the website |
| Interest | Asks questions, compares | Bot with answers, catalog, Mini App |
| Decision | Clarifies details, negotiates | Manager in the chat |
| Purchase | Places the order and pays | Bot or manager, payment link |
| After purchase | Status, delivery, questions | Bot notifications, support |
| Repeat purchase | New offers | Channel, opt-in broadcasts |

A messenger does not replace your website or ads. It usually becomes **the place where a conversation turns into a deal**: the customer arrives from search or an ad and asks questions in the chat.

## Roles of the bot, the manager and the channel

### Channel

A public Telegram channel or a WhatsApp channel is your **company media**: new products, useful content, promotions, reviews. Its job is to keep interest alive and bring people into a one-to-one conversation.

### Bot

The bot covers everything that does not need a person:

- answers common questions instantly, at any hour;
- shows the catalog and prices;
- **collects lead details**: what is needed, budget, contacts;
- sends order status notifications;
- hands the conversation to a manager when the question is complex.

### Manager

A person is needed where trust and flexibility matter: unusual requests, large orders, objections, complaints. Ideally the manager receives the conversation **with the context already collected** by the bot and does not ask the customer the same questions again.

## How to set up the process

1. **Pick a primary messenger**, the one your customers use.
2. **Place entry points**: buttons on the website, links in profiles, a QR code at physical locations.
3. **Script the bot** for the first minutes: greeting, key questions, handoff to a human.
4. **Connect a CRM** so every conversation becomes a deal with a history and an owner.
5. **Set a response standard**: who answers during which hours and what customers see outside them.
6. **Watch the funnel**: how many conversations started, how many became orders, where people go silent.

## Common mistakes

- **A bot with no way to reach a human.** The customer hits a menu wall and leaves.
- **Chats on a manager's personal phone.** When the employee leaves, the customer history leaves too.
- **Aggressive broadcasts.** Pushiness in a messenger annoys people more than in email and leads to blocks.
- **No CRM link.** Leads get lost and nobody works on repeat sales.

## FAQ

### Are messengers only for low-priced products?

No. For simple purchases they can take a customer all the way to payment, and for complex or expensive ones they are a convenient consultation channel where a manager leads the deal.

### Can a bot fully replace sales managers?

Partly, for standard orders. Unusual questions, objections and complaints still need a person, so plan the handoff from the start.

### How do I know the channel pays off?

Track the path from first message to payment in your CRM: how many conversations became orders and how much revenue they brought. Without that link, the channel cannot be evaluated.
