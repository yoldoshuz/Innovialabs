## The short answer: what a sensible test looks like

The creative is now the main lever in Meta Ads: with broad targeting, it decides who sees your ad. So test it systematically:

1. A separate **test campaign** or ad set with a capped budget.
2. **3-5 creatives** with different ideas in one ad set.
3. Quick filtering on **early metrics** (hook rate, CTR), with the final decision based on **CPA** or ROAS.
4. Move winners to the main campaign and switch off the losers.

## Test structure

**What to test first.** Start with **concepts**, not button colors: different customer pain points, different formats (talking-head video, product demo, testimonial, static image), different opening frames. Small iterations only make sense for a creative that has already proven itself.

**How to set up the campaign.**

- One optimization goal — the same event as in your main campaign (for example, `Lead` or `Purchase`).
- One audience for all creatives under test, otherwise you are comparing audiences, not ads.
- Leave the test alone while it collects data: budget and ad edits restart the learning phase.
- For a strict comparison, use the built-in A/B test tool in Ads Manager, which splits the audience so groups do not overlap.

**How many creatives.** Put 15 ads into one ad set with a modest budget and the algorithm will give almost all delivery to two or three of them, while the rest get no data. Fewer creatives with enough delivery each is better.

## Which metrics to judge

| Metric | What it shows | How to calculate |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook rate** | Whether the first seconds grab attention | 3-second video plays / impressions |
| **Hold rate** | Whether people keep watching | ThruPlays / impressions or / 3-second plays |
| **CTR (link)** | Whether people want to learn more | Link clicks / impressions |
| **CPM** | Cost of reaching this audience | Spend / impressions x 1000 |
| **CPA** | Cost of the goal action | Spend / conversions |

Early metrics are a filter, not a verdict. A creative with a high CTR can attract the wrong people, so the final call is made on **CPA** and lead quality in your CRM.

## How long to wait

- Do not judge results on day one: delivery is still unstable.
- Meta exits the learning phase after roughly 50 optimization events per ad set within a week. While learning is in progress, CPA fluctuates.
- A practical rule: before killing a creative on CPA, let it spend an amount comparable to two or three target CPAs. If there are no conversions by then, switch it off.
- A creative with very low hook rate and CTR can be paused earlier, without waiting for conversions.

## How to spot creative fatigue

Fatigue is when the audience is tired of an ad. Signs:

- **frequency** grows while CTR drops week over week;
- CPM rises for the same audience;
- CPA creeps up with no changes to settings;
- ad diagnostics show a creative fatigue warning.

What to do: keep the next creatives ready in advance, iterate on the winner (new opening frame, different copy, a different person on screen) and regularly go back to searching for new concepts.

## Common mistakes

- Testing everything at once: audiences, creatives and copy in a single campaign.
- Drawing conclusions from 300 impressions or a single conversion.
- Killing a creative because of expensive clicks while it delivers cheap leads.
- Moving a winner into the main campaign and immediately rewriting its copy.

## FAQ

### How many creatives should one ad set have?

Usually 3-5. More only makes sense if the budget lets each ad get a meaningful number of impressions.

### What is a good hook rate or CTR?

There is no universal benchmark: it depends on the niche, format and audience. Compare creatives against each other and against your own past results in the same account.

### Should I test static images if I already have video?

Yes. In some niches a simple image with a strong offer performs as well as video and is faster to produce. Test both formats at least at the start.
