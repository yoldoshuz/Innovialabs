## How to choose in one paragraph

Ask two questions: **how often** people use the app and **what** they would pay for. Daily use but few payers — ads fit. Ongoing value — a subscription. One-off or cumulative value — in-app purchases. Freemium is a way to lead people to a paid version through a free one.

## Ads

The app is free; revenue comes from impressions and clicks via ad networks.

- **Pros:** no entry barrier, the whole audience is monetized.
- **Cons:** needs large traffic, ads annoy users and can reduce retention.
- **Fits:** casual games, utilities, news, apps with frequent short sessions.

Formats differ in how intrusive they are: **banners** are barely noticeable, **interstitials** interrupt the flow, **rewarded** ads (a video for a bonus) are received better because the user chooses to watch.

## Subscriptions

Users pay regularly — weekly, monthly or yearly — for access to features or content.

- **Pros:** predictable recurring revenue, an incentive to keep improving the product.
- **Cons:** requires ongoing value or people cancel; tough competition for a place in the budget.
- **Fits:** fitness, learning, music and video, productivity, cloud services.

A subscription works only when users understand what they pay for every time. A free trial helps, but without clear value it converts poorly.

## Freemium

The basic version is free; advanced features are paid, either once or by subscription.

- **Pros:** a wide funnel, users see the value before paying.
- **Cons:** the line is hard to draw — give away too much and nobody pays, too little and people leave.
- **Fits:** notes, editors, work tools, apps with clear "pro" features.

A good line: the core task is free; volume, convenience or professional features are paid.

## In-app purchases (IAP)

Users buy individual items inside the app. They can be **consumable** (in-game currency, extra attempts) or **non-consumable** (unlocking a level, removing ads forever).

- **Pros:** only those who need it pay; flexible pricing.
- **Cons:** uneven revenue, easy to slide into aggressive mechanics that backfire.
- **Fits:** games, apps selling content per item, one-time upgrades.

## Paid download

The app costs money upfront at install.

- **Pros:** a simple model, no ads or pushy upsells.
- **Cons:** the highest entry barrier, no recurring revenue to fund maintenance.
- **Fits:** niche professional tools, apps from strong brands.

## Comparison

| Model | Entry barrier | Revenue regularity | Retention risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ads | None | Depends on traffic | Medium to high |
| Subscription | Medium | High | Low if value is clear |
| Freemium | None | Depends on conversion | Low with a fair line |
| IAP | None | Uneven | Medium |
| Paid download | High | Low | Low |

There is also the model where the app is a sales channel: delivery, ride-hailing, e-commerce. The core business earns the money, and payments for physical goods and services go through regular payment providers rather than store purchases.

## Combining models without hurting retention

- **Paid removes friction.** The classic: a free version with ads, and a subscription or one-time purchase turns them off.
- **Rewarded instead of forced.** Let users choose to watch an ad for a bonus.
- **Do not show a paywall on the first screen** before people see the value. Let them get their task done first.
- **Do not break the free experience** for conversion: annoyed users leave and post bad reviews, which hurts your store rankings.
- **Track retention alongside revenue.** Growing revenue with falling retention means monetization is eating your audience.

## FAQ

### Which model should a new app start with?

Most often freemium or a free version with a clear paid option: you build an audience while testing what people are willing to pay for.

### Can I change the monetization model after launch?

Yes, but carefully. Moving free features behind a paywall causes backlash, so existing users are often kept on their old terms.

### Do ads work for small apps?

With a small audience, ad revenue is usually modest. Ads work better as an add-on to another model or with large, active traffic.
