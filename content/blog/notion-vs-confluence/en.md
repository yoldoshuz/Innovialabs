## The short answer

- **Confluence** suits companies already working in **Jira** and the Atlassian ecosystem, with many teams, strict permissions and formal documentation.
- **Notion** suits small and mid-sized teams that want a **flexible** space: wiki, tasks, databases and notes in one place with minimal setup.

Both handle a knowledge base well. The details decide: integrations, permissions and who will maintain the system.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Criterion | Notion | Confluence |
|---|---|---|
| **Editor** | Block-based, very flexible, drag anything | Familiar page editor with macros and templates |
| **Structure** | Nested pages, teamspaces, databases | Spaces with a page tree |
| **Databases** | A core strength: properties, views, relations, formulas | Available, but not the heart of the product |
| **Jira integration** | Link previews and sync, generally lighter | Deep and native |
| **Permissions** | Teamspaces and pages, granular roles on higher tiers | Global, space-level and page restrictions |
| **Scale** | Comfortable for small and mid-sized teams | Built for large organizations too |
| **Deployment** | Cloud only | Cloud, plus Data Center on your own servers |
| **Learning curve** | Low, but structure needs discipline | Higher, but structure comes built in |

## Editing

In **Notion** everything is a block: a paragraph becomes a checklist, gets dragged elsewhere, a database drops right into the text. Great for living working documents.

The **Confluence** editor is modern too, but pages feel closer to classic documents. **Macros** add tables of contents, embedded Jira issues, status labels and panels. That works well for technical and formal documentation.

## Structure and search

**Confluence** organizes everything into **spaces**: one per team or product, each with a page tree. It enforces discipline: you know where things live.

**Notion** structure is free-form. That helps at the start and hurts as you grow: without an owner and rules, duplicates and orphaned pages appear. On the other hand, databases let you filter articles by section, owner and review date.

## Jira integration

If engineering runs on Jira, **Confluence** has a real edge: Jira issues and filters embed in pages with live statuses, you can create issues from requirement text, and linked pages show up on the issue itself.

**Notion** can preview Jira links and sync data, but it does not offer the same ecosystem-wide connection.

## Permissions and security

- **Confluence**: site, space and page-level permissions, user groups and richer administration on higher tiers. **Data Center** fits when data must stay on your own servers.
- **Notion**: access through teamspaces and pages, guest access, SSO and advanced admin controls on higher tiers. Cloud only.

In regulated industries, check data residency requirements before you choose.

## Pricing

Both tools have a free plan and paid per-user tiers. Prices change, so compare on factors:

- how many members and guests you need;
- whether you need SSO, audit logs and advanced permissions;
- whether you already pay for Jira (Confluence often makes sense in the same contract);
- how much administration the tool needs — that is a cost too.

## Migration

- **Confluence to Notion**: Notion includes a Confluence importer. Text comes across, but macros, complex tables and permissions need manual checking and setup.
- **Notion to Confluence**: usually via HTML or Markdown export followed by manual cleanup. Notion databases transfer the worst.

The effort depends on the number of pages, macros and attachments. A migration is a good chance to delete outdated content instead of moving everything.

## What to choose by team type

- **Product engineering on Jira** → Confluence.
- **Large company with strict permissions and audits** → Confluence.
- **Data must stay on your own servers** → Confluence Data Center or another self-hosted tool.
- **Startup or small team** → Notion.
- **Agency, marketing or operations team** → Notion: databases cover content plans, clients and tasks.
- **One space for wiki and tasks without Jira** → Notion.

## FAQ

### Can we use Notion and Confluence at the same time?

Yes, but split the roles clearly: for example, technical docs in Confluence next to Jira, and marketing and operations in Notion. Without a clear split, people will not know where to look.

### Which is easier for non-technical staff?

Usually Notion: the interface is more intuitive and pages are easier to format. Confluence is also easy to pick up when spaces and templates are set up in advance.

### Is it worth switching if our current wiki is outdated?

Switching alone does not fix stale knowledge. Without page owners and regular reviews, the new wiki will go stale just the same. Fix the process first and change the tool only if the current one genuinely gets in the way.
