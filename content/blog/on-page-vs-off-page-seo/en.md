## The short answer

**On-page SEO** is work on things you control: page content, headings, meta tags, structure, internal links, speed and the technical health of the site.

**Off-page SEO** is the signals that appear outside your site: links from other websites, brand mentions, reviews, listings on maps and directories.

Put simply: on-page answers "what is this page about and is it good", off-page answers "do others trust this site".

## What on-page SEO includes

- **Content that matches the query.** The page answers a specific user question fully and to the point.
- **Title and description.** Unique for every page, with the main topic near the start.
- **H1–H3 headings.** One H1, a logical hierarchy, no keyword stuffing.
- **URLs.** Short and readable: `/services/web-development`, not `/page?id=123`.
- **Internal links.** Connect related pages and help search engines understand the structure.
- **Images.** Compressed files and meaningful `alt` text.
- **Technical basics.** Load speed, mobile version, HTTPS, correct `robots.txt` and sitemap, no duplicates.
- **Structured data.** Helps search engines understand the type of content.

Example: an "Online store development" service page gets a clear H1, a section with project stages, answers to common questions and links to related case studies and articles.

## What off-page SEO includes

- **Backlinks** from relevant, reputable sites. Quality and relevance matter, not volume.
- **Brand mentions** in media, blogs and industry platforms, even without a link.
- **Reviews** on maps, directories and industry services.
- **Local profiles:** Google Business Profile, Yandex Business and similar listings with consistent name, address and phone.
- **PR and social signals:** talks, interviews, guest articles.

Example: a company publishes an expert article in an industry outlet with a link to its site and collects customer reviews on maps.

## Side-by-side

| | On-page | Off-page |
|---|---|---|
| Where it happens | Your site | Other websites |
| Control | Full | Partial |
| What it gives | Relevance and usability | Trust and authority |
| Speed of effect | Usually noticeable sooner | Builds up gradually |
| Main risk | Weak content, technical errors | Paid and spammy links |

## How they work together

A search engine first has to **understand and index** a page — that is on-page work. Then, among many relevant pages, it prefers the ones it **trusts** more — that is where off-page helps.

A sensible order:

1. Fix the technical basics and site structure.
2. Build strong pages for your key queries.
3. Create things worth linking to: research, guides, useful tools.
4. Grow external signals: PR, partnerships, reviews, local profiles.

Links to a weak page barely help. A great page with no external signals can still lose in a competitive niche.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying links first** while the site has duplicates, slow pages and thin content.
- **Mass links from unrelated sites.** Search engines detect them and may demote the site.
- **Over-optimised copy:** keywords in every sentence hurt both readability and rankings.
- **Ignoring local profiles** when the business serves customers in a specific city.

## FAQ

### What matters more for a new site?

Start with on-page work: without it pages index poorly and don't answer queries. Add off-page efforts once you have something worth showing.

### Can you rank without backlinks?

For low-competition niches and narrow queries, often yes. In competitive topics, external signals usually become a noticeable factor.

### Is technical SEO part of on-page?

Yes, it is usually treated as part of on-page or as its own branch within it: speed, indexing, mobile version and error-free pages.
