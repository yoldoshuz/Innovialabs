## The short answer

There is no single price for an online store: two sites that look alike can differ in effort many times over. The cost comes from five groups of factors:

1. **Catalog** — how many products and how complex they are.
2. **Design** — a template or a custom interface.
3. **Integrations** — accounting system, payments, delivery, CRM, marketplaces.
4. **Custom features** — anything beyond a standard store.
5. **Support and ownership** — what you pay after launch.

To get a real estimate, describe these points instead of asking "how much is a store".

## Catalog

The number of products matters less than their **structure**:

- Simple products with one price are easier than products with **variants** (size, color), each with its own SKU and stock level.
- Deep filtering on many attributes requires a well-designed data model and search.
- Separate retail and wholesale prices, personal discounts and multi-currency pricing all add logic.
- Catalog import: clean data means a script; messy data means manual cleanup.

## Design

| Approach | What you get | What drives cost |
|---|---|---|
| Ready-made template | Fast start, standard look | Brand adaptation, template edits |
| Design on a UI kit | Your own style on proven components | Number of unique screens |
| Fully custom design | An interface built around your buying flow | Research, prototypes, animation, responsive layouts |

Every unique screen has to be not only designed but also built for phones, tablets and desktop.

## Integrations

This is usually the most underestimated part of the budget. Each integration means data exchange, error handling and testing.

- **1C or another accounting system**: exporting products, prices and stock, sending orders back. Complexity depends on the 1C configuration and the quality of the data in it.
- **Payment providers**: Payme, Click, Stripe. Each needs server-side handling of callbacks, cancellations and refunds.
- **Delivery**: shipping calculation, shipment creation, status tracking.
- **CRM**: passing orders and customers to AmoCRM, Bitrix24 or a custom system.
- **Marketplaces**: syncing catalog and stock with Uzum, Wildberries, Ozon.

The more systems exchange data, the more important it is to decide which one is the **source of truth** for each data type.

## Custom features

Anything beyond "catalog, cart, payment" is estimated separately:

- loyalty program, bonuses, promo codes with complex rules;
- product configurator or calculator;
- B2B customer account with contract pricing;
- multiple languages and storefronts per country;
- a mobile app or Telegram bot on top of the same store.

## Support and cost of ownership

Launch is not the last payment. Plan for:

- hosting, domain, SSL, backups;
- platform and paid plugin licenses, if any;
- payment provider and marketplace fees;
- security updates and bug fixes;
- improvements that inevitably come up after the first months of sales.

## How to scope a budget

1. **Describe scenarios**, not pages: how a customer finds a product, checks out, pays and receives it.
2. **Split features** into launch must-haves and later items. This is the most effective way to cut the initial budget.
3. **List integrations**, noting which systems already exist and who owns them.
4. **Ask for an itemized estimate** by block, not one total. That shows what you pay for and what can be postponed.
5. **Calculate ownership** for a year ahead, not just development.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing proposals by total price without checking the scope.
- Forgetting content preparation: photos, descriptions, translation.
- Demanding every feature in the first release.
- Leaving no budget for post-launch support.

## FAQ

### Why do estimates from different vendors differ so much?

Usually they price different scopes: one includes a 1C integration and custom design, another a template and manual import. Compare proposals against the same feature list.

### What can I postpone to lower the starting budget?

Most often the loyalty program, an advanced customer account, multiple languages and marketplace integrations. Payments, basic delivery and accurate stock are not worth postponing.

### Can I get an exact price before work starts?

It becomes exact after the requirements are described in detail. Without that, only a range is possible, and the more detailed the specification, the narrower the range.
