## The short answer

New online stores usually lose their first buyers over basics, not button colours: **incomplete product pages**, **payments nobody tested with real money**, **undefined delivery rules** and **a checkout that is painful on a phone**. Each of these is cheap to fix before launch and expensive afterwards, once complaints and refunds start arriving.

## Mistake 1. Poor product data

A product page is a salesperson answering questions on your behalf. When the answer is missing, the buyer leaves or opens a chat.

What usually goes wrong:

- no dimensions, weight, materials or package contents;
- photos in different formats and backgrounds, with no close-ups;
- stock is not synced with the warehouse, so people order items you do not have;
- no consistent SKUs, which breaks integration with your accounting system.

**How to prevent it:** define a product template per category with required fields, keep a single SKU reference list and test the stock export from your accounting system before you open sales.

## Mistake 2. Payments tested only in sandbox

A payment provider's test mode does not reproduce every real scenario. Typical problems after launch:

- the order is paid but the admin panel still shows "unpaid" because the provider's notification is not processed;
- the customer is charged twice after clicking the button again;
- there is no clear message when a payment fails;
- refunds are not set up.

**How to prevent it:** before launch, make a few real low-value purchases with every payment method, then refund them. Check that the order status updates automatically and that the customer receives a confirmation.

## Mistake 3. No delivery logic

"Delivery by arrangement" in the cart loses orders. Buyers want to see **cost and timing** before they pay.

What to decide upfront:

| Question | What to define |
| --- | --- |
| Zones | City, suburbs, regions with separate rates |
| Cost | Flat, by weight, free above a threshold |
| Timing | Days needed for picking and delivery |
| Methods | Courier, pickup point, delivery service |
| Payment | Prepaid or on delivery, per zone |

**How to prevent it:** write the rules as a table, agree them with your couriers or delivery partner, and only then enter them into the store settings.

## Mistake 4. Weak mobile UX

A large share of buyers arrive on phones, often from social networks and messengers. The most frequent problems:

- the "Buy" button sits several screens down;
- registration is required before ordering;
- the phone field has no input mask or numeric keyboard;
- heavy images make pages slow on mobile data.

**How to prevent it:** walk the whole path from ad to payment on an ordinary budget smartphone, not just your work laptop. Allow guest checkout and trim the form to the essentials.

## Mistake 5. Launching without analytics and processes

If tracking and goals are not set up on day one, you will not know where buyers drop off. If nobody owns order handling, the first requests will wait for hours.

**How to prevent it:**

- set up analytics with goals for add to cart, checkout start and payment;
- decide who handles orders and messages, and how fast;
- prepare reply templates for common questions;
- publish your return, delivery and privacy policies.

## Pre-launch checklist

1. Every product page follows the template and stock is synced.
2. Each payment method is verified with a real purchase and refund.
3. Delivery cost and timing are visible before payment.
4. An order can be placed from a phone without registering.
5. Analytics and goals work, order notifications arrive.
6. Someone owns order handling, and return rules are published.

## FAQ

### Can I launch with a small catalog?

Yes, and it is often the smarter choice. Fifty products with complete pages and accurate stock beat two thousand items without descriptions. You can grow the catalog step by step.

### Do I need every payment method from day one?

No. Start with the methods your audience actually uses and test them thoroughly. Add new ones once the core methods run without issues.

### How much time should I set aside for pre-launch testing?

It depends on the number of payment methods, integrations and delivery zones. Plan time for a full run through every scenario on different devices and for fixing what you find, not just for the test itself.
