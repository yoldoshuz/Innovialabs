## The short answer

**Outsourcing** fits when you need to launch quickly, have no experience hiring developers, or the workload is uneven. **An in-house team** makes sense when software is a key business asset that is developed continuously for years.

Often the best option is not "either/or" but **a mix**: a contractor launches the product while you hire people who gradually take over the expertise.

## Comparison on the key criteria

| Criterion | Outsourcing | In-house team |
|---|---|---|
| Speed to start | Fast: the team already exists | Slow: search, hiring, onboarding |
| Cost | You pay for results or hours, no fixed overhead | Salaries, taxes, equipment, office, training — ongoing |
| Control | Through the contract, tasks and demos | Direct, daily |
| Hiring | Not needed | Your job, especially hard without a technical leader |
| Product knowledge | Risks staying with the contractor | Accumulates inside the company |
| Flexibility | Easy to scale up or down | Downsizing is hard and painful |
| Business immersion | Limited | Deep |

## When to choose outsourcing

- You are launching an **MVP** and want to validate the idea before building a staff.
- You need **rare expertise** temporarily: AI, payment integrations, DevOps, design.
- The project is **one-off**: a website, an internal tool, an integration.
- The company has **no technical leader** who could evaluate and hire developers.

## When you need your own team

- The product is your business: SaaS, a marketplace, fintech.
- Development is **continuous**, not project-based.
- You need fast reaction to change and deep understanding of processes.
- You are ready to invest in hiring, management and retention.

## Choosing by company stage

| Stage | What usually works |
|---|---|
| Idea, testing a hypothesis | Outsourcing or a freelancer for the MVP |
| First customers, product growing | Outsourcing plus hiring a technical lead in-house |
| Steady growth | In-house core team, contractors for peaks and rare skills |
| Mature company | In-house for key systems, outsourcing for support and separate areas |

## Hidden costs people forget

**With an in-house team:**

- Time spent sourcing and interviewing, plus hiring mistakes.
- Onboarding before new people are fully productive.
- Management: someone has to assign tasks and accept the work.
- Idle time between projects — salaries are paid anyway.

**With outsourcing:**

- Time spent writing detailed specs and reviewing deliverables.
- Communication overhead if the contractor is in another time zone.
- Lock-in risk: without documentation and code access, switching contractors is hard.

## How to reduce outsourcing risks

1. **Code and access belong to you.** Repository, servers, domains and service accounts are registered to the company.
2. **Documentation is mandatory**: architecture, how to run the project, key decisions.
3. **Regular demos** — you see working results, not just reports.
4. **Milestone payments** with clear acceptance criteria.
5. **A handover plan** — agree in advance how knowledge will move to your team.

## Common mistakes

- Hiring a junior as the first developer with no mentor — they cannot build the architecture alone.
- Choosing a contractor on lowest price alone.
- Outsourcing the entire product with nobody in the company who understands it.
- Building a large team before demand is confirmed.

## FAQ

### Which is cheaper, outsourcing or an in-house team?

It depends on volume and duration. For short and one-off work, outsourcing is usually more economical; for long continuous development, your own team may cost less. Count the full cost, including hiring, management and idle time.

### Can you start with outsourcing and move in-house later?

Yes, it is a common path. The key is owning the code and access from day one and requiring documentation so the handover loses nothing.

### What is outstaffing and how is it different?

With outstaffing, a contractor provides developers who work inside your team under your management. You run the tasks yourself, while the contractor handles hiring and employment paperwork.
