## The key difference in one sentence

**Outsourcing** means handing a task to an external company and receiving a finished result: a website, an app, an integration. **Outstaffing** means an external company provides specialists who work inside your team under your processes, while legally remaining the contractor's employees.

Put simply: with outsourcing you buy a **product**; with outstaffing you buy **people's time**.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Outsourcing | Outstaffing |
|---|---|---|
| Who manages the team | Contractor | You |
| Who assigns tasks to developers | Contractor's manager | Your lead |
| Who owns deadlines and quality | Contractor | Mostly you |
| Processes and tools | Contractor's | Yours |
| Pricing model | Fixed price or time and materials | Rate per specialist per period |
| What you must provide | Clear requirements and acceptance | A tech lead and management |

## Who carries the risk

In **outsourcing**, most delivery risk sits with the contractor: they estimated the work, assembled the team and are obliged to deliver under the contract. Your part is a clear specification, quick answers and accepting milestones.

In **outstaffing**, the contractor is responsible for a qualified specialist showing up and for replacing them if needed. But if the project drifts, the architecture is a poor choice or deadlines slip, that is on your lead.

## How pricing works

- **Outsourcing, fixed price.** Works when the scope is well defined. The contractor builds the risk of uncertainty into the price.
- **Outsourcing, time and materials.** You pay for hours actually spent. Flexible, but requires budget control.
- **Outstaffing.** A monthly rate per specialist. The total depends on how efficiently you organize the work yourself.

Comparing models by hourly rate alone is misleading: outsourcing includes management, testing and responsibility for the result; outstaffing does not.

## Which model fits you

**Choose outsourcing if:**

- you have no CTO or experienced team lead;
- you need a specific product with a clear outcome;
- you do not want to build development processes in-house;
- the project is one-off or has well-defined stages.

**Choose outstaffing if:**

- you have a **tech lead** who can define tasks and review code;
- you have your own team that lacks capacity or specific expertise;
- the product evolves continuously and knowledge must stay with you;
- you need to scale the team quickly without long hiring cycles.

## Common mistakes

1. **Outstaffing without a lead.** Developers without clear tasks and oversight work slowly and in different directions.
2. **Outsourcing without requirements.** A vague spec turns into endless rework and disputes at acceptance.
3. **Choosing on price alone.** A low rate does not mean a cheap project.
4. **Code and access held by the contractor.** In both models, the repository, servers and code rights should be registered to you.

## FAQ

### Can the two models be combined?

Yes. For example, your core product is built by your own team reinforced with outstaffed specialists, while separate projects such as a landing page or an integration are outsourced.

### What if we have no tech lead but need ongoing development?

Start with outsourcing, where the contractor handles management, and look for a technical leader in parallel. Once they join, part of the work can move to an in-house or outstaffed team.

### How is outstaffing different from freelancing?

A freelancer works on their own. In outstaffing, a company is responsible for the specialist: it selects and employs them, provides a replacement and usually guarantees the contract terms.
