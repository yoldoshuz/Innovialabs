## The short answer

**Performance marketing** is advertising you pay for to get a specific action: a click, a lead, a purchase. Results show up in reports within days or weeks.

**Brand marketing** is the work of making people know, remember and trust you. Its results accumulate over months and show up indirectly: more direct visits, more searches for your name, more referrals, willingness to pay more.

It is not either-or. Performance captures demand that already exists. Brand creates demand and makes sure you are the one people remember at the moment of choice.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Parameter | Performance | Brand |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Leads, sales, installs | Awareness, trust, preference |
| **Audience** | People already looking for a solution | The broad market, including future buyers |
| **Channels** | Search ads, conversion-optimized social ads, retargeting, shopping campaigns | Video, reach campaigns, PR, content, sponsorship, outdoor, visual identity |
| **Metrics** | CPA, CPL, ROAS, ROMI, conversion rate | Reach, frequency, awareness, branded search, direct traffic, NPS |
| **Time horizon** | Days and weeks | Months and years |
| **How impact is measured** | Direct attribution in analytics | Surveys, brand lift studies, branded search trends, experiments |
| **What happens if you stop** | Lead flow drops quickly | Effect fades gradually |

## Why performance-only is a trap

Performance is convenient: every dollar spent shows up in a report. But it has limits:

- **Finite demand.** Only so many people are looking for your product right now. Once you capture them, extra budget buys increasingly expensive leads.
- **Rising costs.** Competing in the same auction pushes click prices up, and without awareness your ads have nothing to stand out with.
- **Attribution overvalues the last click.** Someone may have watched your video and read your article before clicking a search ad. The report gives search all the credit.

## Why brand-only is also risky

- The impact is hard to prove and the budget hard to defend.
- Money goes out before sales come in, which is dangerous for a young business.
- Without performance tools, you cannot capture the demand you have already created.

## How a growing business can balance them

There is no universal ratio. The balance depends on your stage, niche and sales cycle length. Practical guidelines:

1. **Early on**, performance is the foundation: it brings revenue faster and data about who your customer is and which messages work. But basic brand work is needed from day one: clear positioning, a solid website, visual identity, reviews.
2. **When performance hits a ceiling**, meaning cost per lead rises while volume does not, increase the brand share: content, video, reach campaigns aimed at your target audience.
3. **Connect the two.** Today's reach video becomes tomorrow's retargeting audience. Ads and the website should make the same promise.
4. **Watch leading brand signals**: branded search queries, direct traffic, share of returning customers. If they grow, brand work is paying off even when direct attribution does not show it.
5. **Test with experiments.** For example, run a reach campaign in one region only and compare sales trends with other regions.

## Common mistakes

- Judging brand campaigns by CPA and switching them off after two weeks.
- Thinking a nice logo is all brand marketing means.
- Making different promises in reach ads and performance ads.
- Relying only on last-click attribution.

## FAQ

### What is brand performance?

An approach where brand goals are pursued with tools that have a measurable response: for example, a reach video where you track not only reach but also the lift in branded search and conversions. It helps stop treating the two directions as opposites.

### When should a small business invest in brand?

From day one in the basics: positioning, website, design, reviews. In large reach campaigns, once performance stops scaling without rising cost per lead and you have budget that does not need to pay back this month.

### How do I know brand marketing is working?

Look at trends over several months: branded searches, direct traffic, conversion rates of performance campaigns, share of repeat purchases and awareness survey results. Judging brand by a single week is meaningless.
