## The short answer

A rebrand is needed when **the brand no longer matches the business**: the company repositioned, moved to a new audience or market, merged with another company or carries a damaged reputation. If the strategy is unchanged and the visuals are simply dated or awkward in digital channels, a **refresh** is enough: an update that keeps the essence. Wanting "something new" is not a reason on its own.

## Refresh or full rebrand

| | Refresh | Rebrand |
|---|---|---|
| What changes | logo, colors, typefaces, graphics | positioning, values, tone, often the name and the whole identity |
| Reason | visuals feel dated or fail in digital | strategy, audience or business structure changed |
| Risk of losing recognition | low | high |
| Scope | design and updating touchpoints | research, strategy, design, communication, rollout |

Many projects called rebrands are really refreshes: simplifying a logo for small screens, adding responsive versions, extending the palette for interfaces.

## Signals that it is time

- **New strategy**: new products, a move upmarket or toward the mass market.
- **New audience or market**: entering other countries, languages and cultures.
- **Merger or acquisition**: several brands need to be combined.
- **The name gets in the way**: confused with competitors, fails in new languages or has legal issues.
- **Reputation**: the brand is tied to events the company wants distance from.
- **Identity chaos**: years of conflicting versions, and a new system is easier than cleanup.

## How to run a rebrand

### 1. Research

- interviews with leadership and staff: where the company is heading;
- conversations with customers: why they choose you and how they describe you;
- competitor analysis: how not to blend in with similar players;
- an **asset audit**: which elements people recognize, such as a color, a symbol, a sound or a tagline.

### 2. Strategy

Define positioning, values, personality and tone of voice. Decide which elements are **kept** and which change. This decision matters more than any sketch.

### 3. Design

The logo and its versions, palette, typography, graphic elements, photo and illustration style, icons and motion. Test the identity on real touchpoints: app icon, messenger avatar, signage, documents and the product interface. Capture the result in a **brand book** and an asset library.

### 4. Rollout

- build an **inventory of every touchpoint**: website, apps, social media, documents, packaging, office, vehicles;
- decide between switching everything at once or in phases, depending on the number of touchpoints and the budget;
- brief **employees** before the public launch so they understand what changed and why;
- explain the change to customers with a short story about why the brand evolved;
- handle technical details: redirects if the domain changes, app store and map listings, legal documents.

## How not to lose recognition

- **Evolution, not revolution**, unless there is a strong reason to break with the past.
- Keep at least one **anchor**: a color, the shape of a symbol or the name.
- If the name changes, use a **transition period** with a note such as "formerly known as".
- **Test** new options for recognition with real people before launch.
- Do not leave old materials in circulation for long, since mixed versions confuse people more than the change itself.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with the logo before defining the strategy.
- Deciding by leadership taste instead of research.
- Underestimating the rollout effort.
- Launching without a brand book, so every contractor interprets the identity differently.

## FAQ

### How much does a rebrand cost and how long does it take?

It depends on scope: how deep the research goes, whether the name changes, and how many touchpoints and markets are involved. A refresh is usually much faster and cheaper than a full rebrand, and rollout is often the largest part of the work.

### Can we rebrand just the website?

You can redesign the website, but if the identity does not change everywhere you get a gap between touchpoints. Update the brand system first and make the website its first touchpoint.

### Should we ask customers before rebranding?

Yes, during research and testing. Do not pick the final option by vote, though: customers help you understand perception, while the decision follows the strategy.
