## The short answer

An **off-the-shelf CRM** (AmoCRM, Bitrix24, HubSpot, Salesforce and others) is a ready product you rent by subscription and configure: pipelines, fields, automations, integrations from a marketplace.

A **custom CRM** is software built for your company's processes. It belongs to you, works exactly the way your team works, but needs development and ongoing support.

For most small and medium businesses with a typical sales funnel, a ready CRM is the right start. Custom development makes sense when the ready product forces the team to work around it every day.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Off-the-shelf CRM | Custom CRM |
|---|---|---|
| Launch time | Days to weeks | Weeks to months |
| Cost structure | Subscription per user, grows with team; paid add-ons | Upfront development, then hosting and support |
| Fit to processes | Standard pipelines, configurable within limits | Exactly your logic |
| Integrations | Ready widgets plus an API with vendor limits | Any system, as deep as needed |
| Data | Stored by the vendor, export available | Stored where you decide |
| Maintenance | Vendor updates the product | You or your contractor |
| Risk | Vendor changes prices, features or terms | Dependence on the team that built it |

## Cost over time

Do not compare the first month. Compare a horizon of three to five years.

**Off-the-shelf** costs grow with:

- the number of users and the plan level;
- paid widgets, integrations and telephony;
- the time of an integrator who configures and fixes things.

**Custom** costs consist of:

- initial analysis, design and development;
- hosting, backups and monitoring;
- a team that fixes bugs and adds features as the business changes.

A small team usually pays less for a subscription. A large team with complex processes may find that subscriptions plus integrators cost comparable money while still not fitting the work.

## Fit to unique processes

Ready CRMs are built around a typical deal: lead, qualification, offer, payment. Signs your processes do not fit:

- You keep data in spreadsheets next to the CRM because there is no place for it inside.
- Managers enter the same information twice.
- Your workflow has steps the CRM cannot model: production stages, multi-level approvals, field visits, complex pricing.
- Reports are assembled manually every week.

If one or two of these apply, a ready CRM plus a small custom module or integration is often enough. If most apply, consider a custom system.

## Integrations

A store's CRM rarely lives alone. It needs to exchange data with the website, Telegram bot, payment gateways, telephony, 1C or ERP, marketplaces and delivery services.

- **Ready CRMs** offer many connectors, but each one follows the vendor's limits: API request quotas, available fields, update frequency.
- **Custom CRMs** can integrate any system directly and in real time, but every integration must be built and maintained.

## Data ownership and compliance

In a SaaS CRM your customer base is stored on the vendor's servers. Check:

- whether you can export everything — contacts, deals, history, files — in a usable format;
- where the servers are located. Uzbekistan has requirements on storing personal data of its citizens on servers in the country; discuss with a lawyer how they apply to you;
- what happens to data if you stop paying.

Some vendors, like Bitrix24, also offer an on-premise version you install on your own server — a middle ground between SaaS and custom.

## Maintenance burden

With a ready CRM, the vendor fixes bugs and releases features. With a custom CRM, this is your responsibility: security updates, backups, monitoring, documentation, and knowledge that should not live in one developer's head.

## A practical decision checklist

Choose an **off-the-shelf CRM** if:

- your sales process is close to the standard funnel;
- you need to start working this month;
- you have no budget or team for ongoing development.

Choose a **custom CRM** if:

- core processes are unique and give you a competitive advantage;
- you need deep real-time integrations with several systems;
- data location and control are critical;
- you are ready to fund support for years, not just the launch.

A **hybrid** is often the best answer: keep a ready CRM for sales and build custom services around it for what it cannot do.

## FAQ

### Can I move from a ready CRM to a custom one later?

Yes. Plan the migration of contacts, deals and history in advance, and check the export options of your current CRM before you sign up.

### Is a custom CRM always more expensive?

At the start, almost always. Over several years it depends on team size, subscription costs and how much work the ready CRM forces you to do manually or through integrators.

### What about AmoCRM vs Bitrix24?

Both are popular in the region. AmoCRM is focused on the sales pipeline, Bitrix24 is a broader suite with tasks, communications and an on-premise option. The right choice depends on your processes.
