## What search intent is

**Search intent** is the user's goal behind a query. Two queries with similar words can need completely different pages: "how to choose a coffee machine" expects an article, while "buy delonghi coffee machine" expects a catalogue with prices and an order button.

Search engines judge how well a page satisfies that goal. If the format doesn't match, even a well-optimised page will rank poorly.

## The four main types

| Type | Goal | Example queries | Fitting format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | Learn, understand | "what is a crm", "how does ssl work" | Article, guide, tutorial |
| Navigational | Reach a specific site | "telegram web", "gmail login" | Brand homepage or section |
| Commercial | Compare options before choosing | "best crm for small business", "tilda vs wordpress" | Review, comparison, roundup |
| Transactional | Take an action | "buy a laptop", "order website tashkent" | Product page, service page, request form |

The lines are not strict. "Website development price" is both commercial and transactional: the person is still comparing but already ready to get in touch.

## How to read intent from the SERP

The most reliable method is to open the results for a query and see what the search engine already treats as the right answer.

1. **What page types rank?** Articles, catalogues, service pages, videos.
2. **Which features appear?** Featured snippets and "People also ask" point to informational intent; shopping blocks and ads point to commercial intent.
3. **Whose sites are they?** Stores, aggregators, media, official brand sites.
4. **How are titles phrased?** "How to…", "Top 10…", "Buy… with delivery".
5. **Are there local results?** A map pack means location matters.

If the top results are almost all articles, a service page is unlikely to get there — and vice versa.

## How to pick the page format

- **One primary intent per page.** Don't try to teach and sell to everyone on the same page.
- **Informational intent:** an article that answers in the first paragraph and then expands on details.
- **Commercial intent:** a comparison with selection criteria, pros and cons and honest limitations.
- **Transactional intent:** a page where the price or the way to get it, the terms and a clear action button are easy to see.
- **Navigational intent** for your brand: make sure your site and key sections are easy to find.

Link formats together: from the article to the service page, from the service page to a comparison or FAQ.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing search volume alone.** A popular query with the wrong intent won't bring the visitors you need.
- **Selling inside an article.** Aggressive sales blocks on an informational page hurt user behaviour.
- **Mixing intents.** A "what is CRM + buy CRM + CRM comparison" page usually loses to three separate ones.
- **Never rechecking the SERP.** Intent can shift over time, especially for seasonal and news-driven topics.

## FAQ

### How do I tell the intent when the results are mixed?

That is mixed intent. See which format dominates the first positions, build the page for it and add a short block for the secondary goal.

### Do I need a separate page for every query?

No. Queries with the same intent and similar results are grouped on one page. A separate page is needed when the user's goal changes.

### Does intent affect conversion?

Yes. A visitor who lands on the right format finds the answer or takes action faster, so matching intent helps both SEO and sales.
