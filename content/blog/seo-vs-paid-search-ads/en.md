## The short answer

- **Paid search ads** (Google Ads, Yandex Direct) are the fast channel. Launch a campaign and traffic arrives. Stop paying and it disappears.
- **SEO** is slow but cumulative. For the first months you invest without much visible return, then your pages bring visitors without paying for each click.

The choice is not about which is "better" but about **how much time you have, what your budget is and whether there is search demand**.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Paid search ads | SEO |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed of results** | from the first days after launch | usually several months or more |
| **Cost structure** | pay per click + setup and management | specialists' work, content, development |
| **After you stop** | traffic stops | traffic declines gradually, unless the site is neglected |
| **Control** | precise choice of queries, location, schedule, budget | positions depend on algorithms, no guarantees |
| **Testing** | quick way to validate demand and offers | too slow for experiments |
| **User trust** | some people skip ads | organic results feel neutral |
| **Competition** | click prices rise with the number of advertisers | competition on content and site quality |

## How the costs work

**With ads** you pay for every click. The price is set by an auction: the more competitors bid on the same query, the higher it goes. Campaign setup and optimization are paid separately. Spending scales directly with traffic.

**With SEO** you pay for the work: audit, technical improvements, content, external mentions. Clicks are free. Early costs are high relative to results, but later the cost per visitor usually falls because the pages you built keep working.

## What fits your business

**Prioritize ads if:**

- you are just launching and need first customers now;
- the offer is seasonal or temporary (a promotion, an event, a course intake);
- you need to quickly test demand for a product or a new offer;
- you serve a small region with few searches.

**Prioritize SEO if:**

- search demand is steady and large (services, products, B2B, education);
- customers research and compare for a long time and need detailed pages and articles;
- click prices in your niche are high and ads eat your margin;
- you plan to stay in the market for years.

**Neither fits well** if nobody knows about the product yet and nobody searches for it. Then demand is built through social media, content and partnerships, and search comes later.

## When to combine both

For most businesses the best option is **using both together**:

1. **Start with ads.** You get leads and data: which queries bring customers and which only burn budget.
2. **Feed what you learn into SEO.** Queries that convert well in ads become priorities for site pages.
3. **Shift gradually.** As organic traffic grows, cut ads on queries where you already rank at the top and keep them where SEO is hard to win.
4. **Share analytics.** Both channels land on the same site, so speed, usability and a clear offer improve both.

## Common mistakes

- **Expecting SEO results in a month** and quitting halfway.
- **Running ads to a weak site.** You pay for clicks, but visitors leave without contacting you.
- **Not measuring return.** Without analytics and goals you cannot tell which channel really brings customers.
- **Choosing "forever."** Revisit the balance between channels as the business grows.

## FAQ

### Do paid ads affect organic rankings?

No, not directly. They are separate systems. Indirectly, ads help by showing which queries bring customers, which guides SEO planning.

### How much does SEO cost compared with ads?

There is no universal figure. Cost depends on niche competition, region, the site's condition and the scope of work. Compare customer acquisition cost in each channel rather than raw budgets.

### Can I turn off ads once SEO starts working?

Partly, yes — on queries where the site ranks consistently at the top. Turn them off completely only after confirming that total leads do not drop.
