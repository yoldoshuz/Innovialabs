## The short answer

- **Shopify** — a hosted SaaS platform. You pay a subscription, pick a theme, add apps, and the store works within days. Hosting, security and updates are Shopify's job.
- **WooCommerce** — a free open-source plugin that turns a WordPress site into a store. You choose hosting, themes and extensions, and you are responsible for updates and security.
- **Custom development** — a store built for your business on a framework or a headless e-commerce engine. Any logic and integrations are possible, but it takes longer and needs a team.

There is no universal winner. The right choice depends on where your business is now and what it needs in the next one or two years.

## Comparison

| Factor | Shopify | WooCommerce | Custom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch speed | Fastest | Fast with a ready theme | Slowest |
| Upfront cost | Low | Low to medium | High |
| Ongoing cost | Subscription, apps, possible transaction fees | Hosting, paid plugins, developer time | Hosting, support and development team |
| Flexibility | Within platform rules and apps | High, limited by WordPress architecture | Full |
| Local payments | Depends on available payment apps | Plugins from providers or community | Any provider via its API |
| Maintenance | Mostly on the platform | On you | On you or your contractor |
| Data and code | Platform-hosted, export available | Fully yours | Fully yours |

## Total cost of ownership

Comparing launch prices is misleading. Count what you will pay over two or three years:

- **Shopify**: plan subscription, paid apps (they add up quickly), theme, and extra transaction fees if you use a payment provider other than Shopify's own where such fees apply on your plan.
- **WooCommerce**: hosting that can handle your traffic, premium theme and extensions with annual licences, regular updates, backups, security fixes and the developer who does all this.
- **Custom**: initial development, then hosting, monitoring, bug fixes and new features. It costs the most at the start but has no per-order platform fees or per-app subscriptions.

## Local payments: check first

For stores in Uzbekistan this is often the deciding factor.

- **Shopify**'s own payment processing works only in a limited list of countries. Local gateways such as Payme or Click can be connected only if a payment app for them exists in the Shopify ecosystem. Verify this before you commit.
- **WooCommerce** usually has plugins for local providers, published by the providers themselves or by the community. Check that the plugin is maintained and compatible with your versions.
- **Custom** development integrates any provider directly through its API: Payme, Click, Uzum, Stripe for foreign cards — and payment logic adapts to your processes.

## Flexibility and scaling limits

Signs a ready platform is starting to hold you back:

- Business logic that apps cannot cover: B2B prices per client, complex discounts, configurable products, subscriptions with special rules.
- Deep integrations: two-way sync with 1C, a warehouse system, several marketplaces, your own CRM.
- Performance: a WooCommerce store with large catalogs and many plugins needs serious optimisation; Shopify handles load well but within its own API limits.
- Many apps that overlap and conflict, and every change becomes a workaround.

A middle ground is **headless commerce**: a platform or open-source engine handles the catalog, cart and orders through an API, while the storefront is built separately for speed and design freedom.

## Which stage fits which option

| Business stage | Best fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Testing a product idea | Shopify or WooCommerce with a ready theme | Launch fast, spend little, learn from real sales |
| Steady sales, small team | WooCommerce or Shopify with careful app choice | Balance of cost and control |
| Growth with unique processes and integrations | Custom or headless | Platform workarounds cost more than development |
| Large catalog, several channels and warehouses | Custom with an OMS and ERP integration | Full control over data and logic |

## Common mistakes

- Starting custom development before the business model is proven.
- Choosing Shopify without checking local payment and delivery options.
- Installing dozens of WooCommerce plugins without a plan for updates.
- Forgetting migration: plan how products, customers and orders will move if you change platforms later.

## FAQ

### Can I start on Shopify or WooCommerce and move to custom later?

Yes, and it is a sensible path. Keep your data clean and exportable, avoid platform-specific hacks where possible, and plan redirects for SEO when you migrate.

### Is WooCommerce really free?

The plugin is free, but the store is not: you pay for hosting, extensions, updates and someone to maintain it. Count these costs before comparing with Shopify.

### When is custom development clearly worth it?

When your processes, integrations or load require so many workarounds on a ready platform that their total cost and risk exceed the cost of building and supporting your own solution.
