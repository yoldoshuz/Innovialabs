## The short answer

Slack is a team chat organised around **channels** — shared rooms for a topic, project or team — rather than around people. It stays useful only if you agree on three things early: **what goes into which channel**, **replies go into threads**, and **everyone tunes notifications** so urgent things stand out. Without these rules Slack quickly becomes a noisy group chat.

## Key concepts

- **Workspace** — your company's space in Slack with its own members, channels and settings. Most teams need exactly one.
- **Channel** — a room for a topic. **Public** channels are visible and searchable for everyone in the workspace; **private** channels are invite-only. Prefer public: it keeps knowledge searchable.
- **Direct messages (DMs)** — one-to-one or small group chats. Fine for personal matters, bad for work decisions others may need later.
- **Thread** — replies attached to one message. They keep the channel readable: the main feed shows topics, the discussion stays underneath.
- **Huddle** — a quick audio or video call right inside a channel or DM, with screen sharing. Good for a five-minute question instead of a long message chain.
- **Status** — emoji and text next to your name ("In a meeting", "Sick", "Focus time") with an automatic clear time.
- **Slack Connect** — shared channels with other companies, such as a client or contractor.

## Notifications: set them up in five minutes

Notification overload is the main reason people hate Slack. Each person should:

1. In **Preferences → Notifications**, choose to be notified about **direct messages, mentions and keywords**, not every message.
2. Set a **notification schedule** — working hours only.
3. For each important channel, open the channel settings and choose **All messages**, **Mentions** or **Nothing**. Mute channels you only read occasionally.
4. Add **keywords** such as your project name or role.
5. Use **Pause notifications** when you need focused time.

## Channel naming

Prefixes make the channel list readable and searchable. A simple scheme:

| Prefix | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `team-` | A department or team | `#team-design` |
| `proj-` | A project with a start and end | `#proj-website-redesign` |
| `help-` | Questions to a specific function | `#help-it`, `#help-hr` |
| `ann-` | Announcements, few posters | `#ann-company` |
| `ext-` | Shared channels with partners | `#ext-client-name` |
| `alerts-` | Automated notifications | `#alerts-payments` |
| `social-` | Off-topic, hobbies | `#social-food` |

Rules for names: lowercase, words separated by hyphens, no abbreviations only three people understand. Add a short **channel description** saying what belongs there, and **archive** channels when a project ends.

## Etiquette rules for the team

- **Reply in threads.** Use "Also send to channel" only when the outcome matters to everyone.
- **One message, not five.** Write the whole thought at once: context, question, deadline.
- **No naked "Hi".** Instead of "Hi, got a minute?" write the actual question.
- **Mentions with care.** `@here` notifies active members, `@channel` notifies everyone in the channel. Reserve them for genuinely urgent or important things.
- **Use reactions** for acknowledgement: a checkmark instead of "ok, thanks".
- **Decisions go to a public channel**, not DMs, so others can find them later.
- **Keep the status honest:** away, on vacation, focus time.
- **Respect time zones and hours.** Use scheduled send for messages outside working hours.
- **Long documents live elsewhere.** Link to the doc or canvas instead of pasting pages of text.

Write these rules in a pinned message or canvas in `#ann-company` and show them to every new member.

## Common mistakes

- Dozens of private channels and DMs — knowledge disappears from search.
- One `#general` for everything — important posts get lost.
- No channel owner — outdated channels pile up.
- Using Slack as a task tracker. Tasks belong in Jira, Trello or Notion; Slack is for talking about them.

## FAQ

### Is the free plan enough for a small team?

It is enough to try Slack, but the free plan has limits on visible message history, integrations and huddle participants. If the team relies on old discussions or several integrations, a paid plan is usually needed. Check current limits on Slack's pricing page.

### What is the difference between @here and @channel?

`@here` notifies only members who are currently active, while `@channel` notifies everyone in the channel, including those away. Many teams restrict `@channel` in large channels to admins.

### Public or private channel by default?

Public. Private channels are for sensitive topics such as HR, finance or security. Everything else should be open so new colleagues can find past decisions.
