## The short answer

A good content plan answers three questions: **what** you post about, **in what form** and **how often**. The process:

1. Define the goal of your social media and your audience.
2. Set **3-5 content pillars**.
3. Choose a **format mix** for each platform.
4. Pick a **frequency** you can actually sustain.
5. Keep the plan in a single **table**, a month ahead.
6. **Adjust** monthly based on the numbers.

## Step 1. Goal and audience

Business social media can serve different goals: awareness, leads, customer support, hiring. Choose one or two main ones, because they determine both your pillars and your metrics.

Then list your **audience's questions and pain points**. The best sources: questions in DMs and comments, sales calls, reviews, search queries. Every frequent question is a ready-made topic.

## Step 2. Content pillars

A **content pillar** is a recurring theme that serves your goal. Examples for a service business:

- **Expertise** — answers to customer questions, breakdowns, tips.
- **Product** — how the service works, your process, prices and terms.
- **Trust** — reviews, portfolio examples, the team, behind the scenes.
- **Engagement** — polls, questions to the audience, discussions.
- **Sales** — direct offers, promotions, calls to submit an inquiry.

Sales posts are necessary, but if the whole feed is advertising, people stop responding. Find the right balance by watching how your audience reacts.

## Step 3. Format mix

One topic can live in several formats:

| Format | Best for |
|---|---|
| Short vertical video (Reels, Shorts) | Reaching new people, quick tips |
| Carousel | Step-by-step guides, checklists, comparisons |
| Stories | Daily contact, polls, behind the scenes, urgent news |
| Photo post | Reviews, case studies, announcements |
| Long text (Telegram, LinkedIn) | Expert breakdowns, opinions |
| Live stream | Q&A sessions, launches |

Save effort by **repurposing**: one breakdown becomes a carousel, a short video, a series of stories and a Telegram post.

## Step 4. Frequency per platform

There is no universal rule: algorithms change and audiences differ. The main principle is that **consistency beats volume**. Starting points to refine with your own data:

- **Instagram** — several feed posts a week and stories almost daily.
- **Telegram channel** — from a few posts a week up to daily, if you have something to say.
- **Facebook and LinkedIn** — a few times a week, focused on quality writing.
- **TikTok and YouTube Shorts** — more often than feed posts, since the format is quick to produce.

With limited resources, one platform with regular content beats three abandoned ones.

## Step 5. Planning template

Keep the plan in a shared table. A minimal set of columns:

| Date | Platform | Pillar | Format | Topic | Goal | Status | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Instagram | Expertise | Carousel | 5 questions before ordering | Saves | Ready | Designer |
| Wed | Telegram | Product | Text | What the first call looks like | Leads | Draft | Copywriter |
| Fri | Instagram | Trust | Reels | A day in a project | Reach | Idea | SMM |

Add a column for links to assets and a separate **idea bank** tab where the team drops topics throughout the month.

## Step 6. Adjust based on analytics

Once a month, compare pillars and formats by metrics tied to your goal:

- **Reach** — for awareness.
- **Saves and shares** — a signal of usefulness.
- **Comments and messages** — engagement and purchase interest.
- **Clicks and leads** — with UTM tags on links so results appear in your website analytics.

Do more of what works and try it in new formats. Rework or drop what consistently fails. Likes on their own are a weak guide.

## Common mistakes

- A three-month plan scheduled day by day that falls apart in week two.
- Copying the same post to every platform without adapting it.
- No link between content and goal: posts go out, leads do not come, and nobody knows why.

## FAQ

### How far ahead should I plan content?

A detailed plan for one month and rough topics for the next works well. Leave open slots for news and timely content.

### Where should I keep the content plan?

Any shared spreadsheet or task tracker with a calendar view will do. Having one plan that everyone can see matters more than the specific tool.

### What if I run out of ideas?

Go back to the sources of questions: DMs, comments, the sales team, reviews. Repurpose your best older posts into new formats, since new followers have not seen them yet.
