## The main point

Being ready to launch is more than "the developers are done". Even a flawless app will stumble if customers cannot reach support, payments fail and staff do not know how to process orders. Below is a **business readiness** checklist an owner can go through personally.

## Legal pages

- **Privacy policy**: what data you collect, why and where it is stored.
- **Terms of service or public offer** if you sell goods or services.
- **Return and delivery terms** for an online store.
- **Consent to personal data processing** in forms.
- Company details and contacts on the site.

Have a lawyer review the texts: requirements depend on the country and the type of product.

## Support processes

- It is clear **who answers customers** and during which hours.
- Contact channels (phone, Telegram, email, chat) work and have been tested with a real message.
- There are reply templates for common questions.
- An escalation path exists: what to do when the issue is technical and needs a developer.

## Analytics

- Analytics is installed and you have checked that events **actually arrive**.
- Key goals are set up: lead, sign-up, payment.
- You have chosen 3–5 metrics that will tell you whether the launch worked.
- Error and uptime monitoring is configured, so you hear about failures before customers do.

## Staff training

- Staff who handle leads and orders **have gone through the product themselves**.
- There is a short guide: where to see new orders, how to change statuses, whom to contact when something breaks.
- Access to the admin panel and CRM has been issued and tested.

## Payments

- Payment is tested with a **real small transaction** in live mode, not only in test mode.
- Refunds and failed payments have been checked.
- Receipts, notifications and customer emails are sent correctly.
- You know how the money shows up in your accounting.

## Rollback plan

| Question | What should be ready |
|---|---|
| What if a serious bug appears? | A way to restore the previous version |
| Where is the data? | A fresh database backup, verified by a restore |
| Who decides to roll back? | A named person and a way to reach them |
| How do we warn customers? | A prepared notification text |

## The launch itself

- Pick a day when the team is available, not right before holidays or a weekend.
- Consider a **soft launch**: first for some users or without advertising.
- Turn on ads only after you are sure everything works.
- In the first days, read customer messages and analytics closely.

## FAQ

### Can I launch without a privacy policy?

If the product collects any personal data, even a name and phone number in a form, you need one. Without it you face legal risks and lower user trust.

### What is a soft launch and why do it?

It means opening the product to a limited audience without active promotion. Bugs are found on a small group of users instead of on all the traffic after an ad campaign.

### Who owns this checklist, the developer or the owner?

Developers help close the technical items, but business readiness is the owner's area: legal texts, support, training and the final launch decision.
