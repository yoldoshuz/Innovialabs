## Why a sprint review matters to the client

A **sprint review** (demo) is the meeting at the end of a sprint where the team shows what was done. For the client it is the main control tool: every one or two weeks you see a **working product**, not a percentage report.

The value appears when the review ends with **decisions**:

- what is accepted as done;
- what needs rework;
- what changes in priorities for the next sprint.

If you just watched and said "fine", the meeting was wasted.

## How to prepare

Preparation takes little time and greatly increases the return.

1. **Recall the sprint goal.** What did the team commit to? Open the sprint task list in the tracker.
2. **Check the acceptance criteria.** How will you know a feature works the way it should?
3. **Invite the right people.** Those who will use the feature: a sales manager, an operator, an accountant. Their view is more precise.
4. **Bring your own news.** Market changes, customer feedback, shifted plans — the team needs to know before planning.

## What to check during the review

- **Does it work on staging**, not just on a developer's laptop.
- **Real scenarios**, not only the ideal path. Ask to see an input error, an empty list, a cancelled action.
- **Realistic data.** Long names, many records, different languages.
- **The mobile version**, if people use the product on phones.
- **What was not done and why.** This is not a reason to blame, but information for planning.

Best of all, ask for staging access and **walk through the scenario yourself** right after the review.

## How to give useful feedback

Bad feedback: "Not quite right", "Make it prettier", "Not what I wanted".

Good feedback answers three questions:

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| What exactly is wrong? | The manager cannot see the customer's phone in the order card |
| Why does it matter? | They have to switch to another system to call back |
| What result is needed? | The phone number is visible in the card and clickable |

Describe the **problem and the goal**, not a ready-made solution. The team may suggest something simpler and better.

Sort feedback by importance: **blocks launch**, **important but can wait**, **nice to have**.

## Turning the review into backlog decisions

Spend a few minutes at the end on a wrap-up:

- **Accepted tasks** are closed.
- **Rework** becomes separate tasks with clear descriptions.
- **New ideas** go to the backlog, not automatically into the current plan.
- **Priorities** for the next sprint are revisited based on what you saw.

Ask the team to send a short summary with the list of decisions. It prevents "but we agreed" arguments later.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping reviews and looking at results once a month.
- Coming to the meeting without knowing what was planned.
- Turning the review into an hour-long debate about button colors.
- Pushing new ideas straight into the current sprint.
- Staying silent during the review and sending comments a week later.

## FAQ

### How long should a sprint review take?

Usually from half an hour to an hour for a one- or two-week sprint. If it regularly runs over, move detailed discussions into separate short calls with the right people.

### What if there is almost nothing to show?

Find the cause: underestimated tasks, blockers on your side, technical problems. Discuss it in the retrospective and adjust the next sprint's scope instead of demanding speed without changing anything.

### Can I accept work right during the review?

Provisionally, if the feature meets its acceptance criteria. For important features, it is better to walk through the scenarios on staging yourself after the meeting and only then close the tasks.
