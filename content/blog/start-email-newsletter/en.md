## The short answer

Launching a newsletter takes five steps:

1. A **platform** with the features you need and your own domain connected.
2. A **signup form** with explicit consent and address confirmation (**double opt-in**).
3. **Segments** from day one: where people came from and what they care about.
4. A **welcome email** that goes out automatically.
5. A **schedule** you can actually keep, not an ideal one.

Never buy email lists: it breaks platform rules and data protection laws, and those sends quickly push your domain into spam.

## How to choose an email platform

Ignore the marketing promises and check the features you will need over the next year:

| Criterion | What to check |
|---|---|
| Email editor | Drag-and-drop blocks, mobile-friendly output, HTML editing |
| Signup forms | Embedded forms, pop-ups, built-in double opt-in |
| Segmentation | Tags, custom fields, behaviour segments (opened, clicked, bought) |
| Automation | Triggered sequences: signup, purchase, inactivity |
| Deliverability | Sending from your own domain, SPF and DKIM setup |
| Integrations | CMS, online store, CRM, API and webhooks |
| Pricing | Per contact or per email — which scales better for you |

From the start, **send from your own domain**: bulk email from a free mailbox address delivers poorly, and some mailbox providers reject it outright.

## How to collect subscribers legally

The foundation is **explicit consent**. People type in their own address and understand what they are signing up for.

- State **what you will send and how often** right in the form.
- Never pre-tick the consent box.
- Link to your privacy policy.
- Turn on **double opt-in**: after signing up, the person gets a confirmation link, and only after clicking it does the address join your list.

Double opt-in filters out typos, other people's addresses and bots, and it gives you proof of consent. Consent rules depend on where your subscribers live: check your local personal data law, and GDPR for an EU audience.

Where to collect addresses: a form in the site footer, a dedicated page offering a useful resource (checklist, guide), checkout (as a separate opt-in box), and offline events with written consent.

## Segment from day one

Even a small list is worth tagging immediately; doing it later is much harder:

- **Source**: website, store, webinar, lead magnet.
- **Interest**: the topic or category they picked.
- **Status**: subscriber, customer, former customer.
- **Engagement**: opens and clicks, or silent.

Add one optional field to the form, such as a topic choice. More fields mean fewer signups.

## The welcome email

This email gets more attention than any other, because the person has just signed up. Include:

- Thanks and the **promised resource**, if there was one.
- What you will send and how often.
- One action: read your best article, browse the catalogue, reply to a question.
- A request to add your address to their contacts.

Keep it short and written by a real person, not "the team".

## Sending schedule

Frequency should match what you promised in the form and what your resources allow. One good email every two weeks, consistently, beats weekly emails for a month followed by silence: subscribers forget who you are and hit "spam".

A practical rhythm:

- Keep a **content calendar** a month ahead.
- Write emails in batches and schedule them.
- Fix a day and time so the newsletter becomes a habit.
- Once a month, review **opens, clicks, unsubscribes and complaints**. Open rates are now distorted by privacy protection in mail apps, so rely on clicks first.

## Common mistakes

- Importing old or bought addresses without consent.
- No unsubscribe link, or a hidden one.
- The first email arrives a month after signup.
- Every email is a sales pitch with no value.

## FAQ

### Do I need double opt-in for a small list?

Yes. At the start it protects your domain's reputation from mistyped and fake addresses. Some people will not confirm, but those who do will actually read your emails.

### Can I send a newsletter from a regular mailbox?

For a dozen people, yes; for a regular newsletter, no. A regular mailbox has no unsubscribe handling, statistics or segments, and bulk sending from it can get the account blocked.

### What should I do with subscribers who stopped opening emails?

Send them a re-engagement email asking whether they want to stay on the list. Remove those who do not respond from regular sends: it improves deliverability for everyone else.
