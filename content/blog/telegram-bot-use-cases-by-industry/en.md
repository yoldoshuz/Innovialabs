## The short answer

A Telegram bot is useful when customers or employees **walk the same path over and over**: pick a product, book a slot, check a status, leave a request, fill in a form. The bot guides people through those steps without an operator, around the clock, and passes the data to your CRM or back-office system.

Below are concrete scenarios by industry. Look for the processes that already eat your managers' time.

## Scenarios by industry

| Industry | Main scenarios | Where the data goes |
|---|---|---|
| Retail | Catalog, ordering, delivery status, loyalty | Inventory, warehouse, CRM |
| Food delivery | Menu, cart, payment, order tracking | Kitchen, couriers, POS |
| Clinics | Doctor booking, reminders, test preparation | Doctor schedules, medical system |
| Education | Course sign-up, timetable, homework, quizzes | LMS, CRM, spreadsheets |
| Real estate | Property matching, viewing booking, lead qualification | Agency CRM |
| HR | Job applications, screening form, onboarding, employee FAQ | ATS, HR system |

## Retail

- **Catalog and ordering.** A short catalog works with buttons; a large one is better as a Telegram Mini App inside the bot.
- **Order status** by order number or phone: the most common support question is answered automatically.
- **Loyalty**: bonus balance, a personal promo code, offers for people who opted in.
- **Returns and exchanges**: the bot collects the order number, reason and photos, so the operator gets a ready ticket.

## Food delivery

- **Menu, cart and payment**, usually in a Mini App, because building a multi-item order in a chat is awkward.
- **Status notifications**: accepted, cooking, handed to the courier.
- **Repeat last order** in one tap.
- **Feedback after delivery**: a quick rating, with negative reviews routed straight to a manager.

## Clinics

- **Doctor booking**: choose a specialist, date and free time from the real schedule.
- **Reminders** the day before with "Confirm" and "Reschedule" buttons, which means fewer empty slots.
- **Procedure preparation**: instructions arrive automatically after booking.
- **Test results** only with proper authentication and respect for medical data rules. If you cannot guarantee that, stick to a "your results are ready" notice.

## Education

- **Course sign-up and payment**, with access granted after payment.
- **Timetable and reminders** for classes and deadlines.
- **Homework submission**: the file or link reaches the tutor tagged with the student and lesson.
- **Short quizzes** with answer buttons and instant checking.

## Real estate

- **Property matching** by district, budget and number of rooms.
- **Lead qualification**: purchase timeline, mortgage or cash, purpose, so the agent gets a warm contact.
- **Viewing booking** with slot selection and a reminder.
- **Alerts for new listings** that match saved criteria.

## HR

- **Collecting applications**: the bot asks basic questions, accepts a CV and filters out candidates who clearly miss formal requirements.
- **Onboarding**: a first-days plan, contacts, links to documents.
- **Answers to common employee questions**: leave, certificates, schedules.
- **Internal surveys** and feedback collection.

## How to pick the first scenario

1. Find **which questions repeat most often** in chats and calls.
2. Choose a flow with **a clear finish**: request created, booking made, status shown.
3. Check **where the bot gets its data**: schedules, stock, statuses. Without an integration a bot quickly starts giving wrong answers.
4. Decide in advance **how to hand the person over to a human** when the bot cannot help.

## Common mistakes

- Building a bot "for everything" instead of one flow that actually takes load off people.
- Sending messages without consent: users block the bot and the channel is lost.
- Not connecting the bot to the CRM, so requests are copied by hand again.
- Launching without analytics, so you cannot see at which step people drop off.

## FAQ

### Do I need a separate bot for each scenario?

Usually not. One bot can run several flows through a menu. Separate bots make sense when the audiences differ, for example customers and employees.

### When do I need a Mini App instead of a regular bot?

When users need to browse, compare and choose a lot: catalogs, carts, calendars, filters. Short flows of a few questions are more convenient to keep in the chat.

### Can I start without a CRM integration?

Yes, to test the idea: requests can arrive in a team chat or a spreadsheet. As volume grows you will need the integration, otherwise manual data entry eats up the savings.
