## The short answer

A company channel works when it **regularly publishes something useful to the reader**, with selling woven in along the way.

- **Content mix**: useful posts, product, behind the scenes, reviews, interactive.
- **Frequency**: a predictable rhythm your team can keep up for months. For most companies that is somewhere between a few posts a week and one a day.
- **Formats**: text with an image, albums, video, video messages, polls, stories.
- **Measurement**: once a month, review reach, engagement and unsubscribes per category.

## Content mix: five categories

| Category | What to post | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Useful | Tips, how-tos, common mistakes, answers to frequent questions | A reason to subscribe and stay |
| Product | New arrivals, offers, how to choose, comparisons | Sales |
| Behind the scenes | Team, process, production, preparing orders | Trust and a human voice |
| Reviews | Real customer reviews and photos, shared with permission | Social proof |
| Interactive | Polls, questions to readers, votes on new products | Reactions, comments, ideas |

The rule is simple: **useful and human posts should clearly outnumber sales posts**. A channel where every post is an ad quickly becomes noise people mute.

## Building the content plan

1. List **20–30 questions** customers ask your managers. These are ready-made topics for useful posts.
2. Assign categories to **days of the week**. Planning gets easier and readers know what to expect.
3. Plan **a month ahead**, leaving room for news and timely topics.
4. Prepare posts in batches and **schedule** them; scheduling is built into Telegram.
5. Name one **owner**. A channel without an owner goes quiet.

An example week for a small business: Monday useful, Wednesday product, Friday behind the scenes or a review, plus a poll every week or two.

## Formats and when to use them

- **Text + image** is the workhorse. The first line acts as the headline because it shows in the preview.
- **Albums**: several product photos, before and after, a step-by-step process.
- **Video**: product demos, short walkthroughs.
- **Video messages** (round videos): a short, informal message from the founder or a specialist. Great for behind the scenes.
- **Polls and quizzes**: a quick way to get reactions and learn what your audience thinks.
- **Stories**: channels can post them after reaching the required boost level. Good for urgent offers and short announcements.
- **Comments**: enable discussions through a linked group if you are ready to answer.

Use a **pinned post** as navigation: who you are, what the channel offers, how to buy or get in touch.

## Measuring reach and engagement

Once a channel passes a certain number of subscribers, **detailed statistics** become available. What to look at:

- **Post views**: reach. Compare posts of the same age, for example one day after publishing.
- **ERR**: average post views divided by subscribers. It shows what share of your audience actually reads you.
- **Engagement**: reactions, comments and shares relative to views.
- **Shares**: the most valuable signal. The post was useful enough to pass on.
- **Joins and leaves** by day: match spikes in unsubscribes to specific posts.
- **View sources** and **active hours**: when to publish.

Once a month, summarize the numbers by category: what drives reach, what gets shared, what causes unsubscribes. Do more of what works and drop the rest.

## Common mistakes

- Posting in bursts: five posts in a day, then two weeks of silence.
- Copying Instagram posts without adapting them to Telegram.
- Long texts with no paragraphs or emphasis.
- Judging the channel by subscriber count instead of reach and reactions.

## FAQ

### How many posts a day is too many?

Watch unsubscribes. If they rise on days with several posts, the pace is too dense. Fewer, steady and relevant posts beat volume.

### Should I enable comments?

Yes, if you are ready to reply quickly and moderate. Unanswered comments full of spam hurt more than having none.

### How do I find out which format works best?

Compare posts from the same category in different formats by views, reactions and shares over the same period. A few weeks is usually enough to see a difference.
