## The short answer

- **Channel** — one-to-many broadcasting. Only admins post; subscribers read, react and, if enabled, comment.
- **Group** — many-to-many chat. All members can write, if you allow it.

If you need to **tell** people things, use a channel. If you need people to **talk** to each other, use a group. If you need both, use a channel with a linked discussion chat.

## The formats

**Channel.** Unlimited subscribers, and only admins see the subscriber list. Every post shows a view counter. You can schedule posts, send them silently, sign them with the author's name and view statistics.

**Basic group.** A small chat for a team or a close circle. Minimal settings.

**Supergroup.** When a group grows or you turn on certain settings (a public link, advanced admin rights), Telegram automatically converts it into a supergroup. It offers:

- fine-grained permissions for admins and members;
- slow mode, which limits how often members can post;
- **topics** (forum mode) — separate threads inside one group;
- join requests approved by an admin;
- hiding chat history from new members.

**Discussion chat.** A regular group linked to a channel. Each channel post is forwarded into it automatically, and comments under the post are messages in that post's thread. The group still works as a full chat.

## Comparison

| | Channel | Group / supergroup | Channel + discussion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who writes | Admins only | Members | Admins post, everyone comments |
| Purpose | News, content, announcements | Conversation, peer help, support | Content with feedback |
| Member visibility | List hidden | Members see each other | Commenters are visible |
| Moderation effort | Minimal | High | Medium |
| Spam risk | Low | High without protection | Medium, in comments |
| Analytics | Views, reach, statistics | Limited | Channel statistics |

## Business scenarios

**A channel fits:**

- company news, promotions and new catalog items;
- expert content that builds trust;
- customer notices: opening hours, changes in terms;
- a private channel for clients or staff via an invite link.

**A group fits:**

- a customer community where people help each other: users of a service, course participants;
- B2B client support — one group per client with your managers in it;
- internal work chats, and with topics, one space with sections per department or project.

**A channel with discussion fits when:**

- you want questions and reactions on specific posts;
- the main feed should stay clean while conversation happens in comments.

## How much moderation it takes

- **A channel** needs almost none: only your team can post.
- **Comments** need watching: spam bots often leave links under popular posts.
- **An open group** needs rules, a pinned message, admins across time zones or a moderation bot: checks for newcomers, link filters, automatic spam removal.

Telegram's built-in tools — slow mode, restricted rights for new members, join requests — cover the basics. Large communities usually add a moderation bot.

## Common mistakes

- Creating a group instead of a channel for news — important posts drown in chatter.
- Opening a group with no rules or moderation — over time it fills with spam.
- Not linking a discussion chat to the channel and losing audience feedback.
- Using a group for support where customers see each other's requests, although they need privacy. A bot or private messages work better here.

## FAQ

### Can I convert a group into a channel?

No, they are different chat types and there is no automatic conversion. Usually you create a channel, move the content over and invite group members to subscribe.

### Should I enable comments on my channel?

If you value feedback and are ready to moderate it, yes. If nobody can keep an eye on comments, leave reactions only.

### What should I use for customer support?

For mass-market customers, a bot or private messages, so requests stay private. A group makes sense for B2B, when your team and one client's staff work in the same chat.
