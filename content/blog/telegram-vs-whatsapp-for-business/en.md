## The short answer

Choose the messenger **your customers already use**. That outweighs any feature: a perfect bot in a messenger your audience ignores will not bring in leads.

If your audience uses both, the tasks decide:

- **Telegram** is better for bots, Mini Apps, content channels and low-cost automation.
- **WhatsApp** is stronger for one-to-one customer chats where it is the default messenger, but bulk messaging and bots go through a paid API with strict rules.

## Audience and region

Messenger popularity varies a lot by country. In Uzbekistan and several other CIS countries, Telegram is the main messenger and news source for many people. In many other countries, everyday chatting happens on WhatsApp.

How to check with your own data:

- ask current customers how they found you and where they prefer to talk;
- if your website shows both buttons, see which one gets clicked more;
- if you sell in several countries, the right channel may differ for each.

## Feature comparison

| Criterion | Telegram | WhatsApp |
|---|---|---|
| Bots | Free Bot API, created via BotFather | Only through the Business API |
| Web UI inside the chat | Mini Apps | Limited forms, no full web apps |
| Messaging a customer first | A bot can only message users who started it | Via API only with an approved template |
| Channels | Channels with no subscriber cap | Channels exist, with fewer business tools |
| Broadcasts | Free in a channel and via bot within rate limits | Paid templates, only with customer opt-in |
| In-chat payments | Payment providers, plus Telegram Stars for digital goods | In some countries; elsewhere a payment link |
| Platform costs | The Bot API itself is free | Fees for template messages and provider services |

## Bots and automation

On **Telegram**, anyone can create a bot in minutes and the Bot API is free: you only pay for development and hosting. You can add a **Mini App** with a catalog, cart or customer account inside the messenger. The one restriction is that a bot cannot message someone until that person has started it.

On **WhatsApp**, automation runs through the **WhatsApp Business API**. It is an official but more controlled route: you need a Meta business account, templates go through review, and business-initiated messages are paid.

## Payments

On **Telegram**, the Bot API lets you accept payments for physical goods and services through a connected payment provider. Digital goods and services inside Telegram are paid with **Telegram Stars**.

On **WhatsApp**, built-in payments are available only in certain countries. Elsewhere, businesses send a **payment link** in the chat.

## Broadcasts and rules

A **Telegram channel** is a free publishing tool for news, promotions and content. Bot broadcasts reach users who started the bot, subject to rate limits, and any user can block the bot in one tap.

On **WhatsApp**, bulk messaging is tightly regulated: you need recipient opt-in and an approved template. Complaints lower the number's quality rating and can lead to restrictions.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing by the owner's personal habit** instead of the customers' habits.
- **Launching both channels without staff** to answer: customers in the "slow" channel leave.
- **Copying a Telegram broadcast strategy** to WhatsApp without accounting for paid templates and consent rules.
- **Not connecting channels to a CRM**, so leads get lost between messengers.

## How to pick your primary channel

1. Find out **where most of your customers chat**.
2. Decide what matters more: **content and community** (Telegram's strength) or **one-to-one chats** in the messenger your audience is used to.
3. Estimate the automation budget, including **per-message fees** on WhatsApp.
4. Launch the primary channel and keep the second one as an **extra entry point** with a clear response time.

## FAQ

### Can a business run on both messengers at once?

Yes, as long as both feed into one CRM or helpdesk. Otherwise replies get lost and customers wait.

### Where is automation cheaper?

Usually on Telegram: the Bot API is free and you pay for development and hosting. WhatsApp adds template message fees and, typically, provider charges.

### Can a Telegram channel replace a website?

As a source of content and news, yes. But you still need a website for search, advertising and trust, since many customers look a company up in a search engine first.
