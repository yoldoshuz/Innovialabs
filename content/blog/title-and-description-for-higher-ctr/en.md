## The short answer

The **title** is the main driver of snippet clicks: it should include the query, promise a concrete benefit and fit in the results. The **description** barely affects rankings directly, but it helps convince people to choose your link. Both tags are written for a specific page, not as one template for the whole site.

## Length limits

Search engines truncate snippets by pixel width rather than exact character counts, so these are guidelines:

| Tag | Guideline | What matters |
|---|---|---|
| title | roughly 50–60 characters | key words first |
| description | roughly 120–160 characters | meaning survives truncation |

You can write longer, but users will not see what gets cut off.

## Title formulas

**Commercial pages:**

- `Query + benefit + city/brand` — "Flower Delivery in Tashkent Within 2 Hours — FlowerShop"
- `Query + modifier + USP` — "Work Laptops: Warranty and Installments"

**Informational pages:**

- `How to + action + result` — "How to Set Up robots.txt: Rules and Examples"
- `What is + term + benefit` — "What Is a CRM and Why Small Businesses Need One"
- `List + topic` — "7 Mistakes When Launching an Online Store"

Only promise what is real: if the title says "within 2 hours", the page must back it up.

## Description formulas

A simple pattern: **what the person gets + a differentiating detail + a soft call to action**.

- Commercial: "We arrange bouquets and deliver across Tashkent. Fresh flowers, photo before dispatch. Order online."
- Informational: "We cover robots.txt directives, templates for popular CMSs and ways to test the file. Step by step, with examples."

## Before and after

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Home — Company | Air Conditioner Repair in Tashkent — Same-Day Visit |
| SEO article | How to Write an SEO-Optimized Article That Ranks |
| Products | Buy Office Chairs: Catalog, Prices, Delivery |

Weak titles do not say what is on the page; strong ones answer the query and give a reason to click.

## Why search engines rewrite snippets

Google and Yandex may show a different title or description if they decide your text poorly reflects the page or the query. Typical reasons:

- the title is too long or stuffed with keywords;
- the same title and description appear on many pages;
- the tag does not match the page content;
- the description is missing, so a text fragment is used instead.

You cannot fully prevent rewriting, but accurate, unique and concise tags are used more often. See [Google's documentation on title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link).

## Common mistakes

- One template for every page.
- Listing keywords separated by commas.
- ALL CAPS, extra symbols and clickbait the page does not deliver.
- Putting the brand first where users do not need it.

## FAQ

### Does the description affect rankings?

Barely, directly. But it influences whether people click the snippet, so it is worth writing by hand for important pages.

### Should I include the brand name in the title?

Usually the brand goes at the end after a dash or pipe. Put it first only when people search specifically for your brand.

### How do I check whether a new title works?

Compare the page's impressions, clicks and CTR in Google Search Console and Yandex Webmaster over equal periods before and after the change.
