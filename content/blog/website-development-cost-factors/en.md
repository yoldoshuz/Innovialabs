## The short answer

You pay for a website as **specialists' time**: designer, developers, content manager, QA engineer. That time depends on six factors — site type, design, CMS, integrations, content and languages. Here is how each one moves the budget.

## Site type

| Type | What it includes | Complexity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Landing page** | One page, a lead form | Low |
| **Corporate website** | Several sections, services, contacts, blog | Medium |
| **Online store** | Catalog, cart, payments, user account | High |
| **Web service / portal** | Custom business logic, roles, dashboards | High and above |

The more logic a site has, the more backend development and testing it needs.

## Design: template or custom

- **Template** — quick start and low price, but the site looks like thousands of others, and adapting a template is sometimes harder than building from scratch.
- **Custom design** — structure planning, prototypes, desktop and mobile layouts, a design system. It takes longer, but the site works for your brand and conversion.

**Animation** matters too: complex interactive effects take time from both the designer and the frontend developer.

## CMS and technology

A **CMS** is the content management system you use to edit the site.

- **Off-the-shelf CMS** (WordPress, Tilda, Bitrix) — many ready modules, fast launch.
- **Headless CMS** (Strapi, Sanity, etc.) plus a modern frontend — flexibility and speed, but more work up front.
- **A custom admin panel** — when you need specific processes that ready-made systems lack.

The right question is not "which CMS is best" but **who will edit what**. If a couple of texts change once a month, you do not need a complex admin.

## Integrations

Each connection to an external system is a separate task:

- payments (Payme, Click, Stripe);
- CRM and 1C — passing leads, orders, stock levels;
- Telegram notifications, SMS, email campaigns;
- analytics, maps, live chat.

Integration cost depends on the quality of the service's API. A standard module connects quickly; a non-standard export from an accounting system can take a lot of time.

## Content

Content is a frequent cause of missed deadlines. Decide early who prepares:

- **copy** for pages and services;
- **photos and video** — a shoot or stock;
- **product cards** — descriptions, specs, images;
- **SEO texts** and metadata.

If the contractor creates content, it is a separate budget line. If you do, plan the time for it.

## Multilingual support

Each language is more than a translation. You need to:

- design language switching and page URLs;
- check layouts, since line lengths differ between languages;
- set up SEO for each version;
- keep content in all languages updated.

In Uzbekistan, Russian, Uzbek and English are often needed. Plan for this from day one rather than bolting it on later.

## What else to account for

- **Hosting and domain** — recurring payments.
- **Responsiveness** — the site must work on phones; this is mandatory, not optional.
- **Maintenance** — updates, backups, fixes.
- **Speed and SEO** — technical optimization is built in during development.

## How to save wisely

1. Launch a **minimal version** and grow it based on analytics.
2. Prepare **content in advance** — it speeds up the project.
3. Use **ready modules** where there is no unique logic.
4. Describe the task in a **technical specification** — fewer rewrites.

## FAQ

### Why is a website builder cheaper than custom development?

A builder gives you ready blocks and hosting, so there is less work. In exchange you accept limits on design, speed, integrations and moving the site elsewhere. That is fine for a simple landing page, not for a complex project.

### How long does it take to build a website?

Like the price, the timeline depends on scope: a landing page is faster than a corporate site, and a store or service takes longer. The most common delay is not development but waiting for content and approvals.

### Should I launch several languages from the start?

If your audience speaks different languages, build a multilingual architecture from the start. Translations can be added gradually, but restructuring the site later costs more.
