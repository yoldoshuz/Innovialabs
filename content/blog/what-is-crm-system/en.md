## The short answer

**CRM (Customer Relationship Management)** is a system where a company keeps all its customers and deals in one place. Instead of notebooks, personal messenger chats and a separate spreadsheet for every salesperson, there is one shared database that shows who the customer is, what they wanted, what they were promised and what the next step is.

Put simply, a CRM answers the question: what is happening with each customer right now, and who is responsible for it?

## What a CRM stores

- **Contacts and companies**: name, phone, email, Telegram, job title and the company the person belongs to.
- **Deals (leads)**: a specific potential sale with an amount, a stage and an assigned salesperson.
- **Interaction history**: calls (often recorded through IP telephony), emails, messenger chats and internal comments.
- **Tasks**: "call back on Thursday", "send the proposal", "remind about payment", each with a deadline and an owner.
- **Sales pipeline**: the stages a deal moves through, for example "new lead → qualified → proposal sent → invoice → paid".
- **Lead sources**: website, ads, Instagram, phone call, referral.

## Problems it solves

### Lost leads

A request from the website or a messenger lands in the CRM and is assigned to someone immediately. If nobody picks it up, a manager can see that. Without a CRM, leads disappear in inboxes and personal chats.

### Forgotten customers

The CRM reminds people about tasks. A customer who asked to be called back in a month will not be forgotten, because the task sits in the system with a date.

### Dependence on one salesperson

When someone leaves or goes on holiday, the full customer history stays in the database. A colleague opens the record and picks up the conversation where it stopped.

### No idea where sales drop off

The pipeline shows at which stage deals stall or fall through. Reports show which channels bring customers and which only spend budget.

### Chaotic support

For service teams, a CRM stores customer requests, their status and purchase history. The agent sees the context and does not ask the customer the same questions again.

### Manual routine

Automations handle repetitive steps: create a task when a deal changes stage, message the customer, pass an order to the warehouse, issue an invoice.

## Types of CRM

| Type | Examples | Good fit when |
|---|---|---|
| **Cloud, off the shelf** | AmoCRM, Bitrix24, HubSpot, Pipedrive | Standard sales process, fast start needed |
| **Self-hosted with customization** | Bitrix24 on-premise edition | You need your own server or deep changes |
| **Custom-built CRM** | Developed for the company | Unusual process, many integrations, the box gets in the way |

Most companies should start with a ready-made cloud CRM. Custom development makes sense when your process differs a lot from the standard one, or when integrations with the website, warehouse, accounting system and messengers become more complex than the CRM itself.

## How to roll out a CRM without pain

1. **Write down your sales process**: where customers come from, which stages they go through, who does what.
2. **Set up the pipeline** to match that process, not the other way round. Too many stages only slow people down.
3. **Connect lead sources**: the website form, messengers, telephony.
4. **Import your customer base** and clean up duplicates.
5. **Agree on rules**: every lead goes into the CRM, every deal has an owner and a next task.
6. **Add automations gradually**, once the basic process works.

## Common mistakes

- Buying a CRM but still running customers through personal chats, so the system stays empty.
- Copying someone else's 15-stage pipeline instead of building your own with 5–6 stages.
- Not connecting the website and messengers, so salespeople copy leads by hand and make errors.
- Choosing a CRM by feature count rather than by how comfortable it is to use every day.

## FAQ

### Does a small business need a CRM?

If you have more than one salesperson or customers come back to buy again, a CRM is already useful. A small team can start with a simple cloud system and a basic pipeline.

### Why is a CRM better than Excel?

A spreadsheet stores data, but it does not remind you about tasks, capture leads automatically or keep conversation history, and it handles several people editing at once poorly.

### Should I choose AmoCRM or Bitrix24?

AmoCRM is usually simpler and focused on pipeline sales and messengers. Bitrix24 is broader: besides the CRM it includes tasks, projects and an internal company portal. The choice depends on whether you need only a CRM or a whole workspace.
