## Short answer

**Digital marketing** is attracting and keeping customers through online channels. Its main difference from billboards or print is **measurability**: you can see which ad brought which lead, what that lead cost and where people drop off before buying.

Any working setup has three layers:

- **Channels** — where people first meet your business: search, social networks, email, messengers.
- **Destination** — where they take the target action: a website, landing page, Telegram bot or app.
- **Measurement** — analytics that connects money spent in channels to leads and sales.

If one layer is missing, you either get no traffic, lose the traffic you paid for, or cannot tell what worked.

## The main online channels

| Channel | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Search ads** (Google Ads, Yandex Direct) | You pay to appear when people type a relevant query | Capturing demand that already exists |
| **Social ads** (Meta Ads for Instagram and Facebook, Telegram Ads) | Ads shown by interests, demographics and behaviour | Creating demand, new or visual products |
| **SMM** | Regular content in your own accounts and communities | Trust, brand, staying in touch |
| **Email** | Letters to people who subscribed or bought | Repeat sales, nurturing long decisions |
| **Content and SEO** | Articles, guides and videos that rank in search | Steady inbound traffic over time |
| **Messengers** | Telegram channels, bots, WhatsApp chats | Fast contact, support, repeat orders |

A useful split: **search captures demand** (people already look for a solution), while **social ads and content create demand** (people did not know they needed you yet). Most businesses need both, in different proportions.

## How channels combine into one system

People rarely buy after a single touch. A typical path looks like this: someone sees an Instagram ad, later searches for your brand, reads an article on your site, subscribes to your Telegram channel and buys after a special offer.

Each channel covers its own part of the path:

- **Awareness** — social ads, video, content.
- **Consideration** — search ads, reviews, comparison pages, **retargeting** (ads shown to people who already visited your site).
- **Conversion** — landing page, form, bot, phone call.
- **Retention** — email, messenger, loyalty offers.

The system holds together only if:

1. Every ad link carries **UTM tags**, so analytics knows where each visit came from.
2. Analytics records **key actions**: form submissions, calls, messenger clicks, purchases.
3. Lead data reaches a **CRM**, so you see which channel brought paying customers, not just clicks.

## Minimum toolset

- **Analytics:** Google Analytics 4 and/or Yandex Metrica.
- **Ad accounts:** Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager, Yandex Direct, depending on your audience.
- **Tag management:** Google Tag Manager, to add tracking without editing site code each time.
- **CRM:** to follow a lead from first contact to payment.
- **Email or messenger tools:** for newsletters, chains and broadcasts.

## Metrics worth knowing

- **CTR** — share of people who clicked after seeing an ad.
- **CPC** — cost per click.
- **CPL** — cost per lead.
- **Conversion rate** — share of visitors who completed the target action.
- **CAC** — total cost of acquiring one paying customer.
- **ROMI / ROAS** — how much revenue or profit marketing spend returned.

Clicks and reach are useful for diagnosing ads, but decisions should be based on CPL, CAC and revenue.

## How to start

1. **Define one goal** and one target action: a request, a call, a purchase.
2. **Prepare the destination:** a fast page with a clear offer and a short form.
3. **Set up analytics and goals before launch**, not after.
4. **Pick one or two channels** that match demand: if people already search for your service, start with search ads; if not, with social ads.
5. **Test with a limited budget**, compare cost per lead and lead quality, then scale what works.
6. **Add retention:** email or messenger follow-ups for those who did not buy right away.

## Common mistakes

- Launching ads without analytics, then guessing what worked.
- Judging channels by clicks instead of leads and sales.
- Spreading a small budget across every channel at once.
- Sending all traffic to the home page instead of a relevant landing page.
- Answering leads hours later, when the interest has already cooled.

## FAQ

### How is digital marketing different from SMM?

SMM is one channel inside digital marketing. Digital marketing also includes search and social ads, email, content, messengers and the analytics that ties them together.

### Which channel should a small business start with?

Start where demand already is. If people actively search for your product, search ads usually give the fastest leads. If the product is new or impulse-driven, social ads are a better first test.

### How much budget do I need?

There is no universal figure. It depends on competition in your niche, cost per click in your region, your conversion rate and margin. The test budget should be large enough to collect a meaningful number of leads per channel before you judge it.
