## The short answer

**Dropshipping** is an e-commerce model where the store holds no inventory. You list products on your site, take the order and payment, then pass the order to a **supplier**, who ships it directly to the customer.

You earn the **difference** between your selling price and the supplier's price. The main upside is no upfront investment in stock. The main downside is that you are accountable to the customer for things you do not physically control.

## How the chain works

1. A customer places and pays for an order in your store.
2. The store forwards the order to the supplier — manually, through a supplier portal or automatically via API.
3. You pay the supplier the wholesale price and shipping.
4. The supplier packs and ships the parcel to the customer.
5. A tracking number comes back to the store and the customer is notified.
6. Questions, complaints and returns go to **you**, not the supplier.

## Where margin comes from and where it goes

On paper the margin is simple: selling price minus supplier price. In practice you subtract:

- **customer acquisition cost** — advertising is usually the largest expense;
- **payment processing fees**;
- **shipping**, if it is not included in the supplier price;
- **returns and refunds**;
- **subscriptions** for the platform, apps and automation services.

Because many stores sell the same products from the same suppliers, competition quickly turns into a price war. Sustainable margin comes from niche, service, content and brand, not from the product itself.

## Risks worth knowing

| Risk | What happens | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery times** | Goods ship from far away, the customer waits | Suppliers closer to customers, honest delivery estimates |
| **Quality** | You never see the product before it ships | Test orders, review checks, several suppliers |
| **Out of stock** | Supplier stock changes and your site does not know | Automatic stock sync |
| **Returns** | The supplier may refuse or complicate returns | Return terms agreed in advance |
| **Packaging and brand** | The parcel arrives unbranded, sometimes with someone else's inserts | Suppliers offering branded packaging |
| **Dependency** | The supplier disappears or changes prices | Backup suppliers for key products |

Also consider **customs and tax rules** for cross-border shipping: they vary by country, and customers may face extra charges on delivery.

## Technical integrations you need

A dropshipping store runs on automation. The minimum set:

- **Catalogue import** — products, descriptions, photos and prices from a supplier feed (XML, CSV) or API.
- **Stock and price sync** — regular updates so you never sell what is unavailable.
- **Automatic order forwarding** to the supplier right after payment.
- **Tracking numbers** and delivery statuses flowing back to the store.
- **Customer notifications** — email, SMS or messengers.
- **Payments** — Payme, Click, Stripe or other gateways depending on your market.
- **CRM or help desk** so customer requests do not get lost.

With several suppliers you also need **order routing**: items from one cart go to different suppliers, and the customer sees a clear status for each part.

## Who the model suits

- **Testing a niche** and demand without buying stock.
- As an **add-on** to a main range, for items that are not worth keeping in a warehouse.
- Projects where value comes from **content, curation and service** rather than an exclusive product.

It is a weaker fit if fast delivery, quality control and your own product brand matter to you.

## FAQ

### Do I need to register a business for dropshipping?

Yes — you accept money from customers and are responsible to them as the seller. Requirements for business structure and taxes depend on the country, so check them with a lawyer or accountant.

### Can I dropship through marketplaces?

Some platforms restrict or prohibit selling without your own stock, or require strict dispatch times. Study the rules of the specific platform before you start.

### How is dropshipping different from made-to-order?

With made-to-order, products are often produced or purchased after payment, but you ship them yourself. In dropshipping the supplier ships, and you never physically handle the goods.
