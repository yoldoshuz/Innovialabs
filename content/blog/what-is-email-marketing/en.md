## The short answer

**Email marketing** is communicating with customers and prospects through messages sent to their inbox. The key difference from advertising: you write to people who **gave you their address and agreed** to hear from you. The audience belongs to you, not to an ad platform, and each extra message costs almost nothing.

Email still works when three conditions are met:

- the list was built honestly, with consent;
- emails respond to a specific action or interest;
- you measure results and clean the list.

## Three types of emails

| Type | When it is sent | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Newsletter** | On a schedule, to the whole list or a segment | Article digest, new products, webinar announcement |
| **Trigger email** | Automatically after an action or inaction | Abandoned cart, welcome series, renewal reminder |
| **Transactional email** | As part of the service, in response to an operation | Order confirmation, receipt, password reset, shipping status |

People expect and open **transactional** emails, so make them clear and well designed. **Trigger** emails usually perform best among marketing emails because they arrive at the moment of interest. **Newsletters** keep the relationship alive between purchases.

Send transactional emails through a separate stream or subdomain, so complaints about newsletters do not hurt delivery of receipts and password resets.

## Consent and compliance

Buying lists or emailing "cold" addresses scraped from public sources is a fast way into the spam folder. Mailbox providers track complaints and engagement, and data protection laws in many countries require confirmed consent.

What to set up:

- A **consent checkbox** in the form that is not pre-ticked.
- **Double opt-in**: a confirmation email after sign-up. The list grows slower but has fewer typos and bots.
- An **unsubscribe link** in every marketing email that works in one click.
- **SPF, DKIM and DMARC** for the sending domain. Without them, emails land in spam more often.

## Where email works

**E-commerce:**

- welcome series after sign-up;
- abandoned cart and abandoned browse;
- replenishment for products that run out (cosmetics, pet food, supplies);
- review requests after delivery.

**B2B:**

- a sequence after someone downloads a guide, checklist or price list;
- webinar and demo invitations;
- a regular expert digest for long sales cycles;
- SaaS onboarding emails that help users take their first steps in the product.

Email is weaker where purchases are impulsive one-offs with no repeat business, and in niches where customers barely use email.

## Key metrics

- **Deliverability**: the share of emails that reach the inbox rather than spam or bouncing.
- **Open rate**: the share of opens. Today it is imprecise: some email clients load messages automatically, which inflates opens. Use it only to compare subject lines within the same list.
- **CTR (click-through rate)**: the share of recipients who clicked a link. More reliable than open rate.
- **Conversion rate**: the share of recipients who completed the target action, such as a purchase, lead or sign-up. Track it with UTM tags and site analytics.
- **Unsubscribes and spam complaints**: a sign that emails are irrelevant or too frequent. Rising complaints quickly hurt deliverability.
- **Bounce rate**: the share of undelivered emails. Remove hard bounces (non-existent addresses) from the list immediately.

## Common mistakes

- Sending the same email to the entire list without segments.
- Keeping inactive subscribers for years, which damages sender reputation.
- Using one large image instead of text.
- Not checking the email on mobile, where most people read their mail.
- Sending from a noreply address and ignoring customer replies.

## FAQ

### Hasn't email died because of messengers?

No, its role has changed. Messengers are convenient for quick messages, while email remains the channel for receipts, documents, long-form content and B2B correspondence. Many businesses use both and pick the channel for the task.

### Which email service should I choose?

Look for automation support, integration with your CRM or CMS, a convenient editor, segmentation and transparent pricing based on list size. For transactional emails, teams often use a separate service with an API.

### How often should I send a newsletter?

As often as you have something useful to say. Start with a clear rhythm, such as weekly or every two weeks, and watch unsubscribes and clicks: if unsubscribes rise, reduce frequency or segment the list.
