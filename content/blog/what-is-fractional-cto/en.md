## The short answer

A **fractional CTO** is a chief technology officer who works for your company a few days a month or a set number of hours per week. They make the same decisions a full-time CTO would, but they are not with you every day and often lead several companies at once.

The core idea: you get **director-level experience and accountability** without paying a full salary for someone you cannot yet keep busy.

## What they actually do

The scope depends on your stage, but it usually covers:

- **Technical strategy.** Choosing the stack, architecture and cloud, and deciding whether to build or buy.
- **Hiring and management.** Interviewing developers, setting up processes and code review, assigning work to contractors.
- **Vendor oversight.** Checking estimates, timelines and code quality when development is outsourced.
- **Security and risk.** Access control, backups, personal data requirements.
- **Business translation.** Turning the owner's goals into a technical plan, and explaining why a feature takes a month, not a week.
- **Investor readiness.** The technical side of due diligence and answering investor questions.

What you should **not** expect: that they will write most of the code. It is a leadership role, not an extra developer.

## Cost compared to a full-time CTO

There are no universal numbers; they depend on market, experience and format. What matters is where the difference comes from:

| Factor | Full-time CTO | Fractional CTO |
|---|---|---|
| Pay | Full salary, taxes, often equity | Hourly or fixed monthly fee |
| Workload | Full-time, even when there is little to do | Exactly as much as you need |
| Hiring | Long search, risk of a bad hire | Quick start, easy to change format |
| Product immersion | Maximum | Limited by hours |
| Availability | Always on the team | As agreed |

A fractional CTO's hourly rate is usually higher than a full-timer's per hour, but the total monthly spend is much lower because you do not pay for idle time.

## When you really need one

A fractional CTO is especially useful if:

- **You are a non-technical founder** about to commission an MVP and need someone to review the spec, estimate and vendor.
- **The product is live**, the team is small, and architecture decisions are made by a junior developer.
- **You work with an outsourcing studio** and cannot judge the quality of its work.
- **Fundraising is coming** or a technical review is planned.
- **Your CTO has left**, and you need someone for the transition.

## When to hire full-time

Signs that part-time is no longer enough:

- the team is large enough to need a leader every day;
- technology is the core of the product and decisions come constantly;
- the fractional CTO regularly exceeds the agreed hours;
- you need deep involvement in culture and hiring.

A good practice is to have the fractional CTO find and onboard their own successor.

## Common hiring mistakes

- **Hiring a senior developer under a CTO title.** A strong programmer does not always know how to manage, budget and talk to the business.
- **Not defining outcomes.** Without quarterly goals it is unclear what you are paying for.
- **Withholding access and authority.** A CTO who cannot decide becomes a consultant.
- **Expecting full availability.** Agree on response times and urgent cases upfront.

## How to choose

1. Ask about companies at a similar stage and the decisions they made there.
2. Give a small paid task: a code audit or a review of your current spec.
3. Check whether they can explain technical topics in plain language.
4. Fix the format: hours, responsibilities, reporting, review date.

## FAQ

### How is a fractional CTO different from an IT consultant?

A consultant gives recommendations and you make the decisions. A fractional CTO owns the technology direction: makes decisions, leads the team and is accountable for the result.

### Do I need a CTO if an outsourcing studio does all development?

Often yes. Someone on your side should review estimates, architecture and quality; otherwise you depend entirely on the vendor and cannot evaluate its work.

### How much time should they spend on the company?

It depends on the stage: a few hours a week can be enough while preparing an MVP, while active development with a team needs more. Review the format every quarter.
