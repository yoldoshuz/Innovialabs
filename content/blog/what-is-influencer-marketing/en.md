## What influencer marketing is

**Influencer marketing** means working with people who hold the attention and trust of the audience you need: Instagram and TikTok creators, Telegram channel authors, YouTubers, industry experts. You are paying not for screen space but for a recommendation from someone followers know.

Its strength is trust and authentic context. Its weakness is measurement: unlike an ad platform, reach is reported by the creator rather than the system, and sales often come later.

## Influencer tiers

Boundaries are conventional and differ between sources, but the logic is the same:

| Tier | Audience (rough guide) | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| **Nano** | under 10K | Close relationship with followers, high engagement, often open to barter |
| **Micro** | 10K–100K | Narrow topic, strong trust, good for niches and local business |
| **Mid-tier** | 100K–500K | Balance of reach and trust, usually has a manager and a rate card |
| **Macro** | 500K–1M | Large reach, broader and more mixed audience |
| **Mega and celebrities** | 1M+ | Brand awareness, high price, weak link to specific sales |

More followers does not mean better. For a niche product, several micro creators in your topic often beat one macro.

## Collaboration formats

- **Sponsored integration** — a post, story, Reel or mention in a video.
- **Review or unboxing** — the creator shows the product in use.
- **Giveaway or contest** — quick follower growth, but often untargeted.
- **Ambassadorship** — a long-term partnership with regular brand mentions.
- **Affiliate program** — payment as a share of sales via a personal link or promo code.
- **Co-created product or content** — a collection, a course, a video series.
- **Barter** — product in exchange for a post; typical at nano and micro level.

## How to vet audience quality

Before paying, ask for **account statistics**: screenshots or a screen recording from the platform's built-in analytics, not a verbal summary. Check:

1. **Location and language.** Does the audience match your market?
2. **Gender and age** against your customer profile.
3. **Post and story reach** relative to follower count. Suspiciously low reach suggests an inactive or bought audience.
4. **Engagement.** Likes, comments, saves, story replies.
5. **Comment quality.** Real questions and discussion are good; repetitive emojis and "nice!" from empty accounts are a red flag.
6. **Sudden follower spikes** with no visible reason.
7. **Past integrations.** How much advertising the creator runs, how followers react, and whether they recently worked with direct competitors.

## How to track results

The key is giving every creator **their own identifier** so sales do not blend together.

**Personal promo code.** For example, `ANNA10`. It works even when someone did not click a link but remembered the brand and came back later. The downside: codes leak to coupon sites.

**UTM link.** A tagged link that tells analytics where the visit came from:

```text
https://example.com/promo?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=influencer&utm_campaign=spring_launch&utm_content=anna
```

If the link is long, use a short link or a dedicated page, but make sure the tags survive the redirect.

**What to measure:**
- clicks and leads from UTMs in GA4 or Yandex Metrica;
- promo code uses and the revenue attached to them;
- cost per lead and per purchase for each creator;
- growth in branded searches and direct visits on publication days — an indirect effect that tags do not capture.

Put everything in one table: creator, format, date, cost, reach, clicks, leads, sales. After a few campaigns it becomes clear who is worth working with again.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing creators by follower count without checking statistics.
- No agreed brief: what to show, key messages, what not to say.
- Forcing a scripted ad instead of the creator's natural style.
- Not labeling ads where the law or platform rules require it.
- Judging results the next day, although some sales arrive later.

## FAQ

### How much does an influencer post cost?

There is no single price list. Cost depends on the creator's tier, topic, format, how long the post stays up and content usage rights. Compare cost per lead or sale after publication, not the price of the post.

### Which is better: a promo code or a UTM link?

Use both. The UTM link shows clicks and on-site behavior; the promo code captures people who came later or directly. Together they give a fuller picture.

### Does influencer marketing work for B2B?

Yes, in a different form: instead of lifestyle creators, work with industry experts, authors of professional Telegram channels and speakers. The audience is smaller but far more precise.
