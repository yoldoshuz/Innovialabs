## What a lead and lead generation are

A **lead** is a potential customer who has shared contact details and shown interest: filled in a form, messaged you, called, or downloaded a resource. An anonymous website visitor is not a lead yet, because you cannot reach them.

**Lead generation** is the process that turns audience attention into those contacts. It has three parts: attract people (ads, content, search), give them a reason to leave a contact (a form, a consultation, a useful resource), and hand the lead to sales so it does not get lost.

## Qualification: MQL and SQL

Not every lead is ready to buy, so leads are split by readiness:

- **MQL (Marketing Qualified Lead)** — matches your customer profile and showed interest, but is not ready for a sales conversation. For example, downloaded a checklist and subscribed to your newsletter.
- **SQL (Sales Qualified Lead)** — checked by sales and confirmed ready for a deal: a real need, budget, authority and a clear timeline.

Between them sits **qualification**: a shared set of criteria that marketing and sales agree on to define a good lead. Without it, marketing reports on volume while sales complains about quality.

Simple qualification criteria:

1. Does the company or person fit your target profile (industry, size, location, income)?
2. Do they have a real problem your product solves?
3. Are you talking to the decision-maker or someone who influences the decision?
4. Is there a timeline and a budget?

## Lead generation channels

| Channel | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Search ads** | Ads shown for queries from people already looking for a solution | B2C and B2B with clear demand |
| **Social ads** | Targeting by interests and demographics | B2C, impulse and visual products |
| **SEO and content** | Articles and pages found through search | B2B, complex products with long decisions |
| **Email** | Nurturing a list you already have | B2B, repeat sales |
| **Messengers and chatbots** | A conversation in Telegram or WhatsApp instead of a form | B2C, markets where people prefer to chat |
| **Cold outreach** | Direct emails and messages to selected companies | B2B with a narrow account list |
| **Partnerships and referrals** | Leads from partners and happy clients | B2B and high-ticket services |
| **Events** | Conferences, webinars, trade shows | B2B |

## B2B vs B2C: what changes

**In B2C**, one person usually decides, the sales cycle is short and lead volume is high. Speed and simplicity win: a short form, a messenger button, a fast reply.

**In B2B**, several people decide, the cycle is long and deals are larger. Expertise and nurturing matter more: useful content, case studies, consultations, follow-up emails. A single quality lead can cost many times more, and that is fine.

## Cost per lead vs lead quality

**CPL (cost per lead)** is what you pay for one contact. It is convenient but risky. If you optimize for CPL alone, ad platforms will find the cheapest people, who are less likely to buy.

Look at the whole chain:

- **CPL** — cost of a contact.
- **Lead-to-SQL rate** — the share of leads that turned out to be real.
- **Cost per SQL** and **customer acquisition cost (CAC)** — what a lead that reached a deal actually cost.
- **Revenue or customer LTV** — whether that spend pays off.

A channel with expensive leads often wins because its leads buy more often. Compare channels by cost per customer, not cost per form. That requires leads to land in a CRM with their source (for example via UTM tags), and deal status to flow back into analytics.

## Common mistakes

- **Judging marketing by lead count** without looking at sales.
- **Long forms for no reason.** Every extra field reduces submissions; ask only what the first step needs.
- **Slow follow-up.** A lead contacted the next day is often already talking to a competitor.
- **No shared qualification criteria** between marketing and sales.
- **Leads living in spreadsheets and chats** instead of a CRM, so their source and outcome are unknown.

## FAQ

### How much should one lead cost?

There is no universal number. An acceptable cost depends on your margin, average order value, lead-to-deal rate and repeat purchases. Work out what you can afford to spend to acquire a customer, then divide it by the number of leads you need for one deal.

### What is the difference between a lead and a customer?

A lead is an interested contact; a customer is someone who paid. Qualification, negotiation and the deal sit between them, and some leads drop out at every step.

### Do I need a CRM for lead generation?

If you have more leads than you can keep in your head, yes. A CRM records the source, status and history of each lead. Without it you cannot compare channels by actual sales.
