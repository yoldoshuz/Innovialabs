## The short answer

**Notion** is a cloud workspace where documents, tables and task lists are built from the same set of building blocks. Instead of a separate text editor, spreadsheet and task tracker, you get one system that you shape around your team.

The core idea: everything in Notion is a **page**, and pages are made of **blocks**. Pages can be grouped into **databases**, which you can then work with as a table, a task board or a calendar.

## The three building blocks

### Pages

A page is a document with a title, an icon and content. Pages nest inside each other, so it is easy to build a tree such as "Company → Departments → Processes". Any page can be shared on its own or published to the web.

### Blocks

Every paragraph, heading, checklist, image, table, code snippet or embedded video is a **block**. Blocks can be dragged around, turned into other block types (a paragraph into a to-do, for example) and linked to. Typing `/` opens a menu with every block type.

### Databases

A database is a collection of pages with **properties**: status, assignee, date, tags, numbers. The same database can be shown through different **views**: table, Kanban board, calendar, timeline, gallery, list. Databases can be connected with **relations**, for example tasks to projects.

## How teams use Notion

- **Knowledge base (wiki)**: onboarding, policies, how-to guides, FAQs.
- **Task and project management**: task boards, sprints, a roadmap on a timeline.
- **Product documentation**: requirements, specs, meeting notes, decision logs.
- **Lightweight CRM**: clients, deals or hiring candidates with statuses.
- **Content calendar**: posts with dates, owners and readiness status.
- **Personal productivity**: notes, habit trackers, weekly planning.

The real strength is that everything is connected: a meeting note can reference a task, and that task shows up in the project database.

## Pricing tiers: what to look at

Notion offers several tiers: **Free**, **Plus**, **Business** and **Enterprise**. Exact prices and limits change, so check the official pricing page. When choosing, the factors matter more than the price tag:

- **Members and guests** — the free plan is aimed mainly at individuals; teams hit its limits quickly.
- **Version history** — how long page history is kept.
- **Permissions** — private teamspaces, granular roles and SAML SSO come with higher tiers.
- **Security and administration** — audit logs, user provisioning and enterprise security requirements sit at the Enterprise level.
- **AI features** — availability and terms depend on the plan.

## Where Notion is a poor fit

- **Strict engineering workflows.** If you need enforced status transitions, complex workflows, velocity reports and deep links to code, Jira, Linear or YouTrack do it better.
- **Heavy analytics and calculations.** Notion formulas are handy, but this is not Excel or a BI tool: pivot tables, large datasets and complex math are awkward.
- **Very large databases.** With tens of thousands of records the interface slows down noticeably.
- **Offline work.** Notion is cloud-first and its offline mode is limited.
- **Regulated data.** If the law requires data to stay on your own server or in a specific country, cloud Notion may not meet the requirement.
- **Teams without an owner.** Flexibility cuts both ways: without someone responsible for structure, the workspace quickly becomes a dumping ground.

## How to start without chaos

1. Create **teamspaces** per department or area.
2. Start with one job, such as a wiki or a task board, not everything at once.
3. Agree on page **templates** and naming rules.
4. Assign a **workspace owner** and owners for key sections.
5. Review quarterly and archive what is outdated.

## FAQ

### Is Notion free?

There is a free plan that works well for personal use and for trying the product. Team use usually needs a paid plan because of limits on members, version history and permissions.

### Can Notion replace Jira or Trello?

Trello, often yes: a Notion board covers similar scenarios. Jira, only for small teams with simple processes; complex workflows and tight integration with development are easier in a dedicated tracker.

### Does Notion have an API?

Yes. Notion has a public API for reading and writing pages and databases. Through it, and through tools like Zapier, Make or n8n, teams connect Notion to forms, CRMs and messengers.
