## The short answer

- **Multichannel retail** means you sell in several places: a physical shop, a website, a marketplace, Instagram. But each channel lives its own life: separate stock, separate prices, separate customers.
- **Omnichannel retail** uses the same channels but connects them into one system. The shopper is one person across all channels, stock is shared, and an order can be placed in one place and picked up or returned in another.

The difference is not how many channels you have but whether **the data is connected** between them.

## What it looks like for the customer

| Situation | Multichannel | Omnichannel |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | The site does not know what is in the shop | Stock visible for every location |
| Returns | Online purchases cannot be returned in store | Return anywhere |
| Rewards | Separate programmes online and offline | One card, one balance |
| History | Store staff cannot see online orders | Staff see the full history |
| Cart | A cart started in the app is lost on the website | Cart is synced |

## Typical omnichannel scenarios

- **Buy online, pick up in store (BOPIS, click and collect).** The customer orders on the website, the system reserves the item at the nearest location, and the customer collects it when convenient.
- **Order in store, deliver to home.** The right size is not on the shelf, so staff place an order from the warehouse or another shop.
- **Return through any channel.** Items bought online can be returned at a physical location.
- **One loyalty programme.** Points are earned and spent the same way everywhere.
- **Ship from store.** An online order is picked at the nearest shop that has the item, not only at the central warehouse.

## What data must be shared

Omnichannel is first of all an integration challenge. You need three unified data layers:

**1. A single customer profile.** One customer, one record, linked to online and offline purchases, loyalty points, support requests and marketing consents. The key is often the phone number. It lives in a CRM or CDP.

**2. Unified stock.** The system knows how much of each item is in every warehouse and shop, including reservations. Stock has to update often enough, or customers will travel to collect items that are already gone.

**3. Unified orders.** Every order, regardless of channel, lands in one order management system (OMS) that shows where to ship from, who is picking and what the status is.

Channels plug into these layers: website, app, POS, marketplaces, Telegram bot, call centre.

## Where to start

1. **Map your systems.** Where do customers, stock, prices and orders live today?
2. **Choose a source of truth** for each data type — for example stock and prices in your accounting system, customers in the CRM.
3. **Start with one scenario** with clear value: often store availability and click and collect.
4. **Merge the customer base** and the loyalty programme.
5. **Prepare processes and people**: who picks orders in store, how staff see online orders, how cross-location sales are credited.

## Common mistakes

- **Calling many channels omnichannel.** Without connected data it is still multichannel.
- **Showing inaccurate stock.** Promising pickup when the shelf is empty is worse than offering no pickup.
- **Ignoring staff incentives.** If online sales are not credited to the store, employees will be reluctant to hand out online orders.
- **Trying to connect everything at once.** Launch scenarios one by one and measure the result.

## FAQ

### Does a small business need omnichannel?

Not always a full platform. But the basics help even with one shop and a website: shared stock, one customer base and the option to collect online orders in store.

### How is omnichannel different from cross-channel?

Cross-channel usually means individual links between channels, such as picking up a web order in store. Omnichannel means all channels rely on the same data and the shopper moves freely between them.

### Which systems are usually involved?

An accounting system or ERP for stock and prices, a CRM for customers and loyalty, an order management system, in-store POS software, and integrations with the website, app and marketplaces.
