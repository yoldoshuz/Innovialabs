## The short answer

**Retargeting** is advertising aimed at people who have already interacted with you: visited the site, viewed a product, started checkout, then left. They see your ads again in search, on social networks and on partner sites of the ad network.

The logic is simple: someone who already knows you usually decides faster than someone seeing the brand for the first time. Retargeting does not bring a new audience — it **converts** the one that already came.

## How it works: pixels and audience lists

1. **Pixel or tag.** You add the ad platform's code to the site: Meta Pixel, a Google Ads tag, a Yandex Metrica counter. It records visits and events — product views, add-to-cart, form submissions.
2. **Audience.** In the ad account you define a rule, for example "viewed the service page in the last 30 days but did not submit a request". The platform collects matching people automatically.
3. **Campaign.** You run separate ads for that audience — with their own copy, creatives and bids.

Besides the pixel, audiences can come from other sources:

- **customer lists** — uploaded phone numbers or emails, matched to platform users in hashed form;
- **social engagement** — people who watched a video, opened a lead form or sent a direct message;
- **app users** — via an SDK and in-app events.

Keep in mind that browsers and operating systems increasingly limit tracking, and many countries require user consent. Audiences shrink over time, which makes server-side event delivery (such as Meta's Conversions API) more important.

## Retargeting vs remarketing

In practice the terms are often used interchangeably. If you do distinguish them:

| | Retargeting | Remarketing |
|---|---|---|
| Who you bring back | anonymous site or app visitors | known contacts: customers, subscribers |
| Main tool | pixel, cookies, advertising IDs | email, messengers, uploaded lists |
| Channel | ad networks | newsletters, plus list-based ads |

Extra confusion comes from Google historically calling its retargeting feature "remarketing". The label matters less than the idea: you are working with people who have already seen you.

## Frequency: when helpful becomes annoying

If the same banner follows someone for weeks, it irritates them and hurts the brand. What helps:

- **Frequency caps.** Many ad platforms let you set a maximum number of impressions per person per period.
- **Audience window.** The further from the visit, the lower the chance to convert. Set a duration after which people drop out of the list.
- **Exclusions.** Always exclude people who already bought or submitted a request — otherwise you pay to show ads to your own customers.
- **Creative rotation.** Refresh ads so people do not see the same thing over and over.
- **Segments by intent.** Someone who abandoned a cart and someone who read one blog post need different messages.

## Where retargeting works best

- **Online stores** — recovering abandoned carts and showing viewed products (dynamic retargeting from a product feed).
- **Long decisions** — B2B, education, real estate, expensive services: people need several touches before committing.
- **Products with a free trial** — reminding those who signed up but never started using it.
- **Landing pages with a form** — bringing back those who opened the form but did not submit it.

It is weaker when site traffic is low: the audience is too small and the platform cannot deliver ads properly. In that case, grow core traffic first.

## Common mistakes

- One broad "all visitors" audience with no segments.
- No exclusion of buyers.
- The same ad running for months.
- Treating retargeting as the main channel and forgetting it only brings back people that other channels attracted.

## FAQ

### Does a small website need retargeting?

With few visitors, audiences will be tiny and ads may not run at all. Focus on attracting traffic first, but install the pixels right away so they start building audiences in advance.

### Is it legal to show ads based on site visits?

It depends on the laws of your country and your audience's. Many jurisdictions require a cookie notice and consent. Describe the data collection in your privacy policy and follow the ad platforms' rules.

### Why does retargeting show such a good return?

Partly because these people were already close to buying. To see the real contribution, compare against a holdout group or look at the customer journey in end-to-end analytics.
