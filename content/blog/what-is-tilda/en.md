## Short answer: what Tilda is

**Tilda** is an online website builder where pages are assembled from ready-made blocks: covers, text sections, galleries, forms, product cards, footers. No coding is needed. You pick a block, replace the text and images, adjust colors and spacing, and publish.

Sites are hosted on Tilda's servers and you connect your own domain. The interface is available in several languages, including Russian and English, which is one reason it is so popular in the CIS among marketers, designers and small business owners.

## Blocks and Zero Block

Tilda gives you two ways to build a page.

- **Block library.** Hundreds of prebuilt blocks grouped by purpose: menus, covers, about, features, testimonials, pricing, forms. They are already responsive, so even without a designer the site looks tidy.
- **Zero Block.** A visual editor where you place elements freely: text, images, shapes, buttons, video. Positions are set separately for each breakpoint (desktop, tablet, phone). Scroll and hover animations are configured here too.

Good Tilda sites usually combine both: standard blocks for typical sections and Zero Block for the hero and anything unusual. One catch: Zero Block does not adapt itself, so **every breakpoint has to be checked and fixed by hand**.

## Plans: what to compare

Tilda has a free plan and paid plans (Personal and Business). Prices change from time to time, so check the official site, and compare capabilities rather than the price tag:

- **Free** lets you try the editor: only part of the block library, no custom domain and a limited number of pages.
- **Personal** unlocks the full library, a custom domain, form integrations and payments for one site.
- **Business** adds several sites per account and code export. Agencies and companies with multiple projects usually choose it.

## What kind of sites Tilda suits

- **Landing pages** for ads, events and product launches.
- **Company sites** of a dozen pages or so.
- **Portfolios** for designers, studios and photographers.
- **Media and blogs** via the built-in Feeds service.
- **Small online stores** with a product catalog, cart and checkout.
- **Prototypes and MVPs** when you need to test demand quickly.

The common thread: content does not change every hour, the logic is simple, and launch speed matters more than unique functionality.

## Forms, leads and payments

Forms are one of Tilda's strengths. A submission can go to several **data receivers** at once: email, Telegram, Google Sheets, the built-in Tilda CRM, or external CRMs such as amoCRM and Bitrix24. For your own systems there is a **webhook** option.

Payments connect to the cart and forms through payment providers. The list of supported providers depends on the country, so check that your acquiring option is available before you start. If your local provider is missing, the usual workaround is an external payment page or a custom integration via webhook.

## Limits that matter as you grow

- **No server-side logic of your own.** User accounts with real data, calculators, complex filters and two-way integrations end up as workarounds or third-party services.
- **Catalogs.** Tilda Store copes with a modest range, but syncing with inventory systems, complex variants and filtering quickly get awkward.
- **Speed.** Heavy Zero Blocks with large images and animations slow down loading, especially on mobile.
- **Platform lock-in.** The site lives on Tilda. Code export exists, but forms, the cart and other services stop working once exported.
- **SEO control.** The basics are there (meta tags, clean URLs, sitemap), but fine technical control is limited.

## How to decide if Tilda fits you

Ask yourself four questions:

1. Is the site mainly about presenting the company and collecting leads?
2. Do you need to launch in days rather than months?
3. Will a marketer update the content without a developer?
4. Do you need user accounts, ERP integration or complex calculations?

If the answer to the first three is yes and to the fourth is no, Tilda is a sensible choice. If complex requirements already exist or will appear within a year, it is worth discussing custom development from the start.

## FAQ

### Can you build an online store on Tilda?

Yes, using the built-in catalog, cart and a payment provider. It works well for a small range of products. With a large catalog and warehouse integration, specialized solutions are more convenient.

### Do you need a designer to build a Tilda site?

Not for a simple site made of standard blocks. For a distinctive brand look and careful work in Zero Block, a designer noticeably improves the result.

### Can you move a site off Tilda later?

You can export the code or move the content to another platform, but dynamic features have to be rebuilt. To keep search rankings, preserve page URLs or set up 301 redirects during the move.
