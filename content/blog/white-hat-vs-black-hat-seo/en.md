## What the difference is

**White hat SEO** is work that makes a site more useful and easier to understand: technical optimization, quality content, clear structure, natural links. It follows Google Search Essentials and Yandex's site quality requirements.

**Black hat SEO** is an attempt to gain rankings by fooling the algorithm instead of helping the user. These tactics can bring fast growth, but search engines detect them both automatically and through manual review.

In between sits **gray hat SEO**: tactics that are not always explicitly banned but become violations when abused.

## Tactics classified by risk

| Tactic | What it is | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Technical optimization, speed, structured data | Helps search engines understand the site | Safe |
| Useful content matching intent | Answers the user's question | Safe |
| PR, mentions, partner publications | Natural links | Safe |
| Guest posts for links | Mass placement with links | Medium, high when abused |
| Keyword over-optimization | Stuffing text with keywords | Medium |
| Buying links | Paid links that pass ranking value | High |
| PBNs | A network of your own sites for links | High |
| Doorway pages | Query-targeted pages funneling to another site | Very high |
| Cloaking | Different content for bots and humans | Very high |
| Behavior manipulation | Bots imitating visits and clicks | Very high, especially in Yandex |

## The risky tactics in more detail

- **Buying links.** Google explicitly treats paid links that pass ranking value as spam. Paid placements should be marked with `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`. Yandex also fights SEO links with algorithms such as Minusinsk.
- **PBNs (private blog networks).** Sites built only to link to the main property. They are exposed by shared hosting, templated content and connected owners. Once detected, the links are devalued and the site may be penalized.
- **Doorway pages.** Many pages for different queries that all lead the user to one place. A direct violation of both search engines' rules.
- **Cloaking.** The bot sees SEO text, the human sees a different page. One of the most severely punished tactics.
- **Behavior manipulation.** Bots imitate searches and clicks. Yandex penalizes this especially actively, and recovering from the filter can take a long time.

## What the consequences look like

1. **Algorithmic demotion**: rankings drop without notice.
2. **Manual actions**: a message appears in Google Search Console, or violation details in Yandex Webmaster.
3. **Removal of pages or the whole site** from results in severe cases.
4. **Lost investment**: money spent on links and manipulation is gone, and recovery takes months.

Important: penalties often arrive with a delay. A site can grow on black hat tactics and then lose everything at once.

## How to choose a contractor and avoid the risk

- Ask **where the links will come from** and how growth is planned.
- Be wary of promises like "number one in a month" or guaranteed rankings.
- Ask for transparent reports: what changed on the site, which publications were placed.
- Check Search Console and Yandex Webmaster regularly for violation notices.

## FAQ

### Can a site recover from a penalty?

Yes, but it takes time. You need to remove the violation (take down bought links, delete doorway pages, disavow toxic links) and, for manual actions, submit a reconsideration request.

### Are guest posts black hat SEO?

Not by themselves. A useful article on a relevant website is normal practice. The problem starts when posts are produced at scale purely for links with commercial anchor text.

### Is the site owner responsible for what an SEO contractor does?

Yes. Search engines penalize the site, not the contractor. That is why you need to understand which methods your contractor uses.
