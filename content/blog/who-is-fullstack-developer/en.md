## The short answer: what fullstack really means

A **fullstack developer** can take a web application from the database to the interface: design the tables, write the API, build the screens, connect them to the server and deploy the result.

An honest caveat: "fullstack" almost never means someone knows everything equally deeply. It usually describes a developer with **one strong side** (most often frontend or backend) and a **working level** in the other. That is enough to deliver a whole feature alone, without handing the task between people.

## What the fullstack "stack" includes

| Layer | What you need to know |
|---|---|
| **Interface** | HTML, CSS, JavaScript/TypeScript, one framework |
| **Server** | One backend language and framework, API design |
| **Data** | SQL, schema design, migrations |
| **Infrastructure** | Git, Docker, deployment, basic Linux |
| **Glue** | Authentication, file handling, third-party integrations |

Many fullstack developers pick one language for the whole stack, such as TypeScript on both client and server, to avoid switching between ecosystems.

## Breadth versus depth

| | Fullstack | Specialist |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed on a small feature** | High: no handoff between people | Lower: frontend and backend need to coordinate |
| **Seeing the whole product** | A strength | Depends on the person and process |
| **Hard, narrow problems** | May lack depth | A strength |
| **Knowledge to keep up with** | Large, easy to fall behind in one part | Smaller but deeper |
| **Responsibility** | Often higher: you own the full chain | Shared across the team |

Neither is "better". They are different profiles for different jobs.

## Where fullstack developers are especially valued

- **Early-stage startups**: a small team needs to build and test a product quickly.
- **Small studios and agencies**: one developer can carry a mid-sized project from start to launch.
- **Internal company tools**: admin panels, CRM extensions, services for employees.
- **MVPs and prototypes**, where testing the idea fast matters more than perfect architecture.

## Where specialists win

- **Complex interfaces**: heavy editors, animations, demanding client-side performance.
- **High-load systems**: scaling, distributed databases, queues.
- **Large teams**: work is split by layer, and depth in one area is worth more.
- **Specific domains**: security, payments, large-scale data processing.

## Is it worth becoming a fullstack developer

The practical path for most people is to **get strong in one part first**, then expand into the other. Trying to learn everything from day one usually ends in shallow knowledge everywhere.

Fullstack suits you if:

- you want to see how the whole product works, not just one part;
- you want to work at a startup or in a small team;
- you enjoy taking a task all the way to done on your own.

Specializing makes more sense if:

- you love going deep on one topic;
- you are aiming for large companies with complex systems.

## Common mistakes

- **Calling yourself fullstack after a couple of tutorials** on each side; interviews expose this quickly.
- **Ignoring infrastructure**: without deployment and basic server knowledge, a feature is not finished.
- **Chasing new technologies** in both layers instead of deepening the fundamentals.

## FAQ

### Can a junior be a fullstack developer?

Technically yes, but early on it is easier to get hired and grow with one clear strength. Breadth usually comes with experience, once you are confident in one part.

### Does a fullstack developer need to design?

No. They implement mockups but are not expected to create them. A basic sense of UI helps, especially in small teams where a designer may not be on every project.

### Which stack should I start with on the way to fullstack?

A convenient option is one language on client and server, such as JavaScript/TypeScript, plus SQL. That way your energy goes into understanding architecture, not switching languages.
